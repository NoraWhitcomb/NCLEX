Question 801CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (The test screens for gestational diabetes, which is usually checked between 24 and 28 weeks): the glucose challenge test is a screening tool for gestational diabetes and is routinely performed at 24 to 28 weeks of gestation. Preeclampsia is screened with blood pressure measurement and urine protein, not with a glucose load. Anemia is assessed with a hemoglobin and hematocrit, which is a separate blood test. Fetal lung maturity is not evaluated by a maternal glucose test and is rarely assessed in routine prenatal care.

Question 802BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Avoid coffee, tea, soda and chocolate for the time period the provider specifies): caffeine blocks the adenosine receptors that regadenoson acts on, so it can produce a falsely normal result and must be held as directed. Most stress tests require fasting for several hours, so a full breakfast is not advised. Some cardiac medications are held before testing, but only the specific drugs the provider names rather than every medication. Decaffeinated coffee still contains a small amount of caffeine and is usually restricted before a pharmacologic stress test.

Question 803BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (An 82-year-old client with new delirium who repeatedly tries to climb out of bed): a confused client who tries to get up alone is at high risk for falls, and a bed alarm alerts staff before the client walks unassisted. An alert client who reliably calls for help can be protected with standard fall precautions and does not need an alarm. A client with paraplegia cannot rise from bed alone, so a bed exit alarm adds little protection. A young adult who walks steadily has a low fall risk and does not need a bed alarm.

Question 804DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (A spare tracheostomy tube of the same size and its obturator): a spare tube and obturator allow rapid reinsertion if the tracheostomy is accidentally dislodged, which is an airway emergency. An extra blood pressure cuff is useful equipment but does not address a threat to the airway. A urinary catheter kit can be obtained from supply if retention develops and is not emergency airway equipment. A warm compress offers comfort only and could add moisture that promotes skin breakdown around a new stoma.

Question 805DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Explain what observation status means and contact the case manager for coverage and discharge details): the nurse can explain the general meaning of observation status, and the case manager coordinates utilization review, coverage questions and discharge planning. Predicting a discharge date before the workup is finished gives the client a promise the nurse cannot keep. Referring the client to the insurer alone shifts the burden to the client when the case manager can help coordinate the answer. Observation is an outpatient status, so telling the client it is billed like an inpatient admission is inaccurate.

Question 806ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Metal earrings, a necklace and bobby pins holding the client's hair in place): ferromagnetic objects can be pulled by the magnet with great force and can heat up, causing projectile injury or burns. A paper identification band contains no metal and stays on so the client can be identified during the scan. A cotton gown with plastic fasteners is the recommended clothing for the scan because it contains no metal. Foam earplugs are routinely provided to protect hearing from the loud noise the scanner produces.

Question 807CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Program the pump to deliver 125 mL each hour until the bag is empty): dividing 500 mL by 4 hours gives 125 mL per hour, which delivers the prescribed volume over the prescribed time. A rate of 100 mL per hour would take 5 hours to infuse 500 mL, which is slower than ordered. A rate of 250 mL per hour would finish the bag in 2 hours, twice as fast as prescribed. A rate of 200 mL per hour would complete the infusion in 2.5 hours, which also exceeds the prescribed rate.

Question 808DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A client with a femur fracture who is suddenly short of breath and confused): sudden dyspnea and confusion after a long-bone fracture suggest fat embolism or pulmonary embolism, which can be rapidly fatal. A request for a heating pad for chronic pain is a comfort need that can safely wait. Cloudy, foul-smelling urine suggests a possible urinary tract infection that needs follow-up but is not immediately life threatening. A blood pressure of 148/92 is elevated but stable and does not represent an acute emergency.

Question 809BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Document a stage 1 pressure injury and record its size and location): nonblanchable redness of intact skin is the defining feature of a stage 1 pressure injury. A stage 2 injury involves partial-thickness skin loss or a serum-filled blister, which is not present here. A deep tissue injury appears as persistent deep red, maroon or purple discoloration, often with tissue that feels boggy. An unstageable injury has full-thickness tissue loss with the base hidden by slough or eschar, so it does not apply to intact skin.

Question 810CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Giving a prescribed antihistamine and possibly resuming the transfusion slowly if the provider approves): hives with stable vital signs indicate a mild allergic reaction, which is treated with an antihistamine, and the provider decides whether the transfusion may resume. Speeding up the infusion would increase exposure to the allergen and could worsen the reaction. Epinephrine and airway preparation are reserved for anaphylaxis, which would include wheezing, hypotension or throat swelling. Fever, chills and hypotension would suggest bacterial contamination, so a sepsis workup is not indicated for isolated hives.

Question 811CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (A vase of fresh-cut flowers arranged in standing water for the windowsill): standing water in flower vases can harbor bacteria and fungi such as Pseudomonas and Aspergillus, which are dangerous for a client with severe neutropenia. A book is a low-risk personal item that supports comfort and diversion during a long hospital stay. A framed photograph has no standing water or soil and can be wiped clean if needed. A clean, laundered blanket poses minimal infection risk and offers comfort from home.

Question 812CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Perforation of the appendix with developing peritonitis that requires urgent surgery): sudden relief of pain followed by a rigid abdomen, fever and tachycardia indicates rupture with spreading infection, a surgical emergency. Pain relief here is not improvement, because the rigid abdomen and rising fever show the condition is worsening. Constipation causes gradual distention and cramping, not a boardlike abdomen with high fever. Gastroenteritis typically causes diffuse cramping with vomiting and diarrhea rather than sudden pain relief followed by rigidity.

Question 813BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Notify the provider immediately to report signs of acute limb ischemia): sudden pain, pallor, coolness, pulselessness and numbness signal acute arterial occlusion, which threatens the limb and needs urgent revascularization. Massaging the leg delays treatment and could dislodge clot fragments further down the artery. Applying heat raises tissue metabolic demand and can burn skin that has lost normal sensation. Elevating an ischemic limb reduces arterial flow further, so the leg is kept level or slightly dependent.

Question 814CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Notify the surgeon immediately and report the volume and character of drainage): more than 100 mL per hour of bright red drainage with a rising heart rate suggests active hemorrhage that may require a return to surgery. Documenting and continuing hourly monitoring delays treatment of a possible hemorrhage. Clamping the tube can cause a tension pneumothorax and is not used to control bleeding. Stripping the tubing creates high negative pressure that can damage lung tissue and is not recommended.

Question 815DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Give a verbal report using a structured format such as SBAR or I-PASS): structured tools organize essential information consistently and allow the receiving nurse to ask questions, which reduces errors at transitions of care. Relying on the record alone removes the chance for clarification and can miss recent changes. Family members are not responsible for professional handoff and may not know clinical details such as medication changes. A written note left at the bedside offers no opportunity for questions and can be lost or overlooked.

Question 816BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Apply ice to the knee, reposition with pillows and offer guided distraction activities): cold therapy, positioning and distraction reduce pain and swelling and work alongside prescribed analgesics. Withholding scheduled analgesia leads to uncontrolled pain and is not the purpose of nonpharmacologic measures. Early mobility is encouraged after knee replacement to prevent stiffness and venous thromboembolism. Prolonged heat over a new incision can increase swelling and bleeding and risks burns.

Question 817DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Sudden shortness of breath with diminished breath sounds on the right side): sudden dyspnea with decreased breath sounds on the affected side suggests a pneumothorax, a serious complication of thoracentesis. A heart rate of 84 with a regular rhythm is within normal limits. A small amount of serous drainage on the dressing is expected after the procedure. Mild soreness at the puncture site is an expected finding that can be managed with comfort measures.

Question 818BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Palpitations, nervousness, heat intolerance and an unplanned weight loss of 3 kilograms): excess thyroid hormone produces signs of hyperthyroidism, including tachycardia, anxiety, heat intolerance and weight loss. Constipation is a sign of too little thyroid hormone and suggests the dose may be inadequate. Weight gain reflects a slowed metabolism, which points to undertreatment rather than overtreatment. Fatigue, dry skin and cold intolerance are classic signs of persistent hypothyroidism.

Question 819BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (A sterile gauze package lands within the outer 1-inch border of the field): the outer 1-inch border of a sterile field is considered unsterile, so any item that touches it is contaminated. The center of the drape is the sterile working area, so an item placed there remains sterile. Sterile touching sterile remains sterile, so a gloved hand contacting another sterile item is acceptable. Pouring without splashing keeps the drape dry and prevents contamination by strike-through.

Question 820APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A ("I want to cut back on drinking because it is hurting my family."): the client names a personal reason and expresses a desire to change, which shows the greatest readiness among these statements. Comparing drinking with coworkers normalizes the behavior and reflects precontemplation. Denying a problem because the client still works is a sign of precontemplation. Considering change at a vague future time shows ambivalence and less readiness than a stated wish to change.

Question 821BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Epinephrine 1 mg/mL for intramuscular injection according to the anaphylaxis protocol): intramuscular epinephrine is the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis because it reverses bronchospasm, vasodilation and airway swelling. Heparin is an anticoagulant and has no role in treating an allergic reaction. Insulin treats high blood glucose and would not reverse the airway or circulatory effects of anaphylaxis. Digoxin slows the heart rate and strengthens contraction but does not treat anaphylaxis.

Question 822DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Platelet count, to detect thrombocytopenia that increases the risk of bleeding): low-molecular-weight heparins can cause thrombocytopenia, including heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, which raises the risk of bleeding and clotting. Glycated hemoglobin reflects long-term glucose control and has no bearing on the safety of enoxaparin. Cholesterol is a cardiovascular risk marker and does not affect the decision to give an anticoagulant. Serum sodium is useful for fluid balance but is not monitored specifically for enoxaparin therapy.

Question 823CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Bring the infant to the parents and offer privacy and unhurried time together): holding the baby helps parents acknowledge the loss and begin grieving, so the nurse honors the request and provides privacy and support. Most facilities support parents in holding a stillborn infant, and refusing the request interferes with healthy grieving. Discouraging contact to avoid distress places the nurse's judgment above the parents' wishes and can complicate grief. A photograph can be a valuable keepsake but does not replace the contact the client has asked for.

Question 824CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Digoxin 0.125 mg orally, given once daily to improve heart failure symptoms): hypokalemia increases the risk of digoxin toxicity and dysrhythmias, so the dose is clarified while the potassium is corrected. Acetaminophen does not interact with low potassium and can be given as prescribed. Loratadine is not affected by serum potassium and poses no added risk at this level. Docusate is a stool softener that does not interact with hypokalemia.

Question 825DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Keep the dominant hand sterile to handle the catheter after cleansing the meatus): the nondominant hand becomes contaminated once it touches the labia, so the dominant hand must stay sterile to cleanse and insert the catheter. Releasing the labia allows them to recontaminate the cleansed meatus, and the nondominant hand is no longer sterile for handling the catheter. A catheter that enters the vagina is contaminated, so it is left in place as a landmark and a new sterile catheter is used. Placing the tray on linen without a barrier exposes the sterile supplies to contamination.

Question 826BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Serum creatinine, trended alongside vancomycin levels to detect declining kidney function): vancomycin can injure the kidneys, so serum creatinine and drug levels are monitored to detect nephrotoxicity and guide dosing. Pancreatitis is not a typical adverse effect of vancomycin, so amylase is not a routine monitoring value. Vancomycin does not characteristically cause hypocalcemia, and calcium does not reflect kidney injury early. Cholesterol is unrelated to vancomycin therapy and does not reflect kidney function.

Question 827AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Stop the contrast infusion and call for emergency help following protocol): hives, wheezing, hoarseness and falling blood pressure suggest a severe reaction, so the contrast is stopped and the emergency response is activated. Oral fluids do not treat an allergic reaction and could cause aspiration if the airway swells. Documenting and observing delays treatment of a reaction that is affecting the airway and circulation. Continuing the scan would expose the client to more contrast and worsen the reaction.

Question 828CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (The client cannot describe which organ the surgeon is planning to remove): a client who cannot explain the procedure has not demonstrated understanding, so informed consent has not been obtained. A low-grade fever is expected with acute cholecystitis and does not by itself prevent surgery. The scheduled start time creates pressure but is not a reason to proceed before consent is valid. Understanding of the procedure is a legal and ethical requirement that must be met before the client signs.

Question 829BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (The opioid may impair the client's judgment and capacity to give informed consent): opioids can cause sedation and impair judgment and recall, which may affect the client's capacity to consent. Pain relief can improve comfort, but drowsiness and poor recall show that understanding is currently impaired. A prior explanation does not make consent valid if the client lacks capacity at the time of signing. The nurse reports concerns about sedation so the surgeon can determine whether the client has decision-making capacity.

Question 830AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The surgeon who will perform the cholecystectomy and can answer questions about risks): the surgeon performing the procedure is responsible for explaining its nature, risks, benefits and alternatives. The nurse may reinforce information and witness the signature but does not obtain consent for the surgery. A spouse cannot provide the explanation, and surrogate decisions apply only when the client lacks capacity. When the client has questions, the nurse refers them to the surgeon before the form is signed.

Question 831AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Notify the surgeon so understanding and capacity can be addressed before the operation): the surgeon must clarify the procedure and determine whether the client has capacity, which protects the validity of consent. Obtaining a signature from a client who does not understand the surgery does not create valid informed consent. Explaining the surgery is the surgeon's responsibility, and the nurse cannot substitute for the surgeon's discussion. The schedule and the spouse's offer do not override the client's right to understand and decide.

Question 832CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Notify the surgeon and document the client's statements in objective, quoted form): notifying the surgeon advocates for the client, and quoting the client's words gives an accurate record of the concern. A spouse may serve as a surrogate only when the client lacks capacity and the proper process is followed, which has not occurred. Willingness to sign does not show understanding, so witnessing the signature now would not support valid consent. Documentation should describe what the client said and did rather than the nurse's opinion of the client.

Question 833BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (The client explains the planned surgery in their own words and then signs the consent): valid informed consent requires capacity and understanding, which the client shows by explaining the surgery before signing. A signature obtained while the client is drowsy does not reflect capacity, even with a family member present. Consent must be obtained before a nonemergency procedure, so signing the form afterward does not meet the requirement. Waiting for the sedation to wear off protects both the client's rights and the validity of the consent.

Question 834CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Fat embolism syndrome from marrow fat released after the long-bone fracture): respiratory distress, altered mental status and a petechial rash 24 to 72 hours after a long-bone fracture form the classic picture of fat embolism syndrome. An allergic reaction usually causes raised hives or flushing rather than petechiae with hypoxemia. Hypoglycemia can cause confusion but does not produce petechiae or severe hypoxemia. A stroke causes focal neurologic deficits and would not explain the petechial rash and respiratory distress.

Question 835ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Reposition the client on the side and give the prescribed anticholinergic medication): side-lying positioning helps secretions drain, and anticholinergic medications such as glycopyrrolate or scopolamine reduce secretion production. Frequent deep suctioning is distressing, can stimulate more secretions and is generally avoided at the end of life. An unresponsive client cannot swallow safely, so oral fluids increase the risk of aspiration. Lying flat allows secretions to pool in the throat and makes the noisy breathing worse.

Question 836APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A ("High-protein meals can reduce absorption, so talk with your provider about meal timing."): dietary protein competes with levodopa for absorption, so spacing doses away from high-protein meals can improve symptom control. Orthostatic dizziness should be reported, but stopping the drug abruptly can cause a severe withdrawal reaction. Taking each dose with a protein shake reduces the amount of levodopa absorbed, although a small low-protein snack can ease nausea. Carbidopa-levodopa relieves symptoms but does not slow or stop progression of the disease.

Question 837DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Stop the heparin infusion and notify the provider following the anticoagulation protocol): bleeding with an elevated activated partial thromboplastin time indicates excessive anticoagulation, so the infusion is stopped and the provider is notified. Vitamin K reverses warfarin rather than heparin and would not correct this problem. Increasing the rate would worsen bleeding in a client who is already over-anticoagulated. Protamine sulfate may be prescribed for significant bleeding, but the infusion is stopped first and the drug requires a provider order.

Question 838CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (In the wall-mounted puncture-resistant sharps container immediately, without recapping the needle): discarding the needle immediately in a puncture-resistant container without recapping prevents needlestick injuries. Leaving a used needle on the bedside table exposes the client and staff to an accidental stick. Two-handed recapping is a common cause of needlestick injury, and sharps do not belong in regular trash. A needle in the linen hamper can injure laundry workers and others who handle the linen.

Question 839BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Hypokalemia from increased potassium excretion in the distal renal tubule): thiazide diuretics increase potassium loss in the urine, and cramps and fatigue are common signs of low potassium. Thiazides tend to cause hyponatremia rather than hypernatremia because they promote sodium excretion. Thiazides reduce urinary calcium loss, so they are more likely to raise serum calcium than lower it. Hyperkalemia is associated with potassium-sparing diuretics and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors rather than thiazides.

Question 840DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Accept the client's rating and implement the prescribed pain management plan): pain is subjective, and the client's self-report is the most reliable measure, even when the client is using distraction to cope. Delaying reassessment until visitors leave postpones treatment of pain the client has already reported. Charting that behavior contradicts the rating dismisses the client's report and can lead to undertreatment. Distraction can complement medication but should not replace analgesia for pain rated 7 out of 10.

Question 841BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (The influenza vaccine, given each season to protect the nurse and clients): annual influenza vaccination is recommended for all health care personnel to protect themselves and their clients. A health care worker with documented measles, mumps and rubella immunity does not need further doses. Varicella vaccine is a two-dose series for those without immunity and is not repeated yearly. Tetanus and diphtheria boosters are given every 10 years rather than annually.

Question 842CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Decreased appetite at lunch and difficulty falling asleep at bedtime): stimulants commonly suppress appetite and disrupt sleep, so growth, weight and sleep patterns are monitored. Stimulants tend to cause weight loss rather than weight gain because they reduce appetite. Methylphenidate typically increases heart rate and blood pressure, which are checked at each visit. Stimulants promote wakefulness, so excessive daytime sleepiness is not an expected effect.

Question 843AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (A venous thromboembolism, such as a pulmonary embolism or deep vein thrombosis): an elevated level of this fibrin breakdown product indicates clot formation and breakdown, and because it is sensitive but not specific, imaging is often needed. Anemia is evaluated with a hemoglobin and iron studies rather than a fibrin breakdown test. Kidney stones cause flank pain radiating to the groin and are evaluated with urinalysis and imaging. Hypothyroidism is diagnosed with thyroid function tests and does not raise fibrin breakdown products.

Question 844DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Rescue the client by moving the client away from the immediate danger): the acronym stands for rescue, alarm, contain and extinguish or evacuate, and removing the client from immediate danger comes first. Closing doors contains smoke and fire but follows rescue and activating the alarm. Activating the alarm is the second step, taken after any client in immediate danger is moved. Using an extinguisher is the final step and is attempted only after clients are safe and the alarm is sounded.

Question 845APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Use the quick-relief inhaler now and seek emergency care if not improving quickly): the red zone signals a medical alert, so the client uses rescue medication and seeks emergency help according to the action plan. A routine appointment later in the week delays care for a potentially life-threatening exacerbation. Inhaled corticosteroid controllers have a slow onset and do not relieve acute bronchospasm. Continuing normal activity and waiting until morning allows the exacerbation to progress.

Question 846DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D ("Hold the cane in your right hand and move it forward with your left leg."): the cane is held on the stronger side and advanced with the weaker leg, which shares the load and widens the base of support. Choosing a hand by comfort may place the cane on the weak side, where it gives little support. Holding the cane on the weaker side shifts weight away from where support is needed and causes an awkward gait. Moving the cane after both legs leaves the weaker leg unsupported during each step.

Question 847CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Offer the child a choice of water or juice to drink after the medicine): toddlers are developing autonomy, and a choice between two acceptable options gives control without making the medication optional. Firm restraint as a first approach increases fear and struggle and overlooks the toddler's need for some control. Detailed explanations of how a medicine works are beyond a toddler's cognitive understanding. Asking a yes-or-no question offers a choice that does not exist and invites the child to refuse.

Question 848BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("My arm is burning and painful around the place where the IV goes in."): potassium chloride irritates veins and can cause phlebitis or tissue damage if it infiltrates, so the nurse stops the infusion and inspects the site. Mild thirst is a common, nonspecific complaint and does not indicate a problem with the potassium infusion. Hunger is unrelated to the infusion and can be addressed without interrupting therapy. Feeling warm under blankets is a comfort issue rather than a sign of infiltration or phlebitis.

Question 849APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Suction the tracheostomy using sterile technique after preoxygenating the client): suctioning removes the secretions obstructing the airway and restores oxygenation, which is the priority. Deflating the cuff does not clear the secretions and can allow them to be aspirated into the lungs. Documentation is needed but must follow action to correct the hypoxemia. The tube is already secure, and changing ties during respiratory distress risks dislodging it.

Question 850CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Assess circulation, movement and sensation and notify the provider immediately): pain unrelieved by opioids, pain with passive movement and paresthesia suggest compartment syndrome, a surgical emergency that requires rapid evaluation. Elevating above heart level can reduce arterial flow in compartment syndrome, and waiting 2 hours allows irreversible muscle and nerve damage. Ice does not relieve pressure inside a closed compartment and delays definitive treatment. More opioid can mask worsening symptoms and delays notification of the provider.

Question 851APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Hang 10% dextrose at the ordered rate per protocol until the new bag arrives): abrupt interruption of the concentrated dextrose in parenteral nutrition can cause rebound hypoglycemia, so 10% dextrose bridges the gap until the new bag is hung. Normal saline contains no dextrose and does not prevent the rebound drop in blood glucose. Clamping and flushing the line stops all glucose delivery and allows the same hypoglycemia to develop. Speeding up the next bag to catch up can cause hyperglycemia and fluid overload and is unsafe.

Question 852ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A ("Move the walker forward about one step, then step into it with your weaker leg."): the client advances the walker a short distance and steps into the frame, leading with the weaker leg, so the body stays within the base of support. Pushing the walker far ahead forces the client to reach and lean forward, which shifts the center of gravity and increases the risk of falling. A standard walker is not used on stairs because it cannot be balanced safely on a single step. Leaning on one handle loads the walker unevenly and can tip it, so weight is spread through both hands.

Question 853BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Assess the client for injuries and report the concern according to agency policy and state law): possible abuse of a vulnerable adult requires an assessment for physical harm and a report through the proper channels, which nurses are legally required to make. Contacting the aide directly is an informal investigation that can alert the alleged abuser and place the client at greater risk. Asking the client to arrange a new aide shifts responsibility to a frightened client and does not trigger the required report. Documenting and delaying action leaves the client exposed to further harm until the next visit.

Question 854CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Stimulate the client, encourage deep breaths and apply oxygen as ordered): residual anesthesia is depressing ventilation, so arousing the client, coaching deep breaths and giving oxygen directly support airway and breathing. An opioid given now would deepen sedation and worsen the respiratory depression. Documenting and rechecking later delays treatment of hypoxemia that needs action now. Lying flat lets the tongue obstruct the airway and limits lung expansion, so the head of the bed is usually raised unless contraindicated.

Question 855CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Stop the feeding, keep the head of the bed elevated and assess the airway): these findings suggest aspiration, so stopping the feeding prevents more formula from entering the lungs while the nurse assesses breathing, suctions as needed and notifies the provider. Resuming the feeding based on a residual volume continues the aspiration that is already causing respiratory distress. Lowering the head of the bed increases reflux of formula and makes aspiration worse. Flushing the tube adds more fluid to the stomach and does nothing to protect the airway.

Question 856APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A ("I can't promise that, because I share information with the team to help you."): honest, consistent limits are essential with this disorder, and information relevant to care is shared with the treatment team. Promising secrecy sets up splitting among staff and could mean withholding information about safety. Refusing to listen rejects the client and closes off communication that may reveal important concerns. Choosing one staff member to share with still breaks the team approach and reinforces splitting.

Question 857BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Try less restrictive measures such as covering the tubing and increasing observation): restraints are a last resort, so the nurse first uses alternatives such as hiding the tubing, frequent checks and asking whether the catheter is still needed. Applying wrist restraints skips the less restrictive options that must be tried and documented first. Restraint orders cannot be written on an as-needed basis under federal rules. Four raised side rails act as a restraint and increase the risk of injury if a confused client climbs over them.

Question 858APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Blood glucose of 486 mg/dL, positive serum ketones and arterial pH of 7.18): diabetic ketoacidosis is defined by hyperglycemia, ketosis and metabolic acidosis, and these three findings together confirm it. Tachycardia, a near-normal temperature and mild hyponatremia can occur with many illnesses and do not define the condition. Mild hyperkalemia and signs of dehydration accompany the disorder but are also seen with simple vomiting. Without ketones and acidosis, a high glucose alone would not distinguish this condition from other hyperglycemic states.

Question 859CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Her body is blowing off carbon dioxide to compensate for the acid buildup): deep, rapid Kussmaul respirations are the respiratory system's compensation for metabolic acidosis, lowering carbon dioxide to raise the pH. Clear lungs without wheezing or chest tightness do not suggest an asthma flare. Anxiety may increase the rate of breathing, but the deep pattern with a pH of 7.18 reflects compensation for acidosis. Recognizing this pattern as compensation helps the nurse track improvement as the acidosis is corrected.

Question 860BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Severe fluid volume deficit and metabolic acidosis from uncontrolled ketone production): profound dehydration and acidosis threaten perfusion and cardiac function, so they are life-threatening problems treated first. Sick-day insulin teaching is important to prevent recurrence but is addressed once the client is stable. Feelings about living with diabetes deserve attention later, but they are not an immediate physiological threat. Using the airway, breathing and circulation framework keeps the focus on correcting volume loss and acidosis now.

Question 861BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Rapid infusion of an intravenous isotonic fluid such as 0.9% sodium chloride): isotonic intravenous fluid restores circulating volume and perfusion first, and the insulin infusion and electrolyte replacement follow according to protocol. Long-acting subcutaneous insulin alone is absorbed unpredictably and cannot be titrated to correct ketoacidosis. Oral fluids are inadequate for severe volume loss and are poorly tolerated by a client who has been vomiting. Fluid resuscitation also lowers glucose by dilution and improves kidney perfusion before insulin is started.

Question 862APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Serum potassium, because insulin drives potassium into cells and levels can fall quickly): insulin shifts potassium into the cells, so levels can drop rapidly and cause dangerous dysrhythmias, and flattened T waves suggest hypokalemia is developing. Insulin does not move sodium into cells, and sodium is monitored but is not the most urgent concern here. An insulin infusion does not cause hemolysis, so hemoglobin is not the priority value. Protocols add potassium to the fluids once levels fall into the normal range to prevent hypokalemia.

Question 863CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Anion gap closing, pH rising to 7.32 and a normal breathing pattern): a closing anion gap and rising pH show that ketoacids are being cleared, and the end of Kussmaul breathing confirms the acidosis is resolving. Rising glucose with ongoing deep, rapid breathing shows worsening hyperglycemia and persistent acidosis. A falling pH and bicarbonate indicate the acidosis is getting worse despite treatment. Resolution is judged by the anion gap, pH and bicarbonate, not by the glucose level alone.

Question 864AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The client is the person named and signed the form voluntarily): as a witness, the nurse attests to the client's identity and that the signature was given freely. Deciding whether the procedure is necessary is the surgeon's medical judgment, not something the witness confirms. Confirming that the client understands the risks and alternatives is part of the surgeon's informed consent discussion. Explaining the procedure is the surgeon's responsibility, and the witness signature does not mean the nurse provided that explanation.

Question 865BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Utensils with built-up foam handles that are easier to grasp): built-up handles increase the gripping surface so weak, painful fingers can hold utensils, and an occupational therapist can recommend specific devices. Heavy utensils help clients with tremor but are harder to lift and grasp with weak, painful hands. A narrow handle requires a tighter grip, which is more difficult for swollen finger joints. A glass without a handle is harder to hold securely than a mug with a large handle and is more likely to slip.

Question 866CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C ("It tells my care team what treatment I want if I can't speak for myself."): an advance directive records a person's own wishes for future care if they lose the ability to make or express decisions. An advance directive applies across care settings, including home, long-term care and hospice. The document expresses the client's own choices rather than handing decisions to the provider. A competent client can change or revoke an advance directive at any time.

Question 867DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Ventricular dysrhythmias such as torsades de pointes on the cardiac monitor): low magnesium, especially with low potassium, increases cardiac irritability and can cause life-threatening ventricular dysrhythmias. Flushing and warmth are signs of high magnesium, not the low level this client has. Bradycardia, hypotension and respiratory depression are features of magnesium excess. Absent reflexes suggest high magnesium, whereas a low level causes hyperactive reflexes and neuromuscular irritability.

Question 868APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (The client reports passing flatus several times during the past shift): passing gas shows that peristalsis has resumed and the bowel is moving contents forward. Increasing firmness and distension suggest ongoing ileus or obstruction rather than recovery. Nausea when suction is paused indicates that gastric contents are not yet moving through the bowel. Absent bowel sounds point to continued postoperative ileus.

Question 869DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D ("It sounds like you're worried about how they'll react; tell me more about that."): reflecting the feeling and inviting the client to say more keeps the focus on the client's concerns. Saying the children will be fine offers false reassurance and dismisses the client's worry. Suggesting a delay gives advice framed as a why question and cuts off exploration of the client's feelings. Sharing the nurse's personal experience shifts the focus away from the client.

Question 870APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Cool, pale and swollen skin surrounding the insertion site): infiltration occurs when fluid leaks into surrounding tissue, making the skin cool, pale and swollen. Redness, warmth and a palpable cord along the vein suggest phlebitis. Bleeding under the dressing points to a loose catheter or a clotting problem rather than infiltration. Fever and chills with tachycardia suggest a bloodstream infection or infusion reaction.

Question 871CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("Take it on an empty stomach and separate it from your other medicines."): sucralfate works best before meals and at bedtime, and it binds other drugs, so levothyroxine is taken at least 4 hours apart from it. Sucralfate forms a protective barrier over the ulcer rather than neutralizing stomach acid. Antacids interfere with sucralfate binding to the ulcer, so they are taken at least 30 minutes apart. Taking it after meals reduces its effectiveness because it should reach the ulcer on an empty stomach.

Question 872DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Assess the client's intent and plan, ensure safety and notify the provider): command hallucinations directing self-harm are a safety emergency, so the nurse assesses risk, starts safety measures and informs the team. Telling the client the voices are not real dismisses the experience and does not address the danger. Music may help with mild hallucinations later, but it does not assess or reduce an immediate self-harm risk. Documenting and continuing rounds leaves a client at risk of self-harm without protection.

Question 873DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("Rinse your mouth with water and spit after each use of the inhaler."): rinsing and spitting removes corticosteroid left in the mouth and throat, which lowers the risk of oral thrush. Changing the schedule without the provider's direction reduces asthma control and does not prevent thrush. Inhaled corticosteroids are daily controller medications and do not relieve an acute attack. Swallowing does not clear the drug from the mouth as well as rinsing and spitting.

Question 874DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Document the client's refusal of blood products and inform the surgical team): a competent adult has the right to refuse treatment, and documenting and communicating the decision lets the team plan blood conservation and alternatives. Enlisting the family to change the client's mind does not respect the client's autonomy. Telling the client that blood will be given anyway threatens to override a valid refusal and could constitute battery. Delaying documentation risks the refusal being missed by the surgical team.

Question 875AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Honor the client's valid order, support the son and notify the provider): a valid do-not-resuscitate order reflects the client's own decision and is followed, while the nurse offers emotional support and involves the provider. Starting resuscitation to calm a family member goes against the client's documented wishes. Removing a grieving son with security escalates the situation and withholds needed support. Beginning compressions while waiting for the provider still violates the valid order.

Question 876CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (A stimulant laxative such as senna, with or without a stool softener): opioids slow intestinal motility, so a bowel regimen built around a stimulant laxative is the standard approach for opioid-induced constipation. Loperamide slows the bowel further and would worsen constipation. Bulk-forming fiber alone can harden stool and cause impaction when motility is slowed by opioids. Antispasmodics reduce bowel motility and can make opioid-induced constipation worse.

Question 877BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Naloxone given intravenously in small doses to reverse opioid effects): naloxone is an opioid antagonist that reverses opioid-induced respiratory depression, and small titrated doses limit abrupt pain and withdrawal. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines and has no effect on morphine. Protamine reverses heparin and does not treat opioid toxicity. Atropine treats symptomatic bradycardia and does not reverse opioid-induced hypoventilation.

Question 878DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Review medications, follow-up appointments and warning signs with the client using teach-back): reviewing medications, appointments and warning signs with teach-back confirms understanding and supports a safe transition home. A printed list without explanation does not confirm that the client understands how to take the new medications. Inviting later calls relies on the client recognizing problems and does not check understanding before discharge. Rushing the discharge reduces time for teaching and increases the risk of readmission.

Question 879DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (A gain of 2 to 3 pounds in a day or 5 pounds in a week): rapid weight gain reflects fluid retention, and many heart failure programs use these thresholds unless the provider gives different instructions. A small loss of half a pound is a normal daily variation and may reflect successful fluid management. A half-pound gain over a week is within normal fluctuation and does not suggest significant fluid retention. A stable weight indicates that fluid balance is being maintained.

Question 880AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Notify the surgeon that the client needs more information before surgery proceeds): explaining the procedure, risks and alternatives is the surgeon's responsibility, and the nurse advocates by alerting the surgeon when understanding is lacking. A signature alone does not equal informed consent if the client was not adequately informed. The nurse can reinforce information, but providing the full explanation for informed consent belongs to the surgeon. A new signature after reading a description still lacks the surgeon's discussion and the chance to ask questions.

Question 881DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Open the airway, support ventilation with a bag-valve mask and give naloxone): these findings suggest opioid overdose, so airway, breathing and naloxone address the immediate threat to life. A urine drug screen does not change emergency treatment and would delay reversal of respiratory depression. Securing the syringe and searching belongings can follow once the client is breathing adequately. The recovery position is not enough for a client breathing only 6 times per minute who needs ventilation and reversal now.

Question 882BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Keep your intake of vitamin K foods like leafy greens about the same daily."): a consistent intake of vitamin K keeps the INR stable, and the warfarin dose is adjusted around the client's usual diet. Cutting out greens is unnecessary and could cause the INR to rise if the diet suddenly changes. Vitamin K counteracts warfarin, so large or sudden increases in greens can lower the INR. Eating salads only on weekends creates swings in vitamin K intake that make the INR fluctuate.

Question 883AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Look up the medication in a drug reference or consult the pharmacist): the nurse must know a medication's action, dose, adverse effects and nursing considerations before giving it, and references or a pharmacist provide that safely. The client's memory of past administration is not a reliable source of drug information. A verified order does not relieve the nurse of the responsibility to understand a medication before giving it. Handing off the client avoids the learning the nurse needs and shifts the assignment without good reason.

Question 884DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Assess the fetal heart rate for changes that suggest cord compression): after membranes rupture, the umbilical cord can prolapse or become compressed, so the fetal heart rate is checked first. A vaginal examination may be done later but is not the first priority and adds infection risk. Maternal temperature is monitored after rupture, but infection develops over hours and is not the immediate threat. Confirming rupture with nitrazine paper can wait until fetal well-being has been assessed.

Question 885ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A ("She'll ride rear-facing until she reaches the seat's highest height or weight limit."): infants and toddlers should stay rear-facing as long as possible, until they reach the height or weight limit set by the car seat manufacturer. An infant held on a lap can be thrown from the adult's arms in a crash, even on short trips. Sitting up is not a milestone for turning the seat, because rear-facing protects the head and neck far longer. A deploying front airbag can strike a rear-facing seat and cause fatal injury, so infants ride in the back seat.

Question 886CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C ("Everyone grieves differently, and there is no set timeline for grief."): grief is individual and varies in length and intensity, and the nurse can follow this response with support and bereavement resources. Suggesting grief resolves in two weeks sets an unrealistic expectation, especially after the loss of a child. Grief lasting longer than a month is normal and does not by itself indicate a disorder. Urging the parents to move past the loss quickly minimizes their grief and can increase guilt.

Question 887BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B ("I keep my pills in a weekly organizer up high, away from my grandkids."): a weekly organizer supports correct dosing, and storing it out of children's reach prevents accidental poisoning. Freezing destroys insulin, so unopened pens are stored in the refrigerator. Pills on an open counter are within reach of young children who visit often. Sharing prescription opioids is unsafe and illegal, and the medication is prescribed only for the client.

Question 888BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (New blood pressure of 154/100 with headache, seeing spots and 2+ proteinuria): new hypertension with a persistent headache, visual changes and proteinuria suggests preeclampsia with severe features. Mild swelling of the hands is common in pregnancy, and a fundal height matching gestational age is reassuring. A fetal heart rate of 140 with moderate variability is normal and reassuring. Recognizing this cluster quickly allows prompt evaluation and treatment before complications develop.

Question 889CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Preeclampsia with severe features related to new hypertension after 20 weeks): new hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria, headache and visual disturbances meets the criteria for preeclampsia with severe features. Gestational diabetes causes hyperglycemia, not new hypertension, headache and proteinuria. Blood pressure does not normally rise this much in the third trimester, and proteinuria with visual changes is not expected. Correctly identifying the condition guides urgent referral to the hospital for monitoring and treatment.

Question 890AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Progression to eclampsia with new onset of generalized tonic-clonic seizures): a worsening headache and light sensitivity signal cerebral irritability, and preeclampsia with severe features can progress to eclamptic seizures. Heartburn is common late in pregnancy, although new epigastric pain would need evaluation for liver involvement. Leg cramps are a common, minor discomfort of pregnancy and do not threaten the client or fetus. Seizures endanger both the client and the fetus, so seizure prevention becomes the priority.

Question 891CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Magnesium sulfate for seizure prevention and antihypertensive medication as ordered): magnesium sulfate prevents eclamptic seizures, and severe-range blood pressure is lowered with medications such as labetalol to prevent stroke. Increasing activity does not treat preeclampsia and is not a priority for a client at risk of seizures. A client with severe features requires inpatient monitoring and treatment, not discharge home. Delivery planning is also considered, because birth is the definitive treatment for preeclampsia.

Question 892AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Respiratory rate, deep tendon reflexes, level of consciousness and hourly urine output): loss of deep tendon reflexes is usually the first sign of magnesium toxicity, followed by sedation and respiratory depression, and urine output matters because magnesium is excreted by the kidneys. Magnesium sulfate does not cause hyperglycemia or ototoxicity, so these checks do not detect its adverse effects. Pupil size and daily weight do not identify magnesium toxicity in time to prevent harm. Calcium gluconate is kept available to reverse toxicity if these assessments become abnormal.

Question 893BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Respiratory rate of 10 breaths per minute with absent patellar reflexes): respiratory depression and loss of deep tendon reflexes indicate magnesium toxicity, so the infusion is stopped and the provider notified. A urine output of 40 mL per hour is above the usual minimum of 30 mL per hour and supports magnesium excretion. Reflexes of 2+ and a respiratory rate of 16 are normal findings during therapy. The nurse also prepares calcium gluconate as the antidote while a serum magnesium level is drawn.

Question 894DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Give 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate and recheck glucose in 15 minutes): the rule of 15 quickly treats hypoglycemia in a client who can swallow, and the treatment is repeated if glucose stays below 70 mg/dL. Delaying treatment for an hour allows the glucose to fall further and risks confusion or seizures. Insulin would lower the glucose even more and worsen the hypoglycemia. A full meal raises glucose too slowly at first, so a snack or meal follows once glucose has recovered.

Question 895AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (When the client is unable to make or communicate health care decisions): a health care proxy acts only when the client lacks capacity to make or communicate decisions, and while the client has capacity, the client decides. Family requests do not activate the proxy's authority while the client can decide for herself. Admission to a facility does not remove a capable client's right to make decisions. A dementia diagnosis alone does not mean the client lacks capacity for a specific decision.

Question 896DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Deep breathing exercises, regular physical activity and cognitive behavioral techniques): relaxation breathing, exercise and cognitive behavioral strategies are evidence-based ways to reduce anxiety symptoms. Caffeine is a stimulant that can increase restlessness, palpitations and anxiety. Alcohol may relax briefly but disrupts sleep and can worsen anxiety and lead to dependence. Daytime napping disrupts the sleep cycle, and poor sleep tends to worsen anxiety.

Question 897DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Add both supplements to the medication list and inform the provider): reconciliation includes over-the-counter products and supplements because they can interact with prescribed drugs, and the provider decides whether to continue them. Clients should not take their own supplements in the hospital without an order. Leaving supplements off the list hides possible interactions, such as increased bleeding risk or added sedation. Stopping the supplements is a decision for the provider, not the nurse.

Question 898DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("I'll take it every day, even on days when I feel fine."): hypertension usually causes no symptoms, and antihypertensives control blood pressure only while they are taken daily. Normal readings show the medication is working, and stopping it allows blood pressure to rise again. Doubling a dose after a missed one can cause hypotension and dizziness. Headache is not a reliable sign of high blood pressure, so taking the drug only with symptoms leaves pressure uncontrolled.

Question 899BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B ("External beam treatment does not make your spouse radioactive, so no precautions are needed."): external beam radiation does not leave a radiation source in the body, so others need no special precautions. Distance precautions apply to clients with internal sealed or unsealed radiation sources, not external beam therapy. Children and pregnant visitors are restricted around internal radiation sources, but external beam therapy poses no risk to them. Radiation from an external beam stops when the machine is turned off, so time limits on visits are unnecessary.

Question 900DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D ("Protein helps your body build new tissue and heal the wound."): adequate protein supplies the building blocks for collagen and new tissue, which supports wound healing, and the dietitian can help plan intake. Protein supports muscle, but its key role here is repairing the damaged skin and tissue. Pressure injuries heal best in a moist environment, and protein does not dry out the wound. Carbohydrates and fats are the main energy sources, and protein is needed for tissue repair.
