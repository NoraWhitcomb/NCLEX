Question 1CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (The client had abdominal surgery two weeks ago with significant postoperative bleeding): recent major surgery and recent bleeding sharply raise the risk of hemorrhage with alteplase and may make the client ineligible for thrombolysis. A childhood history of migraine does not affect eligibility for thrombolytic therapy in an acute ischemic stroke. A family history of diabetes is useful for risk-factor teaching but does not change the decision to give alteplase. Seasonal allergies treated with an antihistamine pose no bleeding risk and do not delay thrombolysis.

Question 2BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Arterial oxygen remains low despite increases in the fraction of inspired oxygen): acute respiratory distress syndrome causes refractory hypoxemia, so arterial oxygen stays low even as inspired oxygen is increased. Imaging in this condition shows bilateral infiltrates, so clear lung fields argue against the diagnosis. The edema is noncardiogenic, so an elevated wedge pressure from left ventricular failure points to cardiogenic pulmonary edema instead. Clients with this syndrome typically breathe rapidly and with great effort, so slow breathing is not a characteristic finding.

Question 3BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Sitting upright on the edge of the bed, leaning forward over a bedside table): sitting upright and leaning forward with the arms supported exposes the back and widens the spaces between the ribs so the clinician can reach the fluid safely, usually with ultrasound guidance. A supine position with slight head elevation spreads the fluid along the back and makes the posterior insertion site hard to reach. Lying on the affected side places the puncture site against the mattress, so a client unable to sit lies with the affected side up instead. Trendelenburg position shifts fluid away from the lung base and pushes abdominal contents against the diaphragm, worsening breathing.

Question 4BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (A client with diabetes, stable glucose levels, and discharge already planned for tomorrow): this client is medically stable and nearly ready to leave, so early discharge with follow-up poses the least risk. A continuous heparin infusion requires frequent laboratory monitoring and dose adjustments that can only be done in the hospital. Chest pain that began four hours ago has not been ruled out as acute coronary syndrome, so the client still needs cardiac monitoring. A new fever the day after abdominal surgery may signal a developing complication that requires evaluation before discharge.

Question 5CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C ("Keep your arm raised above the level of your heart for the first few days."): elevating the casted arm above heart level reduces swelling and lowers the risk of impaired circulation during the first days. Numbness or tingling can signal nerve compression or compartment syndrome and must be reported right away rather than treated with pain medicine. Letting the arm hang down promotes dependent edema, which can make the cast too tight. Inserting objects under the cast can break the skin and lead to infection, so itching is managed with cool air instead.

Question 6AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Esophageal perforation causing leakage of contents into the mediastinum): severe chest pain, fever, tachycardia and painful swallowing after endoscopy with dilation strongly suggest perforation, which is an emergency. Dehydration from fasting may cause thirst and mild tachycardia but does not explain severe chest pain with fever. Mild throat soreness is common after endoscopy, but severe pain radiating to the back with fever is not an expected finding. An allergic reaction to the anesthetic spray would cause hives, swelling or wheezing soon after use rather than fever hours later.

Question 7DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Apply an electronic monitoring bracelet that activates the exit alarm according to policy): electronic monitoring alerts staff when the client approaches an exit while preserving the client's freedom to move about the unit. Locking a client inside a room is a form of seclusion that creates a fire hazard and violates the client's rights. Restraints increase agitation and the risk of injury and are reserved for situations in which less restrictive measures have failed. Routine sedation acts as a chemical restraint and raises the risk of falls and confusion in an older adult with dementia.

Question 8CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Assist the client to the bathroom to empty her bladder, then reassess): a fundus displaced to one side usually means a distended bladder, which can keep the uterus from contracting well, so emptying the bladder comes first. The fundal height is expected, but lateral deviation is not normal and needs intervention before documentation. The fundus is already firm, so massage is unnecessary and causes pain without correcting the cause of the displacement. Moderate lochia, a firm fundus and normal vital signs do not indicate hemorrhage, so notifying the provider is premature.

Question 9CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Sundowning, an increase in confusion and agitation late in the day): sundowning describes the late afternoon or evening increase in confusion, restlessness and agitation commonly seen in people with dementia. Catatonia involves immobility, mutism or posturing rather than restless pacing that follows a daily pattern. Delirium tremens results from alcohol withdrawal and causes tremors, autonomic instability and hallucinations, which do not fit this history. Mania is a mood episode of bipolar disorder that lasts days and does not recur each afternoon after a calm morning.

Question 10AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A ("The test uses sound waves to show how your heart pumps, and it is painless."): a transthoracic echocardiogram uses ultrasound from a transducer placed on the chest, so it is noninvasive and painless. Ultrasound does not use ionizing radiation, which distinguishes it from imaging studies such as computed tomography. No prolonged fasting is required for a transthoracic study, although fasting is needed for the transesophageal approach. Threading a catheter into the heart describes cardiac catheterization, which is an invasive procedure.

Question 11BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Activated partial thromboplastin time or anti-Xa level according to facility protocol): heparin infusions are titrated using the activated partial thromboplastin time or anti-factor Xa level as directed by protocol. The international normalized ratio is used to monitor warfarin and does not reliably reflect heparin effect. Pancreatitis is not a recognized effect of heparin, so a lipase level does not guide the infusion rate. Albumin level does not measure anticoagulant effect and is not used to adjust heparin dosing.

Question 12DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (A client with labored breathing at 34 breaths per minute and a palpable pulse): a respiratory rate above 30 signals a life-threatening but treatable problem, which meets criteria for immediate care in mass casualty triage. An open fracture with stable vital signs is serious but can be delayed and is tagged yellow. A client who can walk is classified as minor and tagged green. A client who does not breathe after airway repositioning is tagged expectant, because scarce resources are directed to those who can survive.

Question 13BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Draw a single line through the entry, mark it as an error, and initial it): a single line keeps the original entry readable, and labeling and initialing it according to policy shows who made the correction. Correction fluid hides the original entry and can suggest tampering with a legal document. Blacking out an entry makes the original information unreadable, which compromises the integrity of the record. Removing and recopying a page destroys part of the legal record and is treated as altering documentation.

Question 14DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Normal clotting function that requires no further action before the scheduled surgery): an international normalized ratio of about 1.0 is normal for a client not taking warfarin and indicates an expected clotting time. Therapeutic anticoagulation with warfarin usually targets a ratio of 2 to 3, so 1.0 does not reflect anticoagulant effect. Liver failure reduces clotting factor production and prolongs the ratio, which is not seen here. A normal value does not indicate an increased bleeding risk, so there is nothing to report to the surgeon.

Question 15AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Turn the client onto her side and begin intrauterine resuscitation measures per protocol): late decelerations reflect reduced uteroplacental blood flow, and side-lying relieves pressure on the vena cava while protocol steps such as stopping oxytocin, giving fluids and notifying the provider follow. Monitoring and documenting alone delays action while the fetus may be experiencing hypoxia. Pushing is appropriate only at full dilation and would not correct the decreased placental perfusion. Increasing oxytocin produces stronger, more frequent contractions that further reduce placental blood flow and worsen the decelerations.

Question 16CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C ("It helps your baby's blood clot and prevents a serious bleeding problem."): newborns have low vitamin K stores because little crosses the placenta and breast milk contains little, so the injection prevents vitamin K deficiency bleeding. Vitamin K does not affect immune function, and protection against infection comes from breastfeeding and newborn immunizations. Jaundice is managed with feeding and phototherapy when needed, and vitamin K does not lower bilirubin. Eye infection is prevented with erythromycin ointment, which is a separate newborn medication.

Question 17ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Speak up right away and ask the colleague to perform hand hygiene first): speaking up before the procedure continues prevents contamination, and a culture of safety expects staff to raise concerns respectfully and immediately. Waiting until afterward allows a preventable break in technique that may cause a catheter-associated infection. Reporting later may be appropriate under policy, but it does not protect this client now. Gloves can have microscopic defects and hands can contaminate them during donning, so gloves do not replace hand hygiene.

Question 18DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("I will count my baby's movements every day and call if they decrease."): daily monitoring of fetal movement helps identify a decrease early, and a decrease is reported promptly for evaluation. Fetal movements should not decline near term, so reduced movement needs prompt evaluation rather than reassurance. Counting only when worried misses gradual changes that a daily routine would detect. Weekly counts are too infrequent to detect a change in fetal well-being in time to act.

Question 19CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C ("Wash your face gently twice a day and avoid squeezing or picking pimples."): gentle cleansing twice daily removes oil without irritating the skin, and avoiding picking reduces inflammation and scarring. Squeezing lesions pushes bacteria and debris deeper into the skin and increases the risk of scarring. Diet is not the main cause of acne, which results from hormones, oil production, clogged follicles and bacteria. Harsh, frequent scrubbing irritates the skin and can worsen inflammation.

Question 20DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Performing hand hygiene before and after every contact with a client or environment): hand hygiene is the single most effective measure for preventing the transmission of organisms in health care settings. Gloves can become contaminated and do not replace hand hygiene, which is still required before and after glove use. Closed doors matter for airborne isolation but do little to stop contact transmission, the most common route. Routine prophylactic antibiotics promote resistance and are reserved for specific indications rather than general infection prevention.

Question 21BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (A fruity odor on the breath with rapid, deep respirations): ketone production causes a fruity breath odor, and Kussmaul respirations develop as the body blows off carbon dioxide to compensate for metabolic acidosis. Hyperglycemia causes osmotic diuresis with large urine output and intense thirst, not low urine output without thirst. Diabetic ketoacidosis is usually defined by glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher, so a value of 90 does not fit this presentation. Fluid losses cause tachycardia and weight loss, so a slow heart rate with weight gain does not fit.

Question 22CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Allow time for the handwashing ritual while gradually working with the client to limit it): abruptly blocking a ritual raises anxiety, so the nurse first allows time for it and then helps the client reduce it gradually. Stopping the client from washing removes the client's means of managing anxiety and can escalate distress. Removing soap is a restrictive approach that provokes anxiety without teaching the client alternative coping. Threatening lost privileges is punitive and does not address the anxiety that drives the compulsion.

Question 23DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Semi-Fowler's or high Fowler's position with the head of the bed elevated): an upright position lowers the diaphragm, improves lung expansion and eases the work of breathing in a hemodynamically stable client. Prone positioning is used for selected ventilated clients with severe respiratory distress and is not routine care for an alert client with an embolism. Lying flat allows abdominal contents to press on the diaphragm and worsens dyspnea. Trendelenburg position restricts lung expansion and is no longer recommended even for hypotension.

Question 24AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (A client two days after an ischemic stroke who reports a sudden, severe headache): a sudden severe headache after a stroke may signal hemorrhagic conversion or rising intracranial pressure and requires immediate assessment. Itching under a cast is common and uncomfortable but is not an emergency when circulation is intact. Clients with heart failure are taught to report a gain of 2 to 3 pounds in a day, so a 1-pound gain is less urgent. Burning on urination is an expected symptom of a urinary tract infection that is already being treated.

Question 25BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Assess the client's immediate safety in private and provide information about available resources): the nurse first assesses immediate danger and helps with a safety plan in a private setting, then shares resources while respecting the client's choices. Involving the partner can escalate the danger and breaks the client's confidentiality. Decisions about leaving belong to the client, and leaving can be a dangerous time, so the nurse supports planning rather than giving directives. Documentation and referral are appropriate later, but they do not address the client's immediate safety.

Question 26AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Ask the client about the reasons for refusing the medication this morning): exploring the reason allows the nurse to identify concerns such as side effects or misunderstandings that may be addressed. Hiding medication in food without consent violates the client's right to refuse treatment. Documenting and notifying the provider are appropriate after the nurse has explored the refusal, but they are not the first step. Insisting the dose must be taken disregards the competent client's autonomy and does not uncover the underlying concern.

Question 27AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Pain that increases with passive stretching of the toes despite recent opioid medication): pain out of proportion that increases with passive stretch and is not relieved by opioids is an early sign of compartment syndrome, a surgical emergency. Itching under a cast is common and is managed with comfort measures. A new cast often feels heavy, which is expected and not a sign of compromise. Warm, pink toes with brisk refill indicate adequate perfusion, and mild swelling is expected after surgery.

The correct answer is A (The heart rate of 116 combined with new, severe abdominal pain): new tachycardia with severe abdominal pain the day after bowel surgery is a change in condition that may signal a serious complication. Question 29CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (A postoperative complication such as bleeding, anastomotic leak, or developing infection): new severe pain with tachycardia after bowel surgery must be evaluated for bleeding, an anastomotic leak or infection. Expected incisional pain improves with analgesia and does not suddenly worsen to 8 out of 10 with a rising heart rate. Anxiety is a diagnosis of exclusion that should not be assumed until physical causes are ruled out. Treating these findings as routine could delay recognition of a life-threatening problem.

Question 30BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Perform a focused assessment of the client at the bedside right now): a client with new tachycardia and severe pain needs a focused assessment immediately, because physical findings guide every next step. Finishing handoff away from the bedside delays assessment of a client whose condition may be deteriorating. Updating the whiteboard is useful communication but is a lower priority than evaluating the client. Assessment data gathered now will also make any report to the surgeon accurate and complete.

Question 31BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Ask the day nurse to stay briefly so they can assess the client together): a joint bedside assessment lets both nurses verify the change, clarify the trend and share responsibility for timely action. Delaying reassessment until after rounds leaves a possibly deteriorating client without evaluation. Calling later loses the chance to clarify information in person while the day nurse can still help. Respectfully asking a colleague to stay is an appropriate form of client advocacy during handoff.

Question 32CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Notify the surgeon promptly using SBAR to report the rigid abdomen and vital signs): a rigid, tender abdomen with fever and tachycardia suggests peritonitis from a possible anastomotic leak, which requires immediate surgical evaluation. Oral intake should be held in a client with a possible acute abdomen, since surgery may be needed. Laxatives can worsen a leak or perforation and are contraindicated when peritonitis is suspected. A structured report gives the surgeon the situation, background, assessment and recommendation needed to act quickly.

Question 33AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The client's change in condition was recognized and treated without delay): effective handoff and advocacy are measured by the client outcome, and here the deterioration was identified and escalated promptly. Finishing handoff on schedule reflects efficiency but would have been a poor outcome if the client's change had been missed. Documenting pain and waiting for the next scheduled check would have delayed treatment of a surgical emergency. Recognizing and escalating changes at handoff is a key safeguard for clients after surgery.

Question 34BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Transfusion-associated circulatory overload from the added volume in a client with heart failure): an older adult with heart failure who develops dyspnea, crackles and hypertension during transfusion most likely has volume overload, so the nurse stops the transfusion, raises the head of the bed and notifies the provider. Transfusion-related acute lung injury also causes dyspnea, but it typically presents with hypotension and fever rather than hypertension. A mild allergic reaction causes hives and itching without crackles or rising blood pressure. A febrile nonhemolytic reaction is marked by fever and chills, and this client's temperature is unchanged.

Question 35AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Write down the result, read it back, and notify the provider promptly): critical results are recorded, read back to confirm accuracy and reported to the provider promptly, especially with electrocardiogram changes. Waiting for a written report delays treatment of a potassium level that can cause a fatal dysrhythmia. Discussing the result with the client may follow, but provider notification for treatment comes first. Relying on memory risks errors, and waiting until rounds delays urgent treatment.

Question 36BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Provide a quiet, low-stimulation environment and offer high-calorie finger foods frequently): reducing stimulation helps calm a manic client, and portable finger foods meet nutritional needs for a client who cannot sit for meals. Television and dayroom noise add stimulation that can worsen agitation. Competitive group games increase stimulation and may provoke conflict with other clients. A manic client has a short attention span and cannot focus on a long written task, which may increase frustration.

Question 37CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (A history of seizures or a current eating disorder such as bulimia): bupropion lowers the seizure threshold and is contraindicated in clients with a seizure disorder or a current or prior diagnosis of bulimia or anorexia. A healed fracture has no interaction with bupropion and does not affect its safety. Seasonal allergies treated with a nasal steroid do not increase the risks of this medication. Nearsightedness is unrelated to bupropion therapy and needs no report.

Question 38DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Serum creatinine of 2.1 mg/dL, increased from a baseline of 1.0 mg/dL): iodinated contrast can worsen kidney function, so a rising creatinine is reported so the provider can weigh the risks and adjust the plan or order hydration. A glucose of 112 is acceptable for a client with diabetes and does not affect contrast safety. A recent dental cleaning has no bearing on contrast administration. Seasonal pollen allergy only slightly raises reaction risk and needs no premedication, unlike a prior reaction to contrast.

Question 39DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Place a surgical mask on the client for the duration of the transport): clients on droplet precautions wear a surgical mask outside the room to contain respiratory droplets at the source. N95 respirators are worn by staff for airborne precautions and are not needed by the client for droplet transmission. Precautions continue outside the room, because others along the route can still be exposed. A gown and gloves address contact transmission and do not block respiratory droplets.

Question 40CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Fever and chills during infusion, kidney toxicity, and low potassium and magnesium): this antifungal commonly causes infusion reactions such as fever and rigors and can damage the kidneys, causing losses of potassium and magnesium. Amphotericin more often causes nausea, anorexia and weight loss than increased appetite. Hair growth and skin darkening are not recognized effects of this drug. Hyperglycemia is not the main concern, while kidney function and electrolytes require daily monitoring.

Question 41BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Set clear, consistent limits on behavior that every staff member enforces): consistent limits enforced by all staff remove opportunities to split the team and reduce manipulative behavior. Making exceptions rewards manipulation and encourages further testing of limits. Letting the client set personal rules gives control that the client may use to exploit staff. Arguing with the client invites power struggles and keeps the focus on the manipulation.

Question 42DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Three tablets, which provide the full prescribed dose of 15 mg): the dose is calculated by dividing 15 mg by 5 mg per tablet, which equals 3 tablets. Giving 5 tablets confuses the tablet strength with the number of tablets and would deliver 25 mg. Two tablets deliver only 10 mg, which is less than the prescribed dose. One and a half tablets deliver only 7.5 mg, half of the prescribed amount.

Question 43DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Bright red drainage appearing in the tubing and the collection canister): bright red drainage suggests active bleeding in the upper gastrointestinal tract and requires prompt reporting. Mild throat discomfort is a common effect of the tube and is managed with comfort measures. Small amounts of clear drainage are an expected finding with gastric suction. Green drainage reflects bile and is common with intestinal obstruction and nasogastric suction.

Question 44BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B ("It partially activates opioid receptors to reduce withdrawal symptoms and cravings."): buprenorphine is a partial opioid agonist that relieves withdrawal and cravings while limiting euphoria and respiratory depression. Alcohol withdrawal is treated with benzodiazepines, not with buprenorphine. Causing unpleasant effects with use describes aversive therapy such as disulfiram for alcohol, not buprenorphine. Opioid use disorder is a chronic condition, and buprenorphine is often continued long term rather than used as a short cure.

Question 45CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Offer scheduled toileting before sleep and keep night lights on in rooms): toileting before sleep reduces the urge to get up alone, and night lights improve visibility if the client does get up. Total darkness increases the risk of tripping when a client gets up during the night. A high bed increases the distance and injury risk of a fall, so beds are kept in the low position. Items placed out of reach prompt clients to get up unassisted, which increases fall risk.

Question 46DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Poor tissue perfusion with anaerobic metabolism, consistent with septic shock): a lactate of 4 mmol/L or higher with hypotension reflects significant tissue hypoperfusion and is a key finding in septic shock. Hyperthyroidism can cause fever and tachycardia but does not explain hypotension with this lactate level in a client with infection. Fluid overload would cause crackles and edema and would not raise lactate with hypotension. A normal lactate is below 2 mmol/L, so this level is not a normal response to fever.

Question 47DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (A client recovering from an uncomplicated inguinal hernia repair with no signs of infection): an immunocompromised client should share a room with someone who has no infection, and a clean surgical recovery poses the least risk. Pneumonia can spread to a neutropenic client even after antibiotics have started. A fever of unknown cause may be infectious and makes the client an unsafe roommate. An infected wound is a source of organisms that can be transmitted to a client with neutropenia.

Question 48DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Bargaining, in which the person dwells on what might have changed the outcome): statements that begin with "if only" are typical of bargaining, when the bereaved person searches for what might have changed the outcome. Acceptance involves acknowledging the loss and reinvesting in life, which this guilt-focused statement does not show. Denial would involve disbelief that the death occurred, yet the client clearly acknowledges it. Anger would be directed outward at others or fate, while this statement turns blame inward, and grief stages do not follow a fixed order.

Question 49BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Give 7.5 mL of suspension, which delivers the prescribed 150 mg dose): 15 kg multiplied by 10 mg/kg equals 150 mg, and 150 mg multiplied by 5 mL per 100 mg equals 7.5 mL. Three milliliters delivers only 60 mg, which is less than half of the prescribed dose. Fifteen milliliters delivers 300 mg, double the prescribed dose, because it confuses weight with volume. One and a half milliliters delivers only 30 mg, which would underdose the child and fail to treat the infection.

Question 50CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Dark, concentrated urine in small amounts with dry oral mucous membranes): small amounts of dark urine and dry mucous membranes reflect fluid volume deficit as the kidneys conserve water. Weight gain over a day reflects fluid retention, not fluid loss. Bilateral pitting edema indicates fluid excess in the interstitial space. Moist mucous membranes indicate adequate hydration rather than a fluid deficit.

Question 51DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (The client is showing signs of progressing sepsis and possible septic shock): rising temperature, heart rate and respiratory rate with falling blood pressure and new confusion signal worsening sepsis with poor organ perfusion that needs immediate escalation. Simple dehydration does not explain new confusion with a blood pressure of 86/48, and oral fluids cannot correct this degree of hypotension. Attributing new confusion to anxiety overlooks reduced brain perfusion, a key sign of septic shock. A steadily worsening pattern over eight hours is not an expected antibiotic response and requires urgent reassessment of the treatment plan.

Question 52ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (The Wong-Baker FACES scale, asking the child to point to a face): the FACES scale uses simple drawings that a verbal preschool child can understand and use to self-report pain intensity. A numeric scale requires an understanding of numbers and their order, so it is generally reserved for children about 8 years and older. A visual analog line demands abstract thinking about a continuous scale, so it is suited to adolescents and adults. The CRIES scale is a behavioral and physiological tool designed for neonates after surgery, not for a verbal preschooler.

Question 53DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Give a structured bedside handoff covering condition, recent changes and pending results): a structured bedside handoff shares the client's status, changes and pending tasks while allowing questions and a joint check of the client. A written summary alone prevents the oncoming nurse from asking questions or clarifying the pending results. Referring the oncoming nurse to the record shifts the burden and risks missing the new fever and outstanding laboratory work. A report at the station focused on diagnoses and medications omits recent changes and pending tasks and lacks a bedside check.

Question 54BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Report the platelet drop to the provider immediately as possible heparin-induced thrombocytopenia): a platelet fall of more than 50 percent between days 5 and 10 of heparin suggests heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, which carries a high risk of new clots. Continuing heparin until tomorrow exposes the client to further immune-mediated platelet activation and possible thrombosis. Raising the heparin rate worsens the underlying problem, because heparin itself is the trigger. Platelet transfusion is generally avoided in this condition unless there is serious bleeding, because it may promote clotting.

Question 55AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (The pancreas, from inflammation that releases digestive enzymes into the blood): amylase and lipase are pancreatic enzymes, and levels above three times normal with epigastric pain radiating to the back meet criteria for acute pancreatitis. Alcohol-related liver injury raises liver enzymes and bilirubin rather than markedly elevating lipase. Acute cholecystitis typically causes right upper quadrant pain and does not by itself raise lipase to this degree. A peptic ulcer causes epigastric pain related to meals, but lipase above three times normal points to the pancreas.

Question 56DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (At least 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly plus muscle strengthening on 2 days): the Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans advise at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity each week plus muscle strengthening on 2 or more days. Thirty minutes on 3 days totals only 90 minutes, which falls short of the weekly aerobic goal. One 60-minute vigorous session is less than the 75 vigorous minutes recommended weekly and lacks muscle strengthening. The guidelines do not set a step target, and muscle strengthening is advised for all adults, not only those trying to lose weight.

Question 57DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("It may help memory and thinking for a time, but it does not cure the disease."): cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil can modestly improve or temporarily stabilize symptoms, but they do not cure Alzheimer's disease or stop its progression. Donepezil does not restore lost memory, and any benefit is modest and develops over weeks. Behavior changes can still occur, so other prescribed medicines are not stopped without the provider's direction. Donepezil is taken daily on a regular schedule, and intermittent dosing based on symptoms is ineffective.

Question 58BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Hives, wheezing, throat tightness and a blood pressure of 84/50 after the infusion): sudden skin, airway and circulatory involvement within minutes of exposure to a new drug matches the clinical picture of anaphylaxis. Redness and itching along the vein suggest local irritation or phlebitis and would not explain wheezing or hypotension. A vague childhood rash and anxiety are relevant history but do not by themselves show that a systemic reaction is occurring. Recognizing the multisystem pattern early allows the nurse to act before airway swelling and shock progress.

Question 59CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Distributive shock from widespread vasodilation and fluid leaking out of the capillaries): mediators released in anaphylaxis cause massive vasodilation and increased capillary permeability, so blood pools in the vessels and fluid shifts into the tissues. Insensible fluid loss from pneumonia develops over hours to days and would not drop the pressure this sharply within minutes. Cardiogenic shock results from pump failure, typically with cool skin and crackles, rather than flushed skin and hives after a drug exposure. The rising heart rate is a compensatory response to the falling pressure caused by the vasodilation.

Question 60APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Compromised airway and circulation from swelling, bronchospasm and falling blood pressure): throat tightness, wheezing and hypotension threaten oxygenation and perfusion and can lead to airway obstruction and cardiovascular collapse within minutes. Itching and hives are uncomfortable but are not immediately life-threatening and improve as the reaction is treated. Anxiety is a real concern, yet it often results from hypoxia and shock and eases once airway and circulation are supported. Using the airway, breathing and circulation framework directs the nurse to the problems most likely to cause death first.

Question 61APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Epinephrine 0.5 mg intramuscularly into the outer thigh per the protocol): intramuscular epinephrine is first-line therapy because it constricts blood vessels, relaxes bronchial muscle and reduces airway swelling, and 0.5 mg is the adult maximum single dose. Diphenhydramine relieves itching and hives but does not reverse hypotension or airway obstruction, so it is an adjunct after epinephrine. Methylprednisolone takes hours to act and does not treat the immediate airway and circulatory threat. Delaying epinephrine while giving adjunct drugs is linked with worse outcomes, including death, in anaphylaxis.

Question 62BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Stop the ceftriaxone infusion and call for emergency help while staying with the client): stopping the trigger limits further exposure, and calling for help brings the team and epinephrine to the bedside while the nurse stays with the client. Slowing the infusion still delivers the drug that is causing the reaction and delays emergency treatment. Flushing the line pushes the antibiotic left in the tubing into the bloodstream, increasing the dose of the trigger. The nurse keeps the intravenous access in place, because it will be needed for fluid boluses and other emergency medications.

Question 63CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Blood pressure 118/74, wheezing resolved and the client reports the throat tightness is gone): a normal blood pressure with resolved wheezing and throat tightness shows that perfusion and the airway have improved, although the client still needs monitoring for a biphasic reaction. Less itching and fewer hives are welcome, but a blood pressure of 88/52 shows ongoing shock that needs further treatment. New hoarseness and stridor indicate worsening upper airway swelling despite the fading skin signs. Skin findings can improve while airway or circulatory problems persist, so they are not reliable indicators of response on their own.

Question 64BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Helping the client return to at least the level of functioning before the crisis): crisis intervention is short term and focuses on restoring the client's previous level of functioning by strengthening coping skills and support. Exploring childhood experiences belongs to longer-term insight-oriented therapy rather than immediate crisis work. Long-term psychotherapy aimed at changing personality is beyond the scope and time frame of crisis intervention. A sleep medication may ease one symptom, but it does not address problem solving, coping or support, which are the focus of crisis care.

Question 65DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A 3-year-old with patterned bruises that do not match the parent's description of a fall): nurses are mandated reporters of suspected child abuse, and injuries that do not fit the explanation given are a key warning sign. Tobacco use in a teen calls for cessation counseling but is not a reportable situation. Declining an influenza vaccine is a parental choice that calls for education and documentation rather than reporting. A teen's wish for privacy about sexual history is appropriate, and the nurse can offer to speak with the teen alone.

Question 66DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Assess the client and give treatment for the chest pain as ordered): a do-not-resuscitate order applies only to resuscitation during cardiac or respiratory arrest, so the client still receives assessment and treatment for chest pain. Limiting care to comfort measures misinterprets the order, which does not restrict other indicated treatments. The alert client is the decision maker, so the family is not asked to decide on care the client can consent to. The client has a pulse and is breathing, so a cardiac arrest response is not indicated.

Question 67DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (A scheduled toileting program with assistance to the bathroom every 2 hours): scheduled toileting retrains the bladder and helps the client reach the toilet before urgency leads to leakage. Adult briefs manage leakage but do nothing to restore continence and may reduce the client's motivation to use the toilet. An indwelling catheter carries a high risk of catheter-associated urinary tract infection and is not indicated for incontinence alone. Severe fluid restriction concentrates the urine, which irritates the bladder, worsens urgency and raises the risk of dehydration.

Question 68BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (A rising heart rate accompanied by new restlessness and anxiety): the body first compensates for blood loss by increasing heart rate, and reduced cerebral perfusion produces restlessness before blood pressure drops. Hypotension usually appears later, after significant blood volume has been lost and compensation fails. Hemorrhage causes cool, pale skin from vasoconstriction rather than warm, flushed skin. Bradycardia is not an expected early response to blood loss, which typically produces tachycardia.

Question 69DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A client 1 day after thyroidectomy who reports tingling around the mouth): perioral tingling after thyroidectomy suggests low calcium from parathyroid injury, which can progress to tetany and laryngospasm. A glucose of 210 mg/dL needs follow-up with the insulin plan but is not an immediate threat. Chronic back pain at 5 out of 10 deserves timely treatment but is stable and expected. Anxiety about discharge is important to address with teaching, but it does not pose a physiological threat.

Question 70CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Clipping hair just before surgery if needed and using antiseptic skin preparation): clipping avoids the tiny skin cuts caused by razors, and antiseptic skin preparation lowers bacterial counts at the incision site. Razor shaving creates microscopic nicks that bacteria colonize, and shaving the night before allows more time for growth. Saline-soaked dressings on a closed incision promote maceration and bacterial growth rather than protecting the wound. Frequent dressing removal exposes the fresh incision to contamination, so the initial sterile dressing is usually left in place for 24 to 48 hours.

Question 71ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Apply cold for the first day or two, then switch to warmth as directed): cold limits inflammation and swelling in the first 24 to 48 hours, and warmth later relaxes the muscles and improves blood flow. Heat applied early increases blood flow to the area and can worsen swelling. Prolonged rest promotes stiffness and deconditioning, whereas gentle activity helps sore muscles recover. Vigorous hourly massage can aggravate swollen tissue, and deep massage is avoided whenever a blood clot is a possibility.

Question 72DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Measuring the apical pulse for a full minute and checking the blood pressure): metoprolol slows the heart rate and lowers blood pressure, so the nurse checks both against the hold parameters before giving the dose. Bowel sounds are not affected in a clinically important way by beta blockers and do not guide this dose. Pupil checks assess neurologic status but do not detect the bradycardia or hypotension that metoprolol can cause. Temperature has no bearing on the safety of a beta blocker dose.

Question 73BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (The serum potassium level, because a low value increases the risk of digoxin toxicity): furosemide causes potassium loss, and hypokalemia increases digoxin binding to heart muscle, raising the risk of toxicity, of which nausea can be an early sign. Loop diuretics increase calcium excretion rather than causing calcium to build up. Cholesterol matters for long-term cardiovascular risk but is not the key safety value for this drug combination. Digoxin does not commonly raise blood glucose, so glucose is not the priority value here.

Question 74CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Contact the chaplain and inform the surgical team of the client's request): meeting spiritual needs is part of holistic care, and coordinating with the chaplain and surgical team honors the request within the time available. Redirecting the client to family substitutes the nurse's judgment for the client's stated preference. The nurse may offer support, but replacing the requested chaplain does not meet the client's specific spiritual need. Delaying the visit until after surgery misses the time when the client wants support most.

Question 75ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Secure the restraints to the bed frame with quick-release ties and check circulation per policy): quick-release ties on the bed frame allow rapid removal in an emergency, and regular circulation and skin checks prevent injury. Side rails move when raised or lowered, so restraints attached to them can pull on the wrist and cause injury. Restraints should allow about two fingers beneath the strap to avoid impairing circulation and damaging the skin. Restraints are removed as soon as the client no longer meets the criteria, rather than kept on until the order expires.

Question 76CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("Report any unusual bleeding, such as black stools, nosebleeds or easy bruising."): clopidogrel is an antiplatelet drug that increases bleeding risk, especially combined with aspirin, so the client reports signs of bleeding promptly. Stopping clopidogrel early after a stent can cause stent thrombosis, so the client does not stop it without talking to the cardiologist. Doubling a dose increases bleeding risk, so a missed dose is taken when remembered unless the next dose is near. Ibuprofen adds to bleeding risk and gastrointestinal irritation when taken with antiplatelet drugs.

Question 77DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (The newborn is adjusting well to life outside the uterus at this time): Apgar scores of 7 to 10 are reassuring and show the newborn is transitioning well to extrauterine life. Moderate difficulty corresponds to scores of 4 to 6, which call for closer support than this newborn needs. Question 78BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Go to the room and assess the client's right heel personally): the RN remains accountable for delegated care and must personally assess any change that assistive personnel report, because assessment cannot be delegated. Massaging lotion into a reddened heel can damage tissue further, and treatment decisions require the RN's assessment first. Passing the finding to the next shift delays assessment of a possible pressure injury. Documentation and equipment decisions follow the RN's own assessment rather than replacing it.

Question 79AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Notify the provider immediately and keep the client on nothing by mouth): sudden severe pain with a rigid abdomen, tachycardia and fever after polyp removal suggests bowel perforation, a surgical emergency. Walking can ease gas pain after colonoscopy, but it does not treat perforation and delays urgent care. A laxative increases pressure in the bowel and could worsen leakage through a perforation. Giving fluids by mouth and delaying reassessment could worsen contamination and postpone surgery.

Question 80CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Wash the puncture site with soap and running water right away): washing with soap and water is the immediate first step, followed by prompt reporting so post-exposure evaluation can begin. Squeezing the wound is not recommended and has not been shown to reduce transmission. Reporting should occur immediately after washing, because some post-exposure treatments work best within hours. Caustic agents such as bleach damage tissue and are not recommended for wound care after a needlestick.

Question 81CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Have the client lie prone for periods during the day as tolerated): lying prone stretches the hip flexors and helps maintain the full hip extension needed for walking with a prosthesis. Prolonged elevation of the residual limb on a pillow keeps the hip flexed and promotes contracture after the first 24 to 48 hours. Long periods of sitting hold the hip in flexion and increase the risk of contracture. A roll under the knee encourages a knee flexion contracture, which also interferes with prosthesis fitting.

Question 82APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Ischemic stroke from a clot that forms in the atria and travels): atrial fibrillation allows blood to pool in the poorly contracting atria, where clots form and can travel to the brain, and hypertension and diabetes raise this risk further. A rapid heart rate does not typically cause hyperkalemia, which is linked mainly to kidney failure and certain medications. Stress can raise glucose modestly, but a hyperglycemic crisis is not the expected complication of this rhythm. Aortic dissection is associated with severe hypertension and connective tissue disorders rather than atrial fibrillation.

Question 83APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Hearing, balance and kidney function, including serum creatinine and urine output): aminoglycosides can cause ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity, so the nurse monitors hearing, balance, creatinine, urine output and drug levels. Changes in color vision are linked with drugs such as ethambutol rather than aminoglycosides. Aminoglycosides are cleared by the kidneys and are not a common cause of liver injury. Thyroid function is not affected by gentamicin and does not guide safe dosing.

Question 84BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Frequent gentle mouth care with ice chips or small sips of fluid as tolerated): frequent mouth care and small amounts of moisture relieve dryness and improve comfort for a client who breathes through the mouth. Salty foods draw moisture from the oral tissues and can worsen dryness and discomfort. Alcohol-based mouthwash dries and irritates the oral mucosa. Lemon-glycerin swabs can briefly stimulate saliva but dry the mouth further with repeated use and may irritate tissue.

Question 85DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Documenting the allergy and making sure it is visible to the team and dietary staff): documenting the allergy and flagging it for all team members and dietary staff provides consistent protection from exposure across shifts and departments. Verbal report to one nurse leaves other staff and later shifts unaware of the allergy. Meals from home shift responsibility to the family and do not protect against exposure from medications or other hospital sources. A door sign may help with visitors, but it does not alert dietary, pharmacy or other team members through the record.

Question 86ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Hold the dose and verify the order and tablet with the pharmacist): a change in appearance may reflect a different manufacturer or a dispensing error, so the nurse verifies before giving the medication. Assuming a generic substitution without checking dismisses a valid client concern and could allow an error to reach the client. Question 88CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (A platelet drop of more than half, new bruising and pink urine): a platelet fall of more than 50 percent together with new bruising and hematuria signals bleeding and a possible serious heparin complication. Constipation and poor sleep are common in hospitalized clients and need attention but are not urgent safety concerns. A heart rate of 96 and an oxygen saturation of 95 percent on low-flow oxygen are acceptable for a client recovering from a pulmonary embolism. Recognizing the combination of laboratory and physical bleeding signs allows the nurse to act before serious hemorrhage or clotting occurs.

Question 89BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Possible heparin-induced thrombocytopenia from an immune reaction to the heparin): a platelet fall of more than 50 percent occurring 5 to 10 days after heparin is started is the typical pattern of this immune-mediated reaction. A heparin infusion does not deliver enough volume to dilute the platelet count by more than half. Iron deficiency affects red blood cell production and does not explain a sharp drop in platelets. Recognizing the timing and size of the drop helps the nurse anticipate that the provider will evaluate for this complication urgently.

Question 90APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Risk for bleeding and new clots related to anticoagulation and possible heparin-induced thrombocytopenia): active bleeding from excess anticoagulation and the risk of new clots from heparin-induced thrombocytopenia are both potentially life-threatening. Constipation should be managed, but it is not an immediate threat compared with bleeding and clotting. Poor sleep affects comfort and recovery, yet it can be addressed after physiological threats are controlled. Paradoxically, this immune reaction raises the risk of new venous and arterial clots even as the platelet count falls.

Question 91APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Stop all heparin and begin a non-heparin anticoagulant such as argatroban): when heparin-induced thrombocytopenia is suspected, all heparin, including flushes, is stopped and a non-heparin anticoagulant such as argatroban is started to prevent new clots. Continuing heparin at any rate keeps exposing the client to the trigger of the immune reaction. Enoxaparin is a low-molecular-weight heparin that can cross-react with the same antibodies, so it is avoided. Stopping heparin without another anticoagulant would leave the client unprotected, because this reaction greatly increases clot risk.

Question 92BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Notify the provider at once and follow the protocol for suspected heparin-induced thrombocytopenia): the platelet fall and its timing suggest heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, and the high clotting time with bleeding also needs urgent action, so the nurse notifies the provider and follows protocol, which usually includes stopping all heparin. Continuing the current rate with active bleeding and a clotting time above target increases the risk of serious hemorrhage. Vitamin K reverses warfarin, not heparin, so it would not correct the effect of this infusion. Protamine sulfate is the reversal agent for heparin, and the provider decides whether it is needed based on the severity of bleeding.

Question 93CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Platelet count rising to 168,000/mm³ with clear yellow urine and no new bruising): a rising platelet count with no new bleeding shows that the immune reaction is resolving and the current anticoagulation is being tolerated safely. A further platelet fall with new calf swelling suggests ongoing heparin-induced thrombocytopenia with a new deep vein thrombosis. Black, tarry stools indicate gastrointestinal bleeding, which needs prompt reporting even if platelets are improving. The nurse continues to monitor platelets and bleeding signs closely during the transition to long-term anticoagulation.

Question 97AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Draw the blood cultures first, then give the antibiotic without delay): cultures drawn before antibiotics give more accurate results, and the antibiotic should follow promptly because early treatment improves survival in sepsis. Drawing cultures after the antibiotic infusion can sterilize the samples and lead to false-negative results. Drawing cultures from a line used for the antibiotic risks contamination and inaccurate results. Delaying cultures until the fever persists means any later samples are drawn after antibiotics and are less reliable.

Question 99BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Eat small, frequent meals and a few dry crackers before getting out of bed): small, frequent meals prevent an empty stomach, and dry crackers before rising often ease morning nausea. Large, high-fat meals empty slowly from the stomach and tend to worsen nausea. Delaying breakfast leaves the stomach empty for longer, which often increases nausea. Fluids are better taken between meals, because large volumes with food distend the stomach and can trigger vomiting.

Question 100CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Severe hypotension with profound weakness, vomiting and a low serum sodium): adrenal crisis causes severe hypotension, weakness, vomiting and hyponatremia from cortisol and aldosterone deficiency and can progress rapidly to shock. Hypertension with weight gain and edema suggests excess corticosteroid or fluid retention rather than adrenal insufficiency. Increased appetite and hyperglycemia are effects of excess cortisol, while adrenal crisis tends to cause low glucose. A moon face and buffalo hump are features of Cushing syndrome, the opposite of adrenal insufficiency.
