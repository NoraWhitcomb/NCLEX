Question 701CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Heavy protein in the urine with a low serum albumin level): damaged glomeruli leak large amounts of protein, so the urine shows heavy proteinuria and the serum albumin falls, which lowers oncotic pressure and causes edema. Gross hematuria is more typical of a nephritic process, and albumin is low rather than high in this condition. Nephrotic syndrome raises serum cholesterol as the liver makes more lipoproteins, and urine protein is markedly increased. Albumin is lost through the kidneys, so a high serum albumin and protein-free urine do not fit this diagnosis.

Question 702CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C ("Discard the 0800 void, then save all urine through the 0800 void tomorrow."): the first void is discarded because it was made before the test began, and every void afterward, including the final 0800 void, is saved to capture exactly 24 hours. Keeping the first morning void adds urine produced before the collection started and makes the result inaccurate. Sampling every 6 hours misses most of the urine produced, so the total volume and clearance would be falsely low. Collecting only overnight urine captures part of the day and cannot measure a full 24-hour excretion.

Question 703CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Pat the skin dry gently, paying close attention to each skin fold): patting rather than rubbing limits friction, and drying the folds thoroughly prevents moisture damage and fungal growth in areas where skin touches skin. Brisk rubbing creates friction and shear that can tear fragile older skin. Hot water dries the skin and can burn a client with reduced sensation, so warm water is used instead. Moisture trapped in skin folds promotes maceration and yeast infection rather than protecting the tissue.

Question 704DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (A serum potassium level of 6.4 mEq/L in the client with heart failure): this potassium level is well above the normal range of about 3.5 to 5.0 and can cause life-threatening dysrhythmias, especially with two potassium-raising medications. A sodium level of 139 falls within the normal range and needs no urgent report. A creatinine of 0.9 reflects normal kidney function after surgery. A hemoglobin of 13.5 is within the expected range for an adult female client.

Question 705DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (An EPA-registered sporicidal disinfectant, such as a bleach-based cleaning product): Clostridioides difficile forms spores that survive on surfaces, and a sporicidal agent such as a bleach-based product is needed to destroy them. Alcohol does not kill these spores, so alcohol-based cleaners leave the environment contaminated. Soap and water can physically remove some organisms but does not reliably kill spores on room surfaces. Most quaternary ammonium products are not sporicidal, and an air freshener only masks odor without reducing contamination.

Question 706DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Provide frequent oral care and apply water-soluble lubricant to the nostril): frequent mouth care relieves dryness in a client who cannot drink, and lubricating and inspecting the naris reduces irritation and prevents pressure injury. Large amounts of ice chips add fluid that is suctioned out with electrolytes, so they are limited to what the provider allows. Taping the tube tightly against the nostril creates pressure that can cause skin breakdown, so it is secured without pulling on the naris. Removing the tube requires a provider order and would interrupt decompression of the obstructed bowel.

Question 707CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (70 mg, matching one milligram for every kilogram of body weight): multiplying 1 mg by 70 kg gives exactly 70 mg for each dose given every 12 hours. A 7 mg dose results from misplacing the decimal and would leave the client undertreated. A 140 mg dose equals the total daily amount, giving twice the ordered dose at one time and raising bleeding risk. A 35 mg dose divides the ordered amount in half and would not provide adequate anticoagulation.

Question 708CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Dependent leg edema, jugular vein distension and an enlarged, tender liver): a failing right ventricle causes blood to back up into the systemic veins, producing peripheral edema, neck vein distension and liver congestion. Crackles, frothy sputum and orthopnea reflect pulmonary congestion from left-sided heart failure. Wheezing alone reflects the airway disease and does not show the systemic congestion of right-sided failure. Low glucose, clammy skin and tremors describe hypoglycemia and are not signs of heart failure.

Question 709DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Stop all oral intake and keep the client sitting fully upright): coughing and a wet voice after drinking are signs of aspiration, so stopping intake and keeping the client upright protects the airway until a swallowing evaluation is done. Changing liquid consistency is a diet change that should follow a formal swallowing evaluation rather than precede it. Continuing the meal at a slower pace still exposes the client to further aspiration. Straws can increase the speed and volume of liquid entering the mouth and raise aspiration risk after a stroke.

Question 710BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Slow the infusion, raise the head of the bed and notify the provider): crackles, dyspnea and distended neck veins indicate fluid volume overload, so slowing the infusion to a keep-open rate, positioning upright and notifying the provider are priorities. Raising the rate would add more volume to an already overloaded circulation and worsen pulmonary congestion. Lying flat with legs raised increases venous return to the heart and makes breathing harder. More oral fluid adds to the overload, and coughing does not remove fluid from the alveoli.

Question 711BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B ("I gave the wrong dose, assessed the client and reported the error right away."): accountability means owning one's actions, checking the client for harm and reporting the error promptly so it can be corrected and prevented. Shifting blame to the pharmacy avoids responsibility for the nurse's own role in checking the medication. Quietly correcting an error without reporting it hides the event and prevents system improvement. Relying on the client's word instead of verifying the allergy band reflects an unsafe shortcut rather than accountable practice.

Question 712BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Denial, refusing to acknowledge a problem that is obvious to others): the client refuses to accept clear evidence of an alcohol problem, which is denial and is common in substance use disorders. Compensation involves stressing a strength to offset a weakness, which the client is not describing. Sublimation redirects impulses into acceptable outlets, such as sports, and does not match this statement. Regression involves childlike behavior under stress, which is not reflected in the client's words.

Question 713AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Suction, supplemental oxygen, emergency airway equipment and reversal agents): moderate sedation can depress breathing, so suction, oxygen, airway equipment and reversal agents such as naloxone and flumazenil must be ready. Toileting supplies may be useful later but do not address the main risk of respiratory depression. Warming and comfort items support the client but are not required safety equipment for sedation. Transport equipment is needed only after recovery and does not protect the airway during sedation.

Question 714CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (It works best when started within 48 hours after symptoms first begin): influenza antivirals shorten illness most when begun early, ideally within 48 hours of symptom onset, although later treatment is still advised for high-risk or hospitalized clients. Waiting a week allows viral replication to peak and greatly reduces the benefit for an otherwise healthy adult. The benefit declines as time passes, so the medicine is not equally effective at any point. Delaying treatment until the fever resolves misses the window when the drug offers the most benefit.

Question 715CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C ("Progestin-only methods or a copper IUD are usually compatible with breastfeeding."): progestin-only methods and the copper intrauterine device do not reduce milk supply and are considered safe during breastfeeding. Estrogen-containing methods can decrease milk supply and raise clot risk in the early postpartum weeks, so they are not preferred. Breastfeeding suppresses ovulation only under strict conditions and is not reliable protection for every client. Douching does not prevent pregnancy and can disrupt vaginal flora.

Question 716DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("Take your medication every day exactly as prescribed to prevent drug resistance."): consistent daily adherence keeps the virus suppressed and prevents the development of drug-resistant strains. An undetectable viral load means the therapy is working, and stopping it allows the virus to rebound. Taking doses only when symptomatic leads to gaps in drug levels and promotes resistance. Doubling up after missed doses is not standard advice and can increase adverse effects without restoring control.

Question 717CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Report the suspected exploitation according to agency policy and state law): suspected financial exploitation of a vulnerable adult must be reported through proper channels, such as the agency and adult protective services. Hiding the wallet may limit losses but leaves the possible abuse unaddressed and unreported. Confronting the aide personally can escalate the situation and interfere with a proper investigation. Delaying a report allows possible exploitation to continue and does not meet mandated reporting duties.

Question 718DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Cleanse gently after each episode and apply a moisture-barrier product): prompt gentle cleansing removes irritants, and a barrier cream or ointment shields the skin from urine and stool to prevent incontinence-associated dermatitis. Plastic-backed briefs trap heat and moisture against the skin and worsen breakdown. Vigorous scrubbing with harsh soap strips protective oils and damages already irritated skin. Waiting eight hours between changes leaves the skin exposed to urine and stool for long periods.

Question 719DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Prolonged vomiting or continuous nasogastric suction removing gastric acid): loss of hydrochloric acid from the stomach leaves excess bicarbonate in the blood, producing metabolic alkalosis with a high pH and bicarbonate. Ketoacidosis adds acids to the blood and causes metabolic acidosis rather than alkalosis. Diarrhea removes bicarbonate-rich intestinal fluid, which leads to metabolic acidosis. Failing kidneys cannot excrete acids or regenerate bicarbonate, so kidney failure causes metabolic acidosis.

Question 720DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Assess the client for injury before moving the client from the floor): the nurse first checks the head, spine, extremities and level of consciousness, which is especially important because anticoagulation raises the risk of bleeding. An incident report is completed after the client is assessed and safe. Moving the client before assessment could worsen a fracture or spinal injury. The family is notified after the client has been assessed and the provider informed.

Question 721CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Stop the infusion, call for help and prepare intramuscular epinephrine per protocol): stridor and hives after a new drug indicate anaphylaxis with airway swelling, so stopping the drug, calling for help and giving epinephrine are first priorities. Oral fluids risk aspiration with a swelling airway, and lying completely flat can worsen breathing in a client with stridor. An oral antihistamine acts too slowly and does not reverse airway edema or hypotension. Documentation and allergy updates are important but come after the emergency is treated.

Question 722AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Use broad-spectrum sunscreen every day and stay away from indoor tanning beds): broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher, protective clothing and avoiding tanning beds reduce ultraviolet exposure, the main modifiable risk for skin cancer. A base tan offers minimal protection and indoor tanning itself raises melanoma risk. Ultraviolet exposure occurs on sunny and cloudy days, so sunscreen is needed daily. Low-SPF products provide too little protection, and the body's vitamin needs can be met through diet or supplements.

Question 723BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Personal fable, the belief that one's feelings and experiences are unique): adolescent egocentrism includes the personal fable, in which teens believe no one else has felt what they feel. Separation anxiety peaks in infants and toddlers and involves fear of leaving caregivers. Concrete operational thinking belongs to school-age children rather than to this adolescent belief. Object permanence develops during infancy and does not explain this statement.

Question 724BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B ("Wash gently with mild soap and use only lotions your care team approves."): irradiated skin is fragile, so gentle cleansing and avoiding unapproved products reduce irritation and breakdown. Perfumed products can irritate the skin, and some lotions applied before treatment can increase the radiation dose to the skin. Heat can injure skin that is already damaged and is avoided over the treatment field. Scrubbing damages fragile skin, and the markings must stay in place to guide treatment.

Question 725DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("I will report unusual bleeding and keep taking it unless my provider says to stop."): bleeding is the main risk of apixaban, and stopping it suddenly raises the risk of stroke from clots. Direct oral anticoagulants do not require routine INR monitoring, unlike warfarin. A missed dose is taken as soon as remembered the same day, and doubling the next dose increases bleeding risk. Vitamin K intake affects warfarin, so restricting green vegetables is unnecessary with apixaban.

Question 726AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A ("Have you been trained to do this, and do you know which findings to report?"): confirming competence and clear reporting expectations addresses the right person, right direction and right supervision before the task is delegated. Having the assistant pass the task to someone else removes the RN's ability to match the task to a competent person. Checking workload is reasonable, but time alone does not show the assistant can perform the task safely. Relying on memory gives no specific direction about timing, parameters or what to report.

Question 727DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (A family with a toddler in a house built in 1950 with peeling paint): homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint, and peeling paint and dust are the major source of lead exposure for young children. Housing built recently does not contain lead-based paint, so the risk for this toddler is low. Lead exposure is most harmful to young children, so a couple without children is not the priority for this teaching. Bottled water and newer housing both reduce the main sources of lead exposure for this family.

Question 734ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Sweep the nozzle from side to side at the base of the fire): the PASS steps are pull the pin, aim at the base, squeeze the handle and sweep side to side, so sweeping is the last step. Squeezing the handle is the third step, coming just before the sweeping motion. Aiming at the top of the flames misses the fuel source, which is why the extinguisher is directed at the base. Smothering with a blanket is not part of the PASS method for using an extinguisher.

Question 745BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Question 747DPhysiological Adaptation

Question 757CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Hyperkalemia, because both drugs reduce the kidneys' excretion of potassium): spironolactone is a potassium-sparing aldosterone antagonist, and lisinopril also lowers aldosterone, so together they can raise potassium to dangerous levels. Discharge planning matters but is not appropriate while the client is hemodynamically unstable. Vaccination is important prevention but is deferred until the acute, life-threatening problem is controlled. Addressing circulation and infection first follows the airway, breathing and circulation framework for setting priorities.

Question 761BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Blood cultures, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics and a 30 mL/kg crystalloid fluid bolus): sepsis guidelines call for cultures drawn without delaying therapy, antibiotics within the first hour and rapid crystalloid resuscitation for hypotension or a lactate of 4 mmol/L or higher. For this 80 kg client, an output of 45 mL/h exceeds the target of 40 mL/h, confirming adequate kidney perfusion.

The correct answer is A (The total is 14, which places the client at risk for pressure injury): the subscale scores add up to 3 plus 2 plus 2 plus 2 plus 3 plus 2, which equals 14, and a score of 18 or less indicates risk. A total of 16 is a miscalculation, and even 16 would still fall within the at-risk range. Lower Braden scores mean higher risk, so 14 is below the cutoff and calls for a prevention plan rather than routine care. A total of 19 is a miscalculation, and a score of 19 would actually fall above the usual risk threshold.

Question 765DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (A 79-year-old client with osteoporosis who takes warfarin for atrial fibrillation): anticoagulation raises the risk of serious bleeding such as intracranial hemorrhage, and osteoporosis makes fractures far more likely after a fall. Metformin alone rarely causes hypoglycemia, and this client has no added bleeding or fracture risk. A young adult using albuterol as needed has no factor that makes injury from a fall more severe. Question 766DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Program the pump at 125 mL/h, delivering the full liter in 8 hours): dividing 1,000 mL by 8 hours gives exactly 125 mL/h, the rate that delivers the ordered volume on time. Question 767BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Neurogenic shock from loss of sympathetic tone below the level of injury): injury above the sixth thoracic level interrupts sympathetic outflow, causing vasodilation, hypotension and bradycardia with warm, dry skin. Hypovolemic shock usually produces a rapid heart rate and cool, clammy skin, not bradycardia. Autonomic dysreflexia causes severe hypertension rather than hypotension and typically appears after spinal shock resolves. Septic shock is unlikely within 3 hours of injury and is usually accompanied by tachycardia and fever.

Question 768BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B ("I can see you're upset and hurting; I'll get your pain medication right now."): acknowledging the client's feelings and acting promptly on the unmet need for pain relief de-escalates anger and addresses its cause. Asking the client to lower the voice focuses on controlling behavior and can feel dismissive while the pain remains untreated. Explaining that other clients are sicker sounds defensive and minimizes the client's pain and frustration. Question 769CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (A client receiving chemotherapy with an absolute neutrophil count of 400 cells/mm³): an absolute neutrophil count below 500 cells/mm³ is severe neutropenia, placing the client at high risk for life-threatening infection. A white blood cell count of 6,800/mm³ is within the normal range and does not require protective precautions. An absolute neutrophil count of 2,100/mm³ is normal, so this client in remission does not need neutropenic precautions. A platelet count of 90,000/mm³ calls for bleeding precautions rather than protection from infection.

Question 770BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (0.9% sodium chloride, which has an osmolality similar to plasma): normal saline is isotonic, so it stays in the extracellular space and effectively expands circulating volume. Ten percent dextrose in water is hypertonic and used mainly to supply glucose, not for volume resuscitation.

Question 771ABasic Care and Comfort

Question 772BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Soft cheeses such as queso fresco made from unpasteurized milk): unpasteurized soft cheeses can carry Listeria, which can cause miscarriage, stillbirth or serious newborn infection. Pasteurized hard cheeses such as cheddar are considered safe during pregnancy. Canned light tuna is a low-mercury fish, and two to three servings a week are recommended in pregnancy. Question 773CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Question 774APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Question 775DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

Question 776AManagement of Care

Question 777AReduction of Risk Potential

Question 778BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Place the baby on the back on a firm, flat crib mattress): back sleeping on a firm, flat surface without soft bedding is the most effective way to lower the risk of sudden infant death. Question 779AReduction of Risk Potential

Question 780DReduction of Risk Potential

Question 781DPhysiological Adaptation

Question 782ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

Question 785DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Functioning suction equipment and oxygen ready to use after a seizure): suction clears secretions and oxygen supports breathing during the postictal period, helping maintain a patent airway. Question 787AReduction of Risk Potential

Question 789APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Withdrawal symptoms often begin within 6 to 48 hours after the last drink): hospitalization abruptly stops drinking, so tremor and sweating begin within hours, hallucinations often appear within 12 to 48 hours and severe withdrawal can follow. Alcohol withdrawal begins within hours to days, not weeks, after the last drink. Anesthesia effects resolve within hours and do not explain tremor, tachycardia and hallucinations two days later. Understanding this timeline helps the nurse anticipate worsening withdrawal over the next few days.

Question 790CPsychosocial Integrity

Constipation is a common postoperative problem but is not life-threatening in this situation. Insomnia and irritability are real concerns, but they are far less dangerous than seizures or delirium tremens. Delirium tremens typically develops 48 to 96 hours after the last drink, so this client is in the window of greatest risk.

Question 791CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Benzodiazepines such as lorazepam given according to CIWA-Ar scores): benzodiazepines are first-line therapy because they calm the hyperactive nervous system and prevent seizures, and symptom-triggered dosing uses CIWA-Ar scores to guide each dose.
