Question 301BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Nausea often eases over weeks; eat smaller meals and report severe abdominal pain."): nausea is a common early effect of GLP-1 receptor agonists that usually lessens with time, smaller meals help, and severe persistent abdominal pain may signal pancreatitis. Changing the dosing schedule on the client's own alters a weekly regimen and should be directed only by the prescriber. Lying down soon after eating can worsen reflux and fullness rather than relieve nausea. Mild nausea is an expected side effect, not an allergic reaction, so holding doses would interrupt therapy unnecessarily.

Question 302AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Increasing confusion and seizure activity related to swelling of brain cells): a sodium of 126 mEq/L draws water into brain cells, and the resulting cerebral edema can cause confusion, lethargy and seizures. Peaked T waves reflect hyperkalemia and are not a direct result of low serum sodium. A positive Trousseau sign with muscle cramps points to hypocalcemia, which is a separate electrolyte problem. Intense thirst with dry sticky membranes suggests hypernatremia, the opposite of this client's imbalance.

Question 303AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (The posterior iliac crest, with the client positioned on the side or abdomen): the posterior iliac crest provides an accessible, safe source of marrow for both aspiration and core biopsy in adults, with the client lying prone or on one side. The sternum may be used for aspiration alone, but it is not used for core biopsy because of the risk of puncturing underlying structures. The proximal tibia is a site used mainly in infants and young children, not in adults. The distal femur is not a standard site for bone marrow sampling in adults.

Question 304AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Pain, warmth and swelling in the left calf that started two days ago): estrogen-containing contraceptives raise the risk of venous thromboembolism, especially in smokers, and one-sided calf pain with swelling suggests deep vein thrombosis. Breakthrough spotting is common in the early months of use and is not an emergency. Breast tenderness is an expected hormonal effect that usually lessens over time. Nausea is often reduced by taking the pill with food or at bedtime and does not need urgent evaluation.

Question 305BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Reassess the pain fully and notify the provider that relief has been inadequate): the nurse evaluates the response at peak effect, and persistent severe pain calls for a full reassessment and a call to the provider to adjust the plan. Asking the client to endure severe pain until the next scheduled dose leaves the pain untreated and delays a needed change in therapy. Giving an additional opioid dose requires a prescription, and administering it without one is outside the nurse's scope. Telling the client that discomfort is expected dismisses a severe pain report that needs reassessment and action.

Question 306DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("Avoid alcohol, and do not drive until you know how this medicine affects you."): benzodiazepines cause drowsiness and impaired coordination, and combining them with alcohol increases central nervous system and respiratory depression. After regular use, lorazepam should be tapered under provider guidance because abrupt stopping can cause withdrawal and seizures. Lorazepam is a sedative, so the client should expect drowsiness rather than increased alertness. Taking extra tablets beyond the prescribed dose raises the risk of oversedation and dependence.

Question 307BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (The rising creatinine suggests possible nephrotoxicity, and the provider should be notified): gentamicin is toxic to the renal tubules, and a doubling of creatinine with falling urine output signals kidney injury that may require a dose adjustment or a different drug. A doubling of serum creatinine is a significant change, not an expected fluctuation. Creatinine reflects kidney function rather than liver function, and liver injury is not a typical aminoglycoside effect. Improving kidney function would lower the creatinine, not raise it.

Question 308BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Check for dizziness and blood pressure changes as the client sits at the bedside): bed rest, opioids and blood loss increase the risk of orthostatic hypotension, so the nurse has the client sit first and checks for dizziness and a blood pressure drop to prevent a fall. Measuring leg circumference helps screen for swelling but does not address the immediate fall risk of standing. Bowel function is important after surgery but does not determine whether it is safe to stand right now. Dressing assessment is part of routine care but does not predict fainting or falls during first ambulation.

Question 309AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Documenting the 0900 medications as given before entering the client's room): medications are documented only after they are given, because charting in advance creates an inaccurate record if a dose is refused, held or delayed. Using two identifiers before every medication is required safety practice. SBAR is a structured, appropriate way to communicate a change in condition to the provider. Hand hygiene on entry and exit is standard infection prevention and needs no correction.

Question 310CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (March 17, 2027, roughly forty weeks from the start of the last period): Naegele's rule adds 7 days to the first day of the last period, subtracts 3 months and adds 1 year, so June 10, 2026, becomes March 17, 2027. April 17, 2027, results from subtracting only 2 months, and the rule does not add time for a first pregnancy. September 17, 2026, adds 3 months instead of subtracting them and fails to advance the year. March 3, 2027, subtracts 7 days instead of adding them to the first day of the last period.

Question 311BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Notify the provider of the weight gain and new crackles for further orders): a gain of 4 pounds in 2 days with new crackles signals fluid overload and worsening heart failure, which usually requires a change in diuretic therapy. Extra fluids would add to the fluid volume and worsen pulmonary congestion. Waiting a week to reweigh delays treatment of a rapid gain that needs action now. Lying flat increases venous return and breathlessness, so the head of the bed should stay elevated.

Question 312APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (The CIWA-Ar, which scores symptoms such as tremor, sweating and agitation): the revised Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment for Alcohol rates withdrawal signs and symptoms, and its score guides symptom-triggered benzodiazepine dosing. The CAGE questionnaire screens for alcohol use disorder but does not measure the severity of active withdrawal. The Glasgow Coma Scale measures level of consciousness and is not designed to track withdrawal symptoms. The Braden Scale predicts pressure injury risk and has no role in grading alcohol withdrawal.

Question 313DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Assess contractions and fetal status for preterm labor and notify the provider promptly): regular contractions with back pain and increased discharge before 37 weeks suggest preterm labor, which needs prompt evaluation so treatment such as antenatal corticosteroids can be considered. Braxton Hicks contractions are irregular and ease with rest and hydration, so labeling regular contractions this way misses possible labor. Sending the client home before evaluation delays assessment of cervical change and fetal well-being. A visit next week is far too late for contractions occurring every 5 minutes.

Question 314DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Get adequate calcium and vitamin D daily and do regular weight-bearing exercise): adequate calcium with enough of the vitamin that aids its absorption, combined with weight-bearing and resistance exercise, helps maintain bone density after menopause. Swimming benefits the heart but is not weight-bearing, so it does little to stimulate bone formation. High caffeine intake increases urinary calcium loss rather than improving absorption. Smoking accelerates bone loss, so cessation should begin now rather than after a diagnosis is made.

Question 315DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Use the albuterol first, then wait about five minutes before the fluticasone): the bronchodilator opens the airways first so the inhaled corticosteroid can reach deeper into the lungs. Inhaling the corticosteroid first deposits more of it in narrowed airways, reducing its effect. The sequence matters because bronchodilation improves delivery of the second medication. These two drugs are commonly used together and do not need to be separated by several hours.

Question 316APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A ("Report chest pain or sudden shortness of breath to the nurse right away."): sudden chest pain or shortness of breath may signal a pulmonary embolism from a dislodged clot, which needs emergency treatment. Massaging the affected leg could dislodge the clot and should be avoided. Activity follows the provider's plan, and unsupervised walking on heparin also increases fall and bleeding risk. The length of anticoagulation is based on the treatment plan, not on how the leg feels.

Question 317DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Cardiogenic shock caused by failure of the damaged left ventricle to pump): pump failure after a large infarction produces low blood pressure and poor perfusion along with backward congestion seen as crackles and distended neck veins. Hypovolemic shock causes flat neck veins and clear lungs, and large fluid boluses here could worsen pulmonary congestion. Anaphylaxis typically presents with hives, wheezing and swelling, which this client does not have. Neurogenic shock follows spinal cord injury and produces warm, dry skin with bradycardia rather than cool skin and tachycardia.

Question 318BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Confirming identity using the client's room number and the name on the door): a room number is not an acceptable identifier because clients change rooms, as happened overnight on this unit, and identity must be confirmed with two personal identifiers. Checking allergies before a new antibiotic is correct safety practice. Having the client state name and date of birth uses two acceptable identifiers. Comparing the wristband with the medication record at the bedside verifies the right client.

Question 319CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Giving insulin coverage as ordered and continuing regular blood glucose monitoring): parenteral nutrition contains concentrated dextrose, hyperglycemia is common, and insulin coverage with frequent glucose checks is the usual response. Dextrose would raise an already elevated glucose and worsen the hyperglycemia. Abruptly stopping the infusion can cause rebound hypoglycemia, so rate changes are made gradually by order. A glucose of 245 mg/dL needs treatment and close monitoring, not a recheck a week later.

Question 320APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Set the pump at 6 mL/h, because the concentration is 1 unit/mL): the bag contains 100 units in 100 mL, which is 1 unit/mL, so 6 units per hour requires 6 mL/h. A rate of 0.6 mL/h assumes 10 units per milliliter and would deliver only 0.6 units per hour. A rate of 60 mL/h would deliver 60 units per hour, a tenfold overdose. A rate of 1 mL/h confuses the concentration with the rate and delivers only 1 unit per hour.

Question 321ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Head of the bed raised to at least 30 to 45 degrees): elevating the head of the bed reduces reflux of formula and lowers the risk of aspiration pneumonia during continuous feeding. Lying flat on the back allows gastric contents to reflux into the airway. A head-down position greatly increases the risk of regurgitation and aspiration. Turning to the side does not protect the airway when the head of the bed remains flat.

Question 322ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Lock the commode wheels and keep the call light within the client's reach): locked wheels keep the commode from rolling, and an accessible call light lets the client summon help before standing. Closing the door fully means staff cannot see or hear a client at high risk of falling. Placing the commode far from the bed increases the walking distance and fall risk at night. Removing the call light prevents the client from calling for help when ready to return to bed.

Question 323CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (An alert adult after bowel surgery who demonstrates correct use of the button): patient-controlled analgesia is safe only when the client is alert enough to understand the device and press the button when pain occurs. A client with dementia and confusion cannot reliably use the device as intended. Family members should not press the button, because doing so bypasses the safety feature in which sedation stops further doses. A heavily sedated client is already at risk for respiratory depression and cannot self-administer doses safely.

Question 324BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Inject it into your outer thigh at the first signs, then call 911."): epinephrine is given into the outer thigh at the first signs of anaphylaxis, and emergency care is still needed because symptoms can return. Antihistamines act too slowly to treat anaphylaxis, and delaying epinephrine increases the risk of death. Waiting for the throat to close allows a severe reaction to progress before treatment begins. The thigh is the recommended site, and the client needs emergency evaluation rather than resting at home.

Question 325BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Brush and floss gently every day and see your dentist regularly."): phenytoin can cause gingival overgrowth, and thorough daily oral hygiene with regular dental visits reduces its severity. Stopping brushing allows plaque to build up, which worsens gum inflammation and bleeding. Mouthwash alone does not remove plaque, and alcohol-based products can irritate inflamed gums. Gingival overgrowth usually persists while the drug continues, so it needs ongoing care rather than waiting.

Question 326DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Blood glucose of 105 mg/dL and the client is alert and oriented): a glucose above 70 mg/dL with a return to normal mental status shows the hypoglycemia has resolved, and the client then eats a snack or meal. A glucose of 62 mg/dL is still below 70 mg/dL, so the 15-gram treatment should be repeated. Tachycardia and shakiness are ongoing adrenergic signs of low blood glucose. Persistent confusion means the brain is still not receiving enough glucose.

Question 327DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D ("We keep the gun unloaded in a lockbox and lock the ammunition separately."): storing the firearm unloaded and locked, with ammunition locked in a separate place, is the safest way to reduce injury risk to children. A loaded gun in an unlocked nightstand is easily reached by a curious child. Teaching children not to touch guns does not reliably prevent them from handling one they find. Hidden firearms are often found by children, so hiding is not a substitute for locked storage.

Question 328BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (The 60-year-old man with crushing chest pressure, pallor and sweating): crushing chest pressure with pallor and sweating may represent an acute myocardial infarction, a time-sensitive threat to life. The hand laceration is controlled with pressure and the woman's vital signs are normal, so the injury can wait safely. An ankle injury with strong pulses and the ability to walk is stable and less urgent than possible cardiac ischemia. Question 329CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Her bleeding is controlled and her vital signs are stable, so she can wait): triage ranks clients by acuity, and controlled bleeding with normal vital signs is far less urgent than possible myocardial infarction. Hand lacerations can involve tendons or nerves and still need evaluation and repair, so dismissing the injury is inaccurate. Age alone does not determine triage priority, because physiologic stability and threat to life do. Arrival order matters only among clients with similar acuity, which is not the situation here.

Question 330AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (A 12-lead electrocardiogram to look for ST-segment changes of ischemia): guidelines call for a 12-lead electrocardiogram within 10 minutes of arrival for suspected acute coronary syndrome so that an ST-elevation infarction can be identified and treated quickly. A chest computed tomography scan may be considered later but would delay identification of a heart attack. A chest radiograph can help rule out other causes but does not diagnose myocardial infarction. The electrocardiogram determines whether emergency reperfusion is needed, which makes it the first priority.

Question 331AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The 22-year-old woman with a sore throat and fever who is drinking fluids): a stable young adult with a sore throat and fever, no airway compromise and normal swallowing has a minor illness suited to fast-track care. A newly arrived client with chest pain needs full emergency evaluation, including a rapid electrocardiogram, in a monitored area. A client recovering after cardiac catheterization requires cardiac monitoring and access-site checks that a fast-track bay cannot provide. Fast-track areas improve flow by moving low-acuity clients away from beds needed for higher-acuity care.

Question 332CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Reassess circulation, loosen the splint as needed and raise his triage priority): new numbness, coolness and pallor suggest compromised circulation from swelling or a tight splint, which needs prompt neurovascular reassessment and escalation. Additional ice can further reduce blood flow and does not address possible vascular compromise. Documenting the change without escalating care leaves a potentially limb-threatening problem untreated. Triage is ongoing, and a change in condition requires moving the client up in priority and notifying the provider.

Question 333BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Clients were seen by acuity and reassessed promptly when their condition changed): effective triage matches the order of care to clinical urgency and includes ongoing reassessment, as shown when the man with the ankle injury was moved up after his circulation changed. Seeing clients strictly by arrival time could delay care for life-threatening conditions such as chest pain. An acuity level assigned once without reassessment misses deterioration in waiting clients. Ongoing reassessment allowed the department to respond safely to changes in the waiting room.

Question 334DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (The client with a new tracheostomy who has noisy, gurgling respirations): gurgling respirations in a client with a new tracheostomy suggest secretions obstructing the airway, which can quickly become life-threatening and needs suctioning. A discharge question is a nonurgent need that can be addressed after the airway is secured. Help with meal containers is a comfort need that can be delegated to assistive personnel. A scheduled dressing change is routine care that can safely follow the airway assessment.

Question 335DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Verify client and product with a second qualified person and use a blood set): two-person bedside verification prevents transfusion errors, and platelets are infused through an appropriate blood administration set with normal saline. Platelets are stored at room temperature with gentle agitation, and refrigeration reduces their function. Baseline vital signs are needed before starting so that a transfusion reaction can be recognized. Dextrose solutions are not used with blood products because they can cause clumping or hemolysis.

Question 336BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Malignant hyperthermia, a hypermetabolic reaction to certain anesthetic agents): rigidity, tachycardia, rising end-tidal carbon dioxide and a rapidly climbing temperature after volatile anesthetics or succinylcholine signal malignant hyperthermia, which is treated with dantrolene and cooling per protocol. Postoperative inflammatory fever is usually low grade and does not cause muscle rigidity. Sepsis can cause fever and tachycardia but does not produce sudden muscle rigidity within an hour of anesthesia. Dehydration may raise the heart rate but does not cause rigidity or a temperature of 104°F.

Question 337BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Arrange for a qualified medical interpreter to be present during the consent discussion): a qualified medical interpreter ensures accurate communication of risks, benefits and alternatives and protects the client's confidentiality. Family members, especially minors, should not interpret because they may omit or change information and are placed in an inappropriate role. A form in a language the client cannot read well does not ensure informed understanding. Nodding does not confirm comprehension, and simplifying English does not overcome a language barrier.

Question 338BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (A client 2 days after hysterectomy who is ambulating and tolerating a regular diet): a float nurse should receive a stable client whose care resembles the nurse's usual practice, and postoperative gynecologic care closely matches postpartum skills. A newly placed chest tube needs a nurse experienced in assessing drainage systems and respiratory changes. An insulin infusion for ketoacidosis requires frequent titration and electrolyte monitoring by an experienced nurse. A titrated diltiazem infusion for new atrial fibrillation needs close cardiac monitoring by a nurse familiar with this care.

Question 339CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Lifting a heavy client alone while bending forward at the waist): lifting heavy loads alone while bending at the waist strains the lower back and is a leading cause of back injuries in nursing. Working with a coworker distributes the load and reduces injury risk. Mechanical lifts are the recommended method for moving clients who cannot bear weight. Adjusting the bed to waist height lets the nurse work with a straight back and reduces strain.

Question 340BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Treat the hypoglycemia per protocol, recheck the glucose and inform the stroke team): hypoglycemia can mimic stroke and must be corrected first, typically with IV dextrose per protocol, and the result is shared with the stroke team. Oral fluids are unsafe before a swallow screen because the client may aspirate. Thrombolytic therapy is not considered until the low glucose is corrected and its possible role in the deficits is excluded. Delaying glucose treatment for a full neurologic examination prolongs brain injury from low glucose.

Question 341APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Screening for latent tuberculosis with a skin test or blood assay): tumor necrosis factor inhibitors such as adalimumab weaken defenses against infection and can reactivate latent tuberculosis, so screening is required before starting. Cataract screening is associated with long-term corticosteroid use, not with starting adalimumab. Baseline hearing tests are needed for ototoxic drugs such as aminoglycosides rather than for this biologic. Kidney stones are not a recognized risk of adalimumab, so a renal ultrasound is not part of pretreatment screening.

Question 342DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Digoxin toxicity, possibly worsened by low potassium from the diuretic): nausea, anorexia, visual changes and bradycardia are classic signs of digoxin toxicity, and diuretic-induced hypokalemia raises the risk, so the nurse holds the dose and notifies the provider. Low blood glucose causes shakiness, sweating and confusion rather than yellow visual halos. Allergic reactions present with rash, itching or swelling, not nausea with visual changes. These findings signal toxicity that needs evaluation, not effects that will resolve without intervention.

Question 343CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Regular insulin, a short-acting insulin that can be given intravenously): regular insulin is the standard insulin used for continuous intravenous infusions in diabetic ketoacidosis. NPH is a suspension that is given only subcutaneously and must not be infused intravenously. Insulin glargine is a basal insulin given subcutaneously and is not used for intravenous infusion. Insulin detemir is also a subcutaneous basal insulin and is not appropriate for an infusion.

Question 344DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("Tell your provider about mood changes such as depression or irritability."): levetiracetam can cause behavioral and mood changes, including irritability, depression and suicidal thoughts, which should be reported promptly. Doubling a missed dose raises the risk of side effects such as sedation and dizziness. Antiseizure drugs are not stopped suddenly because abrupt withdrawal can trigger seizures. Levetiracetam controls seizures but does not cure epilepsy, and treatment usually continues long term.

Question 345CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (A client with active pulmonary tuberculosis who has a productive cough and night sweats): active pulmonary tuberculosis spreads by airborne particles and requires an airborne infection isolation room with negative pressure and N95 respirator use. A client after appendectomy has no transmissible infection, so a private room is a preference rather than a need. A wrist fracture poses no infection risk to a roommate. Hypertension is not contagious, so this client can safely share a room.

Question 346BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Decreased or absent breath sounds over the right side of the chest): air in the pleural space keeps the lung from expanding, so breath sounds are diminished or absent on the affected side. Air outside the lung muffles sound transmission, so breath sounds are reduced rather than increased. Dullness to percussion suggests fluid or consolidation, whereas a pneumothorax produces hyperresonance. Diffuse crackles point to fluid in the alveoli, as in pulmonary edema, rather than a collapsed lung.

Question 347CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Program the pump at 100 mL/h to infuse 50 mL in 30 minutes): 50 mL divided by 0.5 hour equals 100 mL/h, which delivers the full bag in 30 minutes. A rate of 50 mL/h would take a full hour to infuse the bag, twice the ordered time. A rate of 25 mL/h divides the volume by 2 instead of by the time and would take 2 hours. A rate of 200 mL/h would finish the bag in 15 minutes, infusing it too quickly.

Question 348DManagement of Care

A sore throat with low-grade fever can be evaluated in the clinic or by appointment. A localized itchy rash without breathing difficulty or facial swelling is not an emergency. An ankle sprain that allows weight bearing can be managed with rest, ice and a clinic visit if needed.

Question 349BReduction of Risk Potential

Active bowel sounds with a soft abdomen show normal gastrointestinal function. A stable weight early in feeding is expected and does not indicate intolerance. Question 350CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

Localized itching that resolves with washing is a minor reaction that does not need emergency care. Faint redness that fades quickly is a mild local response without airway involvement. Dry, cracked skin reflects irritant contact dermatitis, which is not life-threatening.

Question 351CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

A wound colonized with VRE spreads by contact, so the wound is covered and staff use gown and gloves, but a mask for the client is not needed. A urinary tract infection is managed with standard precautions and poses no respiratory transmission risk during transport. A client recovering from fracture repair has no transmissible respiratory illness and does not need a mask.

Question 352DBasic Care and Comfort

Question 353CManagement of Care

Question 354DBasic Care and Comfort

Cream soup contains dairy and is part of a full liquid diet. Milk of any fat content is opaque and is a full liquid rather than a clear liquid. Question 355BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Offering the client a glass of thin water because the client requested it): thin liquids move too quickly for a client with dysphagia to control, so giving them against a mildly thick (nectar-thick) order raises the risk of aspiration. Checking for pocketed food is a safe practice, because food held in a weak cheek can later be aspirated. Small bites at a slow pace give the client time to swallow fully and lower the risk of choking. Sitting fully upright during meals uses gravity to protect the airway and is the recommended position.

Question 356ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Contact precautions with gown and gloves, plus hand hygiene using soap and water): norovirus spreads through contact with stool, vomit and contaminated surfaces, and soap and water remove it more reliably than alcohol rubs. Droplet precautions alone do not address the main contact route, and alcohol-based rub is less effective against norovirus. Norovirus is not an airborne disease, so a negative-pressure room and respirators are not required. Standard precautions alone are not enough during an outbreak, and relying on alcohol-based rub leaves hands contaminated.

Question 357ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (The client with disseminated herpes zoster involving multiple skin areas): disseminated herpes zoster can spread the varicella virus through the air, so it requires airborne and contact precautions in a negative-pressure room. Influenza spreads by large respiratory droplets and is managed with droplet precautions in a standard private room. A wound colonized with MRSA requires contact precautions, not an airborne isolation room. A urinary tract infection caused by Escherichia coli is managed with standard precautions.

Disturbed sleep is a real concern, but it is not life threatening and can be addressed after glucose is corrected. Question 361APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Give 15 to 20 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate, such as juice, by mouth): a conscious client who can swallow safely is treated with oral fast-acting carbohydrate, which raises glucose within minutes. This approach follows the protocol and avoids the delay of obtaining intravenous access. Fat and protein slow absorption, so a peanut butter and cheese snack does not correct a dangerously low glucose quickly enough. Question 362BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Recheck the point-of-care blood glucose and repeat treatment if still below 70): the protocol calls for a recheck 15 minutes after treatment, and another dose of fast-acting carbohydrate is given if glucose remains low. Symptoms can ease before glucose is safely restored, so only a measured value confirms recovery. Giving insulin early would drive glucose down again and could cause a more severe low. Letting the client sleep until morning labs could allow a recurrent low to go unnoticed for hours.

Question 363CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Glucose of 98 mg/dL, with the client alert, oriented and no longer sweating): a normal glucose with return of alert, oriented mental status and resolution of sweating shows the hypoglycemia has resolved. Because breakfast is more than one hour away, the nurse then gives a snack with protein and complex carbohydrate. A glucose of 52 mg/dL is still below 70 mg/dL, so treatment must be repeated even though symptoms are easing. Ongoing confusion despite a glucose of 74 mg/dL is not a full recovery and needs further assessment and provider notification.

Question 364AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (A 45-year-old with obesity whose mother and sister have type 2 diabetes): obesity, age over 35 and diabetes in first-degree relatives are major risk factors, and screening is recommended for this client. A young adult of normal weight with no risk factors has low risk and does not meet screening criteria. A young marathon runner with a normal body mass index has low risk, because regular activity and healthy weight protect against diabetes. A young adult of normal weight who exercises regularly has low risk and does not need screening at this time.

Question 365CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Adults 50 to 80 with 20 pack-years who smoke now or quit within 15 years): the USPSTF recommends annual low-dose CT for adults aged 50 to 80 with at least a 20 pack-year history who currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years. A chronic cough is a symptom that calls for diagnostic evaluation, not a criterion for screening. Adults with no smoking history do not meet the screening criteria, even with secondhand smoke exposure. The ages of 55 to 75 and the 30 pack-year threshold reflect older guidance that was replaced in 2021.

Question 366DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (An 85-year-old with dementia who relies on an exhausted adult child for care): cognitive impairment, dependence on a caregiver and caregiver stress are major risk factors for elder abuse and neglect. A client who lives with a spouse and manages personal finances keeps independence and control, which lowers risk. Regular volunteering provides social contact and outside observers who could notice signs of abuse. A client who drives and takes part in community activities is independent and socially connected, which lowers risk.

Question 367DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Follow the treatment plan by redirecting the client and providing supervision after meals): excessive exercise after meals is a compensatory behavior, and consistent limits with supervision support weight restoration. Praising the activity reinforces the compensatory behavior and undermines the treatment plan. Trading exercise for eating sets up a bargain that rewards compensatory exercise and conflicts with the activity limits. Journaling can be helpful, but letting the exercise continue allows the compensatory behavior to go on.

Question 368ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Use a bed or chair alarm and check on the client at frequent intervals): alarms and frequent checks alert staff when the client gets up while preserving the client's freedom and dignity. Routine sedation increases confusion, falls and aspiration risk in an older adult and is used only as a last resort. Restraints can cause injury, agitation and strangulation and are not used when less restrictive measures have not been tried. Locking a client in a room is a form of seclusion that is not acceptable and creates a safety hazard in an emergency.

Question 369CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Fear of losing control and of bodily injury from procedures and treatments): school-age children value industry and mastery, so they fear losing control, bodily injury and separation from their peers. Stranger anxiety is typical of older infants and is not the main fear of a school-age child. Concern about body image and acceptance by romantic partners is typical of adolescents. Protest, despair and detachment in response to separation from parents are characteristic of toddlers.

Question 370DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (The potassium level drawn at 1400 is still pending and needs follow-up): a pending potassium result for a client receiving potassium is an unresolved issue that the next nurse must act on to keep care safe. Room temperature and lighting preferences support comfort but do not affect the client's immediate safety. Television preferences are personal details that do not influence clinical decisions during the next shift. Family visiting plans are helpful but are less important than following up on a pending laboratory result.

Question 371DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Blood pressure falling from 128/76 to 88/50 with a heart rate of 124): a falling blood pressure with a rising heart rate suggests hemorrhage or hypovolemic shock and needs immediate intervention. Pain rated 3 out of 10 after an opioid shows that pain control is working. Mild nausea that responds to an antiemetic is common after anesthesia and is managed by the nurse. Hunger is a reassuring sign of returning bowel function, and the diet is advanced according to the prescription.

Question 372DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Measuring vital signs for a stable client admitted two days ago): measuring vital signs on a stable client is a routine task with a predictable outcome that falls within the assistive personnel's role. Auscultating lung sounds is an assessment that requires nursing judgment and stays with the RN. Evaluating the response to an opioid requires clinical judgment and cannot be delegated. Initial teaching about a new medication device is a nursing responsibility that cannot be delegated to assistive personnel.

Question 373CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Staff nurses collect fall data, identify problems and help test practice changes): frontline nurses see care processes directly, so they gather data, spot problems and help test changes in practice. Every fall, with or without injury, must be reported so the team can find patterns and correct system problems. Quality improvement depends on input from staff who deliver care, not only on decisions made by managers. Effective quality improvement focuses on system causes in a just culture rather than on blaming individuals.

Question 374DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Pyridoxine, also called vitamin B6, taken once daily with the isoniazid): isoniazid depletes pyridoxine and can cause peripheral neuropathy, especially in clients with alcohol use or diabetes, so pyridoxine is given to prevent it. Phytonadione reverses clotting problems and has no role in preventing neuropathy from isoniazid. Ascorbic acid does not protect the nerves from the effects of isoniazid. Ferrous sulfate treats iron deficiency anemia and does not prevent isoniazid-related neuropathy.

Question 375CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Uncompensated respiratory acidosis, because carbon dioxide is high and bicarbonate is normal): the pH is below 7.35, the PaCO₂ is above 45 mm Hg in the opposite direction, and the bicarbonate is within 22 to 26 mEq/L, so no compensation has occurred. Metabolic acidosis requires a low bicarbonate, but this value is normal and the carbon dioxide is high. Partial compensation would show a bicarbonate above 26 mEq/L, which is not present here. The pH of 7.30 is below the normal range and shows acidosis, so neither alkalosis nor full compensation applies.

Question 376APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Give intramuscular epinephrine in the anterolateral thigh per the anaphylaxis protocol): intramuscular epinephrine is the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis, because it reverses airway swelling, bronchospasm and hypotension. Antihistamines are adjuncts that relieve hives but do not treat airway swelling or shock, and delaying epinephrine is dangerous. Cool compresses relieve skin symptoms only, and raising the head of the bed can worsen hypotension. Corticosteroids act too slowly to treat acute anaphylaxis and are not a substitute for epinephrine.

Question 377CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (A client who received an opioid an hour ago and feels dizzy standing): opioids cause sedation and orthostatic dizziness, which greatly increase the risk of falling after surgery. An alert client with a steady, normal gait has a low fall risk. A client who walks independently and takes no sedating drugs has few fall risk factors. A client who received no sedation and is ready for discharge does not show the risk factors that call for a fall plan.

Question 378CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("Keep heat sources off the patch, and fold used patches sticky sides together for disposal."): heat speeds fentanyl absorption and can cause overdose, and used patches still contain drug that must be folded and disposed of safely. Cutting a patch damages the release system and can deliver an unpredictable dose, so sleepiness is reported to the provider instead. A heating pad increases absorption and can cause dangerous respiratory depression. Leaving the old patch on while the new one is applied doubles the dose and raises the risk of overdose.

Question 379BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B ("It helps the team and family work through difficult ethical decisions together."): an ethics committee offers guidance and facilitates discussion when values conflict, such as decisions about end-of-life care. The committee is advisory and does not issue legal rulings, which are made by courts. Nursing errors are handled through risk management and human resources processes, not the ethics committee. Medication orders and staffing are managed by providers, pharmacy and nursing leadership rather than an ethics committee.

Question 380DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Discuss the sedative with the provider and implement fall precautions overnight): sedative hypnotics increase falls in older adults, so reviewing the medication and using fall precautions lowers the client's risk. Giving the drug earlier does not remove its sedating effect during the nighttime trips to the bathroom. A higher dose increases sedation, confusion and the risk of a serious fall. Raising all four side rails acts as a restraint, and clients who climb over rails can suffer worse injuries.

Question 381DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Combining behavioral counseling with FDA-approved medication such as nicotine replacement therapy): counseling combined with approved medication produces the highest quit rates and is recommended by the USPSTF. Quitting without counseling or medication has much lower success rates than combined treatment. Smokeless tobacco still delivers nicotine and carcinogens and is not a cessation method. Smoking fewer cigarettes still carries cardiovascular risk, and clients often inhale more deeply to compensate.

Question 382CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Reaching across the sterile field to place a package on the far side): reaching over a sterile field can drop particles or brush the surface with an unsterile arm, contaminating the field. Treating the outer 1-inch border as contaminated is correct sterile technique. Opening the first flap away from the body keeps the nurse from reaching over the sterile contents. Items below the waist or out of sight are considered contaminated, so keeping them above the waist and in view maintains sterility.

Question 383APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Check the current serum potassium level and clarify the prescription with the provider): ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril raise potassium, so adding a supplement without a current level risks dangerous hyperkalemia. Many salt substitutes contain potassium chloride, which would add further to the risk of hyperkalemia. Holding the antihypertensive without a prescription is outside the nurse's role and does not address the missing potassium level. Taking the two drugs together does not improve absorption and ignores the interaction that raises potassium.

Question 384ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Trace the tubing from the insertion site to the bag for kinks, clamps or infiltration): tracing the line finds the cause, such as a closed clamp, a kinked tube, a bent arm or an infiltrated site, so it can be corrected. Increasing the rate does not fix the obstruction and can force fluid into the tissue if the site has infiltrated. Forceful flushing can push a clot into the circulation or rupture the vein. Switching to gravity flow hides the problem and loses the pump's protection without finding the cause.

Question 385CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C ("Around 6 months, when your baby can sit with support and shows interest."): most infants are ready for solids at about 6 months, when they have good head control, sit with support and show interest in food. Cereal in a bottle raises the risk of choking and excess calories, and it does not improve sleep. Fussiness after feeding is not a sign of readiness, and starting solids early can displace breast milk. Waiting until 12 months can lead to iron deficiency and feeding difficulties.

Question 386DManagement of Care

Delaying all documentation risks inaccurate records and missed communication between caregivers. Leaving after accepting report could be considered client abandonment. The correct answer is A (Focus on the client's feelings and recent stressors rather than on the arm): conversion symptoms express psychological stress, so the nurse helps the client explore feelings and build coping skills. Question 390APsychosocial Integrity

Question 397BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Notify the provider of the findings and avoid massaging the affected leg): unilateral calf pain, warmth, redness and swelling after joint surgery suggest deep vein thrombosis, which needs prompt evaluation and treatment. Question 398DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Superficial partial-thickness burn, also called a second-degree burn injury): blisters, moist red skin, blanching and severe pain show injury into the dermis with intact nerve endings. Superficial burns are red and painful but do not form blisters. Full-thickness burns appear dry, leathery or white and are often painless because nerve endings are destroyed. Burns that reach muscle or bone appear charred and painless, which does not match these findings.

Question 399BPhysiological Adaptation

Question 400ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control
