Question 201AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Failing to check the client's vital signs after the procedure as prescribed): negligence is a failure to meet the standard of care, and missing prescribed postprocedure vital signs could allow bleeding or instability to go unnoticed and harm the client. Reporting a medication error and completing an incident report meets professional and legal expectations for safety. Raising the side rails for a sedated client as prescribed follows the plan of care and protects the client from falls. Accurate and timely documentation is part of the standard of care and supports safe communication among the team.

Question 202BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (A client with asthma who has a respiratory rate of 32 and uses accessory muscles): a rapid respiratory rate with accessory muscle use signals respiratory distress, which is an airway and breathing problem that can quickly become life-threatening. Moderate pain after a hip fracture needs prompt management but does not threaten the airway or breathing. A fasting glucose of 190 mg/dL needs follow-up and possible insulin coverage but is not an immediate threat to life. A low-grade fever in a client with pneumonia is expected and can be addressed after the client in respiratory distress is assessed.

Question 203DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Folic acid, which reduces side effects such as mouth sores and nausea): methotrexate is a folate antagonist, and folic acid supplementation lowers the risk of mouth sores, nausea, liver enzyme elevation and some blood count changes. Vitamin K is used to reverse warfarin or treat clotting factor deficiency and is not routinely given with methotrexate. Iron is not a standard supplement with methotrexate because the drug does not cause iron loss. Methotrexate does not cause renal potassium wasting, so routine potassium replacement is not indicated.

Question 204BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Catatonia, a psychomotor syndrome that needs prompt treatment to prevent complications): immobility, mutism, staring, posturing and refusal to eat or drink are classic signs of catatonia, which can lead to dehydration, malnutrition and blood clots without prompt treatment. Severe anxiety usually causes restlessness and verbal distress rather than mutism, posturing and refusal of fluids. Delirium tremens typically appears within a few days of stopping alcohol and causes agitation, tremors, fever and hallucinations rather than stillness. An acute dystonic reaction causes sudden painful muscle spasms of the neck, eyes or tongue rather than prolonged staring and refusal to eat.

Question 205DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Dark red lochia rubra with a fleshy odor and a few small clots): lochia rubra is expected for about the first 3 days after birth and may contain a few small clots. Clots the size of a plum suggest excessive bleeding or retained tissue and should be reported to the provider. Lochia serosa usually begins around day 3 or 4, so it would be early on the second day. Lochia alba appears after the first week or two and would be unexpected this soon after birth.

Question 206AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Explore the client's reasons and confirm that the client understands the possible consequences): respecting autonomy begins with making sure the refusal is informed, after which the nurse notifies the provider and documents the discussion. Giving a transfusion that a competent client has refused violates the client's rights and could be considered battery. Asking family to pressure the client is coercive and undermines the client's right to decide. Documenting the refusal alone leaves out the steps that make the refusal informed and keep the provider aware.

Question 207BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Combine current research and guidelines with clinical expertise and client preferences): evidence-based practice integrates the best available research with clinical expertise and the values and preferences of the client. Online forum recommendations are opinions that have not been critically appraised and are not reliable evidence. Tradition and familiarity do not show that a method is effective or safe. Personal experience is one part of clinical expertise but is not sufficient without research evidence and client input.

Question 208DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Stop the infusion, stimulate the client, support breathing and give naloxone per protocol): these are signs of opioid-induced respiratory depression, so stopping the opioid and supporting breathing come first, with naloxone given per protocol or prescription. Relying on family to keep the client awake does not treat the respiratory depression and delays emergency care. Waiting an hour to recheck could allow hypoxia and respiratory arrest to develop. Teaching about the dosing button is not appropriate for a client who is difficult to arouse and in immediate danger.

Question 209BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Support the client's right to decide while involving the family as the client wishes): a competent adult makes their own health care decisions, and the nurse respects the client's choice about how much the family is involved. Sending the family away removes support the client may want and does not respect the client's wishes. Allowing the children to decide takes away the autonomy of a client who is alert and able to decide. Older age alone does not suggest incapacity, so requesting a capacity evaluation without other cues is not justified.

Question 210DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Secretions in the airway or kinked ventilator tubing that is increasing resistance): high-pressure alarms are most often caused by secretions, coughing, biting or kinked tubing that increase resistance to airflow. Disconnection lowers airway pressure and triggers a low-pressure alarm rather than a high-pressure alarm. A cuff leak allows air to escape and also causes a low-pressure or low-volume alarm. A power problem triggers a power or ventilator failure alarm rather than a high-pressure alarm.

Question 211BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Replace the pump, then remove, label and report the damaged one per policy): damaged electrical equipment is a fire and shock hazard, so it is taken out of service, labeled and reported according to policy after a safe replacement is in place. Moving the cord and delaying the repair request leaves a shock and fire hazard in use at the bedside. Taping a frayed cord does not make it safe and hides the defect from other staff. Running the pump on battery leaves faulty equipment in use and risks the battery failing before the shift ends.

Question 212BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Stop the infusion and follow the facility's extravasation protocol for this drug): extravasation of a vesicant can cause severe tissue damage, so the nurse stops the infusion at once and follows protocol, which may include aspiration and an antidote. Heat helps with some vesicants but worsens injury from others, so a compress is applied only as the drug-specific protocol directs. Slowing the rate continues to deliver the vesicant into the tissue and increases damage. Flushing the catheter pushes more drug and fluid into the injured tissue.

Question 213DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("Most babies begin smiling socially in response to people at about 2 months."): CDC milestone checklists list smiling when spoken to or smiled at as a social milestone at about 2 months of age. Waiting until 6 months would delay recognition of a possible developmental concern. By 9 months most infants show stranger anxiety, which develops long after the first social smile. A first social smile at 12 months would be a significant delay that warrants evaluation.

Question 214ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Put on gloves, clean up the spill and disinfect the area according to policy): standard precautions require gloves and an approved disinfectant for blood spills, and prompt cleanup also prevents a slip and fall. Covering the spill leaves blood on the floor and creates a fall and exposure hazard. Touching blood without gloves exposes the nurse to bloodborne pathogens. Leaving an uncleaned spill while waiting for housekeeping puts the client and others at risk of slipping and exposure.

Question 215DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("Schedule a dental visit by the first birthday or within 6 months of the first tooth."): early dental visits help prevent early childhood caries and give parents guidance on oral hygiene, fluoride and feeding habits. Waiting until age 5 misses years of prevention when early childhood decay commonly develops. Waiting for pain or visible decay means problems are found late instead of prevented. All primary teeth are usually in by about age 3, which is later than recommended for a first visit.

Question 216DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Acknowledge the experience, say you do not hear voices, and redirect to reality): the nurse accepts that the experience is real to the client without agreeing with it and helps focus on real events, while briefly assessing for command content that affects safety. Lengthy questioning about the voices reinforces the hallucinations instead of focusing on reality. Agreeing that the voices are real supports the false perception and can increase the client's distress. Leaving the client alone may increase isolation and prevents the nurse from assessing safety.

Question 217AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (An expected finding showing that the chest tube and drainage system are patent): tidaling, the rise and fall of water with breathing, reflects normal pressure changes in the pleural space and shows the system is working. An air leak is shown by continuous bubbling in the water-seal chamber, which is absent here. Clamping is not indicated for an expected finding and could cause a tension pneumothorax. A blocked or kinked tube would stop the fluctuation rather than produce it.

Question 218CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Confirm placement on an X-ray before the first feeding, then monitor markings and aspirate): radiographic confirmation is the most reliable initial check, and afterward the nurse monitors the external length marking and aspirate per policy. Auscultating instilled air is unreliable because sounds from a tube in the lung or esophagus can be mistaken for gastric placement. Clients with a stroke may have reduced cough and gag reflexes, so the absence of coughing does not rule out airway placement. The bubbling test is inaccurate and may cause aspiration of water if the tube is in the airway.

Question 219BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (An 82-year-old who takes a diuretic and a sleeping pill and voids at night): advanced age, diuretic use, sedative use and nighttime trips to the bathroom combine to create the greatest fall risk. A young adult under observation for a mild allergic reaction has few fall risk factors. A young client who is walking independently after an appendectomy is at low risk. A wrist cast and acetaminophen do not affect balance or alertness in the way that sedatives and diuretics do.

Question 220DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis, appearing as a shallow open area): a stage 2 pressure injury involves partial-thickness loss of skin with exposed dermis, which may also appear as an intact or ruptured serum-filled blister. Exposed bone, tendon or muscle describes a stage 4 pressure injury. Full-thickness loss with visible fat describes a stage 3 pressure injury. Intact skin with nonblanchable redness describes a stage 1 pressure injury.

Question 221CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Stay with the client, speak calmly in short sentences and guide slow breathing): a client in severe anxiety needs a calm presence and simple, direct guidance, and slow breathing helps reduce the physical symptoms. Detailed teaching is not effective during a panic attack because severe anxiety narrows the ability to process information. Leaving the client alone can increase fear and loss of control. Telling the client nothing is wrong dismisses real distress and can damage trust.

Question 222CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Hyponatremia caused by water retention that dilutes the serum sodium level): excess antidiuretic hormone causes the kidneys to retain water, which dilutes serum sodium, and treatment often includes fluid restriction. Large volumes of dilute urine with hypernatremia describe diabetes insipidus, the opposite of this condition. Kidney function is not the problem in this syndrome, and hyperkalemia is not a typical finding. Hypercalcemia from a parathyroid-like hormone is a different paraneoplastic syndrome and does not explain water retention.

Question 223ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (On an individualized schedule, often every 2 to 3 hours): current guidelines recommend an individualized repositioning schedule based on mobility, skin and tissue tolerance, condition and the support surface, often every 2 to 3 hours for high-risk clients. Repositioning once a day leaves pressure on the same areas for many hours and greatly increases injury risk. A client with a spinal cord injury may not feel discomfort, so waiting for requests is unsafe. Once per shift allows pressure to build for 8 to 12 hours, which is far too long for a high-risk client.

Question 224CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Keep the client within close sight, including during personal care, as policy requires): one-to-one observation means continuous close monitoring according to policy, with hazardous items removed from the environment. Checks every 15 minutes are a lower level of observation and leave time for self-harm. A razor is a hazardous item that should not be available to a client on suicide precautions. Hourly checks do not meet the requirement for continuous one-to-one observation.

Question 225DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Place the affected left arm in the sleeve first, then the unaffected arm): dressing the affected side first and undressing it last reduces strain on the weak limb and makes dressing easier. Starting with the stronger arm leaves too little slack to fit the weak arm into the sleeve. Leaving the order to comfort does not build a consistent, safe technique for the client and spouse. Moving both arms together is difficult with one-sided weakness and can strain the affected shoulder.

Question 226DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Liver toxicity from high total doses, hidden acetaminophen in combination products and alcohol): acetaminophen can cause severe liver damage, and the risk rises with regular alcohol use and unrecognized acetaminophen in cold products. Acetaminophen is not associated with kidney stone formation. Gastrointestinal bleeding is a concern with aspirin and other nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs rather than acetaminophen. Acetaminophen does not depress respiration, which is a concern with opioids.

Question 227AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A ("I will hold my baby semi-upright and hold the bottle during every feeding."): holding the infant semi-upright and holding the bottle reduces choking and aspiration risk and supports bonding. Microwaving formula creates hot spots that can burn the infant's mouth even after swirling. Adding cereal to a bottle increases choking risk and is not recommended to promote sleep. Propping the bottle increases the risk of choking, ear infections and tooth decay.

Treating the situation as a discipline issue overlooks the staffing shortage behind the refusal. A reminder about job duties does not reduce the workload that is preventing the care from being done. Analyzing the cause accurately helps the charge nurse choose a solution that improves both safety and teamwork.

A mediation meeting is useful but delays care for clients whose skin is at risk. Writing a report focuses on blame and does nothing to relieve pressure on the clients' skin. Once the clients are repositioned, the charge nurse can turn attention to workload and team communication.

Question 231CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Rebalance the assignments and meet privately with both staff members to discuss concerns): redistributing the workload fixes the underlying problem, and a private meeting allows open discussion that supports teamwork. Discussing a staff conflict at the nurses' station exposes it to visitors and clients and can embarrass staff. Leaving staff to work it out alone ignores the unequal assignments and may let the conflict grow. Combining a workload fix with private conflict resolution addresses both the cause and the strained relationship.

Question 232BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Reposition the two overdue clients now, then continue with the staffing plan): immediate repositioning relieves pressure and prevents further skin injury, and the charge nurse is available to provide that care. Bedbound clients cannot shift their weight effectively on their own, so pressure would continue. Delaying another hour extends pressure on skin that is already injured. Taking direct action also models teamwork for the staff while the longer-term plan is put in place.

Question 233AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Clients are turned on schedule, and staff agree on a plan for sharing tasks): safe client care combined with cooperation and an agreed plan shows that both the care problem and the conflict were resolved. A complaint and a warning create a winner and a loser and do not fix the workload problem. Avoidance may reduce open tension but leaves the conflict unresolved and weakens teamwork. Effective conflict management is measured by safe outcomes for clients and a working relationship among staff.

Question 234CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Notify the provider, explain the risks to the client and document the discussion): a competent adult may leave, but the nurse informs the provider, explains risks and alternatives, documents the discussion and usually asks the client to sign a form. Claiming insurance will not pay is misleading and is not an appropriate way to influence the client. Proceeding with discharge steps before the provider is notified and risks are explained bypasses the informed refusal process. Holding a competent client against their will is false imprisonment.

Question 235DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Go to the client, assess responsiveness and symptoms, and obtain full vital signs): the nurse first determines whether the client is stable by assessing symptoms and vital signs, which guides the report to the provider and next steps. Silencing the alarm and waiting delays assessment of a possibly unstable rhythm. Walking increases cardiac demand and could worsen symptoms or cause a fall. A rhythm strip is useful documentation but does not replace assessing the client.

Question 236BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Refer the client to the social worker or a financial counselor for help): social workers and financial counselors can help the client explore insurance options, assistance programs and payment plans. Early discharge for financial reasons could compromise recovery and does not address the concern. False reassurance dismisses the client's worry and may not be accurate. Waiting until after discharge leaves the client anxious now and delays access to resources.

Question 237DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (It reduces cravings for alcohol and blocks the pleasant effects of drinking): naltrexone is an opioid antagonist that reduces craving and the rewarding effects of alcohol, which helps prevent relapse. Flushing and vomiting with alcohol describe disulfiram, which works by a different mechanism. Naltrexone does not substitute for alcohol and has no sedative or intoxicating effect. Withdrawal seizures and tremors are treated with benzodiazepines rather than naltrexone.

Question 238DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("Options include breathing techniques, position changes, IV medications and an epidural."): a range of nonpharmacologic and pharmacologic methods is available, and the choice depends on the client's preference, labor progress and the provider's assessment. Many methods besides epidural analgesia can effectively reduce labor pain. Analgesia is commonly given during active labor and does not require full dilation. Labor medications are used safely with monitoring, so avoiding all medication is not necessary.

Question 239CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Clean them daily and store them in a labeled cup with water or soaking solution): daily cleaning and storage in a labeled, covered cup with liquid keep dentures clean, moist and safe from loss or damage. Very hot water can warp dentures and change their fit. Dentures left dry can warp, and a tissue is easily thrown away by mistake. Dentures wrapped in a napkin on a meal tray are often discarded with the trash.

Question 240DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Use short, simple sentences and give the client one instruction at a time): short sentences and one-step instructions reduce confusion and agitation for a client with dementia. Several questions at once overwhelm a client with impaired memory and processing. Rapid speech is harder to follow and can increase frustration. Arguing with a mistaken belief often increases distress and may cause the client to grieve the loss again.

Question 241CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Face the client in good light, speak clearly and check the client's understanding): facing the client allows lip reading and visual cues, and checking understanding confirms the teaching was received. Teaching only the daughter excludes the client from their own care. Shouting distorts speech and can make words harder to understand. Speaking from behind removes the visual cues the client relies on.

Question 242CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (A client with a head injury who has a new dilated, nonreactive right pupil): a new dilated, nonreactive pupil is a sign of rising intracranial pressure and possible brain herniation, an immediate threat to life. A saturation of 90% is often an acceptable target for a client with chronic lung disease and is unchanged. A glucose of 186 mg/dL needs coverage per protocol but is not life-threatening. Moderate pain with medication due soon can be managed shortly after the unstable client is seen.

Question 243DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Hold the nitroglycerin and notify the provider about the recent sildenafil use): nitrates combined with a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor taken within 24 hours can cause severe hypotension, so the provider must choose another treatment. A reduced dose still carries the risk of a dangerous drop in blood pressure. A normal blood pressure now does not prevent the severe hypotension caused by this interaction. Sublingual nitroglycerin dissolves under the tongue, and water does not make the combination safe.

Question 244DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D ("I have a severe headache that gets much worse when I sit up."): a headache that worsens when upright suggests a cerebrospinal fluid leak at the puncture site and should be reported. Hunger after fasting for the procedure is expected and is easily addressed. Fatigue after a poor night of sleep is common and does not suggest a complication. Mild soreness at the puncture site is expected after the procedure.

Question 245ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Marking the operative site and performing a team time-out just before the procedure): site marking and a time-out with the whole team before the incision are key steps of the Universal Protocol for preventing wrong-site surgery. A single check at admission is not enough because verification must be repeated up to the start of the procedure. Schedules can contain errors and are not a substitute for active verification with the client and team. Reviewing consent after the procedure is too late to prevent an error.

Question 246BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Tall, peaked T waves that may progress to more dangerous dysrhythmias): hyperkalemia causes tall, peaked T waves and can progress to widened complexes and life-threatening dysrhythmias. Prominent U waves and flattened T waves are associated with low potassium. Elevation of the ST segment suggests acute myocardial injury rather than an electrolyte problem. A shortened QT interval is associated with high calcium rather than high potassium.

Question 247APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A ("Remove the patch at bedtime as directed to provide a nitrate-free period."): a daily nitrate-free period, often overnight, helps prevent tolerance to nitroglycerin. The old patch is removed before a new one is applied to a different site to prevent excess dosing and skin irritation. Cutting a patch alters the delivered dose, and headache is an expected effect that is usually treated with a mild analgesic. Nitroglycerin patches are designed for daily application rather than multiday wear.

Question 248CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Adjust the roller clamp until the drip rate is 31 drops per minute): 1,000 mL multiplied by 15 drops per mL equals 15,000 drops, and 15,000 divided by 480 minutes is 31.25, which rounds to 31 drops per minute. A rate of 125 is the hourly volume in milliliters and does not account for the drop factor or minutes. A rate of 8 drops per minute comes from dividing the hourly volume by the drop factor instead of multiplying by it and dividing by 60. A rate of 21 drops per minute would match a drop factor of 10 rather than 15.

Question 249DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Draw up 12.5 mL of the suspension in an oral dosing syringe): 10 mg multiplied by 25 kg equals 250 mg, and 250 mg divided by 100 mg multiplied by 5 mL equals 12.5 mL. Giving 25 mL would deliver 500 mg, which is twice the prescribed dose. Giving 5 mL would deliver only 100 mg, which is less than half the prescribed dose. Giving 10 mL would deliver 200 mg, which is below the 250 mg prescribed.

Question 250BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B ("Regular checkups, often every 6 to 12 months, based on your dentist's advice."): regular visits help prevent and detect problems early, and the dentist sets the interval based on individual oral health. A 5-year interval is too long to catch cavities and gum disease early. Good home care is important but does not replace professional checkups at regular intervals. Many dental problems cause no pain until they are advanced, so waiting for symptoms delays care.

Question 251DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A client two days after a stroke who coughs and chokes while drinking water): coughing and choking while swallowing after a stroke signals dysphagia, which a speech-language pathologist evaluates and treats to reduce aspiration risk. Hearing loss that affects conversation calls for an audiology referral rather than a swallowing or speech evaluation. Difficulty transferring after hip replacement is addressed by physical therapy, which focuses on mobility and strength. A new diabetic foot ulcer needs wound care and possibly a wound care nurse, not speech-language services.

Question 252DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D ("Pain in the missing foot is real, and there are treatments that can help."): phantom limb pain is a real neurologic experience after amputation, and validating it opens the door to treatment with medications, mirror therapy and other measures. Burning in the missing foot is a typical phantom sensation and does not by itself indicate an incision infection. Telling the client the feeling will fade within a day gives false reassurance, because phantom pain can persist for months. Saying the pain is not treated dismisses a real symptom that responds to analgesics, nerve pain medications and other therapies.

Question 253AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Urine output has increased to 50 mL per hour over the last two hours): a rising urine output shows that kidney perfusion is improving as circulating volume is restored. A heart rate climbing to 130 suggests ongoing or worsening volume depletion rather than recovery. A further drop in blood pressure indicates that the deficit is not being corrected and needs prompt reporting. Persistent dry mucous membranes and thirst show that the client remains dehydrated.

Question 254DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (A bedside swallow screen to check for difficulty swallowing and aspiration risk): facial droop and slurred speech after a stroke suggest possible dysphagia, so a swallow screen must be passed before any oral food, fluids or medications. A baseline weight is useful for nutrition planning but does not protect the client from aspiration at this meal. Blood pressure monitoring is important after a stroke, yet a normal reading does not show that swallowing is safe. Hearing affects communication but does not determine whether the client can swallow food without aspirating.

Question 255CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Hold the warfarin dose, assess for signs of bleeding and notify the provider): an INR of 4.8 is above the therapeutic range and raises bleeding risk, so the nurse holds the dose, checks for bleeding and obtains provider direction. Giving the full dose would push the INR even higher and increase the danger of hemorrhage. Adding a large amount of food rich in vitamin K is an unpredictable way to change anticoagulation and is not a nursing decision. Reducing the dose on the nurse's own judgment is outside the nurse's scope without a provider order.

Question 256CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Ciprofloxacin 500 mg by mouth twice daily for seven days for the infection): ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone with a boxed warning for tendinitis and tendon rupture, and the risk is higher in older adults who take corticosteroids. Amoxicillin can cause rash, diarrhea or allergic reactions but is not linked to tendon rupture. Docusate is a stool softener that may cause mild cramping but does not affect tendons. Acetaminophen requires attention to the daily maximum to protect the liver, not monitoring for tendon pain.

Question 257BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Diabetic ketoacidosis in a client with type 1 diabetes who missed insulin doses): a low pH with a low bicarbonate and a normal carbon dioxide level indicates metabolic acidosis, which results from ketone accumulation in diabetic ketoacidosis. Hyperventilation blows off carbon dioxide and produces respiratory alkalosis with a high pH. Opioid overdose depresses breathing, retains carbon dioxide and causes respiratory acidosis. Prolonged vomiting removes stomach acid and leads to metabolic alkalosis with a high bicarbonate.

Question 258APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Coffee-ground emesis, black tarry stools and a hemoglobin of 7.2 g/dL): coffee-ground emesis shows partly digested blood from the stomach, black tarry stools reflect blood passing through the gut and the low hemoglobin confirms significant loss. Ibuprofen use is a risk factor for ulcers, yet a risk factor and anxiety do not show that bleeding is happening. The oxygen saturation, mildly fast breathing and normal creatinine are not specific to gastrointestinal bleeding. Active signs of blood loss together with a low hemoglobin are the cues that best confirm the problem.

Question 259CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (The client has hypovolemia from blood loss with compensatory tachycardia and vasoconstriction): a fast heart rate, low blood pressure, cool pale skin and delayed capillary refill reflect the body compensating for lost circulating volume. Fluid volume excess would produce findings such as crackles, edema and high blood pressure, which this client does not have. Anxiety can raise the heart rate but does not explain low blood pressure, cool skin and delayed refill in a client who is bleeding. Recognizing hypovolemia early allows the nurse to anticipate rapid volume replacement before shock worsens.

Question 260BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Restoring circulating blood volume to maintain perfusion to the vital organs): ongoing blood loss with tachycardia and hypotension threatens perfusion to the brain, heart and kidneys, so volume restoration comes first. Teaching about safer pain relief is important to prevent recurrence but can wait until the client is stable. Discharge planning starts early in the stay, yet it does not address the immediate threat to life. Physiological needs that affect circulation take precedence over education and planning in priority setting.

Question 261BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Insert two large-bore IV lines, give fluids and blood and start an IV proton pump inhibitor): large-bore access allows rapid fluid and blood replacement, and an IV proton pump inhibitor reduces gastric acid to help stabilize the bleeding site. Oral intake is usually withheld during an active bleed in case endoscopy is needed, and ibuprofen would worsen mucosal injury and bleeding. A single small catheter at a keep-open rate cannot deliver the volume this client needs, and oral antacids do not control an active bleed. Acetaminophen, if ordered, is a safer choice for the headache because it does not damage the gastric lining.

Question 262APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Keep the client NPO and prepare for an upper endoscopy with procedural sedation): upper endoscopy allows the provider to see the bleeding ulcer and treat it directly, and NPO status lowers aspiration risk during sedation. A colonoscopy examines the lower bowel, while coffee-ground emesis points to a source in the stomach or duodenum, and a diet before the procedure is unsafe. Barium coats the gastrointestinal lining and can obscure the view during endoscopy, so it is avoided in an acute upper bleed. Preparing the client promptly for the correct procedure shortens the time to definitive treatment of the bleeding.

Question 263CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Heart rate 90, blood pressure 118/72 mm Hg and a stable repeat hemoglobin): a normal heart rate, improved blood pressure and a hemoglobin that is holding steady show that the bleeding has stopped and volume is restored. New confusion with a rising heart rate suggests reduced brain perfusion from continued bleeding and needs immediate reporting. Another black stool with falling blood pressure suggests rebleeding, even though some dark stool may pass as old blood clears. The nurse continues to trend vital signs and hemoglobin to confirm that the improvement is sustained.

Question 264BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (The serum potassium level, because loop diuretics increase potassium loss in the urine): furosemide promotes potassium excretion, and cramps, weakness and palpitations are classic signs of hypokalemia that can lead to dangerous dysrhythmias. Loop diuretics increase calcium excretion rather than causing it to accumulate, and these symptoms point more strongly to potassium loss. Sodium can shift with diuretics, but reduced swelling reflects fluid removal and does not indicate a high sodium level. Diuretics tend to raise rather than lower blood glucose, and glucose does not explain the palpitations.

Question 265CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Notify the surgeon that the client needs more information before the procedure begins): informed consent requires that the client understand the procedure, and the surgeon who performs it is responsible for providing that explanation. Reassuring the client about the surgeon's experience dismisses the concern and does not resolve the lack of understanding. A signature alone does not make consent valid when the client states that they do not understand the surgery. Explaining the procedure, risks and alternatives in detail is the surgeon's duty, although the nurse may reinforce information afterward.

Question 266DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Starting regular aerobic exercise and following a heart-healthy eating pattern): primary prevention aims to stop disease before it develops, and exercise with a healthy diet lowers the risk of coronary artery disease. Medication after a stent treats established disease to prevent complications, which is tertiary prevention. Cardiac rehabilitation helps a client recover function after a heart attack and is also tertiary prevention. Screening blood pressure and cholesterol detects disease early in people who may already have it, which is secondary prevention.

Question 267BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Starting norepinephrine as a vasopressor, titrated to a mean arterial pressure of at least 65): when the mean arterial pressure stays below 65 mm Hg after adequate fluids, guidelines call for a vasopressor, with norepinephrine as the first choice. Fluids are reassessed using perfusion markers rather than stopped outright, and stopping them would not raise the low pressure. Restlessness in shock often reflects poor brain perfusion, so sedating the client hides a warning sign without treating the cause. Antibiotics should continue on schedule, because delaying therapy while awaiting culture results increases mortality in sepsis.

Question 268BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Your stools may turn dark, and taking it with orange juice can improve absorption."): iron commonly turns stools dark green or black, and the ascorbic acid in orange juice enhances iron absorption. Dark stools are an expected effect of iron, so stopping the medication for this reason would leave the anemia untreated. Calcium in milk, along with antacids, reduces iron absorption, so the doses should be separated. Chewing or crushing iron tablets can stain teeth and irritate the mouth, and some forms are designed to be swallowed whole.

Question 269CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Cover the exposed bowel with sterile dressings moistened with sterile normal saline): covering the organs with moist sterile dressings keeps the tissue from drying out and protects it from contamination until surgical repair. Pushing the bowel back in can injure the tissue and introduce infection, so the nurse leaves it in place. The nurse stays with the client and uses the call light to summon help, because leaving the client alone risks further deterioration. A low Fowler position with the knees bent reduces abdominal tension, while sitting upright in a chair increases strain on the wound.

Question 270BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Avoid taking it with milk or antacids, and protect your skin from the sun."): dairy, antacids and iron bind tetracycline and reduce absorption, and the drug causes photosensitivity that can lead to severe sunburn. Milk lowers the amount of drug absorbed, so taking it with breakfast milk makes it less effective. Iron in the multivitamin binds tetracycline, so the two should be separated by several hours. Tetracycline increases sensitivity to sunlight, so the client needs sunscreen and protective clothing rather than tanning.

Question 271APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A ("Caring for your parent sounds exhausting, so let's talk about support and respite options."): acknowledging caregiver strain and offering resources such as respite care, hospice services or support groups addresses the caregiver's exhaustion directly. Comparing the caregiver to other families minimizes the distress and discourages further discussion. Urging the caregiver to keep going adds guilt and pressure without offering any help. Jumping to nursing home placement bypasses the caregiver's feelings and the many supports that could help at home.

Question 272CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Placing a bundle of soiled bed linens on the floor beside the client's bed): placing soiled linens on the floor spreads microorganisms to the environment, so linens go directly into a linen bag or hamper. Hand hygiene before helping a client eat is correct practice and needs no correction. Gloves are appropriate when handling urine, so this action follows standard precautions. Carrying soiled linens away from the uniform prevents transferring organisms to clothing and is correct technique.

Question 273DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D ("When I'm stressed, I take a long walk and then call a close friend."): physical activity and reaching out for social support reduce stress in ways that protect physical and emotional health. Withdrawing from everyone for weeks leads to isolation and can worsen anxiety and depression. Using alcohol to unwind is a maladaptive pattern that can lead to dependence and does not resolve the stressor. Stopping eating when upset harms nutrition and can signal an unhealthy pattern that needs further assessment.

Question 274DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Call for help, activate the emergency response and begin high-quality chest compressions): for a client in cardiac arrest, the nurse activates the emergency response and starts compressions immediately to restore circulation to the brain and heart. Documenting the time matters for the record, but another team member can do it and it does not support circulation. Checking glucose delays compressions, and every minute without CPR lowers the chance of survival. The family is notified after the emergency response is underway, not before lifesaving care begins.

Question 275CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C ("I can't give legal advice, but I can connect you with our patient relations office."): the nurse does not give legal advice or referrals but can direct the client to the patient advocate or patient relations department to address the complaint. Judging whether the client has a case is a legal opinion outside the nurse's role and dismisses the client's concern. Referring the client to a relative's law firm creates a conflict of interest and is inappropriate professional behavior. Redirecting to wound care avoids the concern and may increase the client's anger and mistrust.

Question 276DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Cluster nighttime care, reduce noise and dim the lights in the room and hallway): grouping care to limit interruptions and creating a quiet, dark environment support the client's normal sleep cycle. Caffeine in regular coffee is a stimulant that delays sleep onset and fragments sleep. Hourly vital signs are not ordered for this stable client, and frequent waking further disrupts rest. Light and sound from a television through the night interfere with deep sleep rather than promote it.

Question 277AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Notify the provider immediately about the blood pressure and new neurologic symptoms): a pressure this high with headache and vision changes suggests a hypertensive emergency with possible organ damage that needs immediate provider evaluation. Resting and rechecking in four hours delays treatment while target organ injury may be progressing. Giving an extra dose without an order is outside the nurse's scope, and the pressure must be lowered in a controlled way. Waiting a week for a medication adjustment overlooks signs of an emergency that require same-day care.

Question 278AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A ("I should promptly report a new lump, swelling or change in size in a testicle."): routine screening self-examinations are not recommended for men without symptoms, but any new lump, swelling or change in size needs prompt evaluation. Testicular cancer is most common in young men between about 15 and 35 years of age, so waiting until 50 misses the highest-risk years. A painless lump is a classic early sign, so delaying evaluation until a yearly visit can allow the cancer to spread. A hard lump is not normal even when it does not hurt, and it should be reported rather than watched.

Question 279CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("I won't stop it suddenly, and I'll report fever or other signs of infection."): long-term corticosteroids suppress the adrenal glands, so they must be tapered, and they also suppress immunity and raise the risk of infection. Prednisone weakens bones and contributes to osteoporosis, so calcium supplements are usually continued. Corticosteroids raise blood glucose, so monitoring may need to increase rather than decrease. Stopping abruptly after months of therapy can trigger adrenal crisis with severe hypotension.

Question 280ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Keep the door closed except when people need to enter or leave the room): negative pressure isolation pulls air into the room and vents it safely, which works only when the door stays closed. Opening the door every hour lets infectious droplet nuclei escape into the hallway. Leaving the door partly open for observation breaks the pressure difference and exposes others to the organism. Airborne particles are released throughout the day and night, so propping the door open during daytime puts staff and visitors at risk.

Question 281DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Giving IV fluid resuscitation and titrating it to the client's hourly urine output): large burns cause massive capillary leak and fluid shifts, so fluid resuscitation guided by urine output prevents burn shock once the airway is secured. Skin grafting is performed after the client is hemodynamically stable and the wounds are prepared. Positioning and therapy to prevent contractures are important but follow stabilization of circulation. Early nutrition supports healing, yet it does not take priority over restoring circulating volume in the first day.

Question 282BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Idarucizumab given intravenously as a specific reversal agent for dabigatran): idarucizumab is a monoclonal antibody fragment that binds dabigatran and reverses its effect within minutes. Protamine sulfate reverses heparin and has no effect on dabigatran, which is a direct thrombin inhibitor. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepine sedation and does not affect anticoagulation. Phytonadione reverses warfarin by restoring vitamin-dependent clotting factors, a pathway that dabigatran does not use.

Question 283APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A ("Inhale one dose every day at the same time, even when you feel well."): tiotropium is a long-acting anticholinergic bronchodilator taken once daily to keep the airways open and prevent exacerbations. Using it only during colds removes the steady maintenance effect the client needs to control symptoms. The capsules are placed in the inhaler device and inhaled, and swallowing them delivers no drug to the lungs. Sudden shortness of breath calls for the short-acting albuterol inhaler, because tiotropium acts too slowly for rescue.

Question 284APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Ionized calcium, because citrate in stored blood binds calcium in the bloodstream): citrate used to preserve stored blood binds calcium, so rapid transfusion can cause hypocalcemia with perioral tingling and muscle twitching. Chloride changes are not a typical concern with transfusion and do not explain tingling and twitching. Phosphate is not the key electrolyte affected by rapid transfusion, and these symptoms point to low calcium. Citrate can also bind magnesium, but hypocalcemia is the most common and urgent problem after rapid transfusion.

Question 285APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Assist the client with nutrition, fluid intake and personal hygiene each day): meeting basic physiological needs such as food, fluids and hygiene comes first when depression prevents self-care. Career planning is a later goal that requires energy and concentration the client does not yet have. Group therapy is valuable, but physical needs and dehydration must be addressed before participation. Medication teaching is more effective after the client's basic needs are met and concentration improves.

Question 286CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (15 mL/h, because the dose is 24,000 mcg/h and the bag has 1,600 mcg/mL): the dose is 5 mcg/kg/min times 80 kg, or 400 mcg/min, which equals 24,000 mcg per hour, and 400 mg in 250 mL gives 1,600 mcg/mL, so 24,000 divided by 1,600 equals 15 mL/h. Setting the pump at 24 mL/h treats milligrams per hour as if they were milliliters per hour and leaves out the drug concentration. A rate of 1.5 mL/h comes from misplacing the decimal in the concentration, which is 1.6 mg/mL rather than 16 mg/mL. A rate of 0.25 mL/h is the volume needed each minute, so it must be multiplied by 60 to give the hourly rate.

Question 287ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (The nurse moves each of the client's joints through its range without client effort): passive range of motion is performed entirely by the nurse or caregiver for a client who cannot move the joints independently. Moving the joints without help is active range of motion, which this paralyzed client cannot perform. Moving against resistance is a strengthening exercise that requires voluntary muscle activity. Using the strong arm to move the weak arm is a self-assisted exercise, so the client still contributes effort.

The correct answer is A (New confusion, a diuretic, a bedtime sedative and frequent urination during the night): acute confusion impairs judgment, the diuretic and infection increase urgency, the sedative impairs balance and frequent nighttime voiding prompts unassisted trips to the bathroom. Living alone and handling finances describe the client's usual function and do not raise fall risk in the hospital tonight. Recognizing the full cluster of risk factors helps the nurse plan targeted fall prevention measures.

Question 289BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Delirium caused by the urinary tract infection, which can resolve with treatment): a sudden change in attention and orientation over days with fever and infection is typical of delirium, which often improves as the cause is treated. Normal aging does not cause an abrupt loss of attention and orientation in a previously independent adult. Dementia develops gradually over months to years, while this client's change began suddenly three days ago. Treating the infection and reducing contributing medications are key steps in reversing delirium.

Question 290CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Risk for injury from a fall while getting out of bed unassisted): the client is confused, sedated and trying to stand from a high bed, so immediate safety from a fall takes priority. Teaching about fluid intake cannot be absorbed by a delirious client and can wait until the client is safe and alert. Question 291ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Lower the bed, place the call light within reach, add a bed alarm and schedule toileting): a low bed, a reachable call light, a bed alarm and regular toileting reduce fall risk while avoiding restraints. Four raised side rails act as a restraint and can cause serious injury if a confused client climbs over them, and a dark room increases disorientation. Restraints increase agitation, injury and delirium and are a last resort only after less restrictive measures fail. Scheduled toileting is especially helpful because it meets the urgency that drives the client to get up alone.

Question 292CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (The bedtime zolpidem, which can worsen confusion and raise fall risk in older adults): sedative hypnotics such as zolpidem appear on the Beers Criteria list for older adults because they increase delirium, falls and fractures. A multivitamin does not meaningfully interact with ceftriaxone and does not contribute to the client's confusion. The antibiotic treats the infection that is causing the delirium, so stopping it would likely worsen the confusion. The furosemide schedule may also be reviewed so the second dose is not given late enough to cause nighttime urination.

Question 293BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (The client has had no falls, and orientation is improving as the infection clears): freedom from falls combined with improving orientation shows that both the safety measures and the treatment of the infection are working. Ongoing confusion that requires restraints indicates that the plan has not achieved its goals and needs revision. A fall without injury is still a fall, so the plan has not met the goal of preventing falls. Continued evaluation of mental status and safety helps the team decide when the client is ready for discharge.

Question 294BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B ("I had a pacemaker put in three years ago for a slow heart rate."): pacemakers and other implanted electronic devices can be affected by the magnetic field and require device-specific evaluation and approval before an MRI. A penicillin allergy does not affect MRI safety, although any history of contrast allergy would be reviewed separately. A past appendectomy without implanted metal hardware does not create an MRI safety concern. Glasses are removed before entering the scanner room, but wearing them does not require further screening.

Question 295CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Rapid, weak pulse with cool, pale and clammy skin on the extremities): the body compensates for blood loss with a fast heart rate and vasoconstriction, which produces cool, pale and clammy skin. Blood pressure falls rather than rises as circulating volume is lost, and the pulse becomes weak. Warm, flushed skin suggests distributive shock such as early sepsis, not hemorrhage. A slow heart rate with warm, dry skin is more typical of neurogenic shock from a spinal cord injury.

Question 296BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (A 70-year-old client who smokes and had an open upper abdominal surgery): older age, smoking and an upper abdominal incision that makes deep breathing painful greatly increase the risk of atelectasis and pneumonia. A young adult after tonsillectomy needs bleeding and airway monitoring but has fewer pulmonary risk factors. A skin biopsy under local anesthesia carries minimal risk for pulmonary complications. A young nonsmoker after knee arthroscopy has a low risk because the surgery does not affect breathing mechanics.

Question 297AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (A daily supplement containing 400 micrograms of folic acid, started before conception): folic acid taken before conception and during early pregnancy reduces the risk of neural tube defects, which form in the first weeks after conception. High-dose supplements of this vitamin are teratogenic and can cause birth defects, so they are avoided before and during pregnancy. Iron needs rise during pregnancy, so waiting until after delivery misses the period of greatest need. Newborns receive an injection at birth to prevent bleeding, so a maternal supplement before conception is not recommended for this purpose.

Question 298BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Stop the infusion right away, assess the client and notify the provider): flushing and itching during a vancomycin infusion usually reflect an infusion reaction linked to a rapid rate, so the nurse stops the infusion, checks for serious signs and reports, and it may be restarted at a slower rate once symptoms resolve if prescribed. Oral vancomycin is not absorbed into the bloodstream and cannot treat a systemic infection. Speeding up the infusion would worsen the reaction because it is caused by a rapid rate. This reaction is not a true allergy, and continuing without assessment and provider input could allow symptoms to progress.

Question 299ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Turn pot and pan handles toward the back of the stove while cooking): turning handles inward keeps a curious toddler from grabbing a pot and spilling hot contents. A water heater set to 120 degrees Fahrenheit or lower reduces the risk of scald burns, while water at 140 degrees can burn skin in seconds. Hot drinks are a common cause of scalds, so they are kept out of a toddler's reach. A toddler can pull a hanging tablecloth and bring hot food or liquids down onto themselves.

Question 300CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C ("Use a low setting, place a cloth between the pad and skin and limit the time."): a low setting, a protective layer and limited time prevent burns, which is especially important when neuropathy reduces the ability to sense heat. Direct skin contact concentrates heat and can cause burns before the client notices discomfort. Falling asleep on a heating pad allows prolonged exposure and is a common cause of serious burns. Starting on the highest setting is unsafe for a client with reduced sensation, who may not feel warmth until injury occurs.
