Question 901BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (New crackles in both lung bases with a serum potassium rising to 6.1 mEq/L): oliguria allows fluid and potassium to build up, and crackles with hyperkalemia signal fluid overload and a risk of fatal dysrhythmias. Dry, itchy skin is common in kidney dysfunction but does not threaten breathing or cardiac rhythm. Fatigue and a headache that resolved are nonspecific findings that do not need urgent intervention. A slowly rising creatinine is expected in acute kidney injury and is less urgent than pulmonary congestion with dangerous potassium levels.

Question 902ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Turn the client onto the side and move nearby objects away from the bed): side-lying lets secretions drain from the mouth to protect the airway, and clearing the area prevents injury from hard objects. Placing anything in the mouth during a seizure can break teeth, injure the client or the nurse, and obstruct the airway. Restraining the limbs during a seizure can cause fractures or soft tissue injury and does not stop the seizure. Giving fluids by mouth during a seizure creates a high risk of aspiration because the client cannot swallow safely.

Question 903AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Phenylketonuria screening collected as part of the newborn blood spot panel): phenylketonuria is detected through the newborn blood spot screen, and early dietary treatment prevents intellectual disability. Apgar scoring evaluates the newborn's immediate adaptation to birth and does not detect metabolic disease. The Ballard assessment estimates gestational age from physical and neuromuscular findings rather than screening for inherited disorders. Head circumference helps identify abnormal head growth but cannot detect an inborn error of metabolism.

Question 904BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Take metformin with your meals to help reduce stomach upset and nausea."): taking metformin with food lessens its common gastrointestinal effects, which usually improve over the first few weeks. Taking it on an empty stomach increases nausea and abdominal discomfort rather than preventing them. Metformin rarely causes hypoglycemia when used alone because it does not stimulate insulin release. Mild diarrhea is a common early effect, and the client should discuss any change with the provider rather than stopping the drug alone.

Question 905BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Temperature of 100.6 degrees F (38.1 degrees C) with a low neutrophil count): fever in a client with neutropenia may be the only sign of a life-threatening infection and requires urgent evaluation and antibiotics. A small weight loss with poor appetite is a common chemotherapy effect that is managed with nutritional support. Hair thinning is an expected adverse effect of many chemotherapy drugs and does not threaten the client's safety. Fatigue that improves with rest is expected during chemotherapy and can be addressed during routine care.

Question 906CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (A client who reports new chest pressure with sweating that started a few minutes ago): new chest pain with diaphoresis may signal acute coronary syndrome and needs immediate assessment to limit heart muscle damage. Perineal care for a catheterized client is routine and can be delegated or done after urgent needs are met. Morning stiffness is an expected, chronic finding in rheumatoid arthritis and is not an emergency. Discharge questions are important for safe transition home but can wait until unstable clients are assessed.

Question 907APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Adjust the infusion per the heparin protocol or notify the provider for new orders): a high aPTT means excessive anticoagulation, so the rate is reduced or held following the protocol or the provider's direction. Raising the rate would deepen anticoagulation and increase the risk of serious bleeding. Vitamin K reverses warfarin, while protamine sulfate is the reversal agent for heparin when one is needed. Leaving the rate unchanged allows the client to remain over-anticoagulated until the next draw.

Question 908CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Sitting upright at 90 degrees with the chin slightly tucked as the therapist instructed): an upright posture with a chin tuck narrows the airway entrance and helps food move safely into the esophagus. Tilting the head back opens the airway and makes it easier for food and liquid to enter the trachea. Lying flat while eating lets food pool in the throat and greatly increases the chance of aspiration. Lowering the head below the hips allows gastric contents and food to flow toward the airway.

Question 909CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Keep smoking, open flames, and gas burners well away from the oxygen equipment): oxygen strongly supports combustion, so smoking, flames, and heat sources near the equipment create a serious fire risk. Spare tanks should be secured in a stand or laid flat and kept away from heat sources, which can raise pressure and fire risk. Petroleum-based products are flammable around oxygen, so a water-based lubricant is used for nasal dryness. A concentrator needs open air around it for ventilation, and enclosing it in a closet can cause overheating.

Question 910CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Give 4.7 mL of the suspension, drawn up in an oral syringe): 22 lb divided by 2.2 equals 10 kg, 10 kg times 15 mg/kg equals 150 mg, and 150 mg times 5 mL per 160 mg equals 4.6875 mL, which rounds to 4.7 mL. A volume of 10.3 mL results from using the weight in pounds instead of kilograms, which would give more than double the safe dose. A volume of 9.4 mL is twice the correct amount and would expose the child to an overdose of acetaminophen. A volume of 2.3 mL is half the needed amount and would give too little medication to control the fever.

Question 911DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Tension pneumothorax with pressure on the heart and great vessels): air trapped in the pleural space pushes the mediastinum toward the unaffected side, reducing venous return and requiring emergency needle decompression. Asthma causes wheezing and bronchospasm but does not shift the trachea or eliminate breath sounds on one side. Heart failure causes bilateral crackles and congestion rather than a tracheal shift with one-sided absent breath sounds. Pneumonia causes localized crackles and fever, and it does not push the trachea toward the opposite side.

Question 912DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("If my ankles start to swell, I will stop taking the pill right away."): ankle edema is a common effect of amlodipine that should be reported, but stopping the drug abruptly can cause blood pressure to rise. Taking the medication at the same time daily keeps blood levels steady and shows correct understanding. Rising slowly helps prevent dizziness from orthostatic hypotension, which can occur with antihypertensive drugs. Reflex tachycardia or palpitations can occur with dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers and should be reported.

Question 913CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (A scented candle the family placed on the windowsill near the bed): an open flame near supplemental oxygen can ignite quickly and cause a serious fire, so the candle must be removed. A plastic water pitcher poses no fire risk and helps the client stay hydrated. A battery-operated clock does not create sparks and is safe to keep near oxygen. Cotton is the preferred fabric around oxygen because it is less likely than wool or synthetics to build static.

Question 914DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Integrity versus despair, reviewing life and finding meaning and acceptance in it): older adults review their lives, and a sense of peace and acceptance reflects successful achievement of ego integrity. Generativity versus stagnation is the task of middle adulthood, centered on productivity and caring for future generations. Intimacy versus isolation belongs to young adulthood, when people form close partnerships. Industry versus inferiority is the task of school-age children, who gain confidence through mastering skills.

Question 915BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Hold the next dose and notify the provider about the new ringing in the ears): tinnitus can signal aminoglycoside ototoxicity, which may become permanent, so the drug is held while drug levels and kidney function are reviewed. Continuing the dose and only documenting the symptom risks further damage to the inner ear. Ringing in the ears is a warning sign of toxicity rather than a harmless expected effect. Slowing the infusion does not prevent ototoxicity once symptoms appear, and the provider must decide whether to continue therapy.

Question 916CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Assess the client's pulse and check that the monitor leads are firmly attached): a client who is alert and talking has a perfusing rhythm, so a flat line most likely reflects a loose or disconnected lead. Calling a code for an alert, talking client uses emergency resources unnecessarily and delays finding the equipment problem. Chest compressions on a client with a pulse can cause injury, including rib fractures. Epinephrine treats true cardiac arrest, and giving it to a client with a pulse could cause dangerous tachycardia and hypertension.

Question 917APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (A sore throat with fever or chills that develops while taking the medication): methimazole can cause agranulocytosis, and sore throat with fever may be the first sign of a serious infection that needs a blood count. Feeling colder can occur as excess thyroid hormone falls and is not an emergency, though it is mentioned at the next visit. Modest weight gain is expected as the metabolic rate slows toward normal with treatment. A mild headache relieved by rest is not a recognized dangerous effect of antithyroid therapy.

Question 918CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Acetylcysteine to replenish glutathione and protect the liver from injury): acetylcysteine restores glutathione so the liver can neutralize the toxic acetaminophen metabolite, and it works best within 8 to 10 hours. Naloxone reverses opioid overdose and has no effect on acetaminophen toxicity. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines and would not prevent acetaminophen-induced liver damage. Atropine treats organophosphate or cholinergic poisoning and bradycardia rather than acetaminophen overdose.

Question 919APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Keep daily routines consistent and limit noise and bright lights in the room): predictable routines and reduced sensory input help children with autism feel safe and decrease anxiety and behavioral escalation. Frequent changes in caregivers disrupt predictability and usually increase distress for children with autism. Changing the environment each day removes familiar cues and can trigger anxiety and meltdowns. A busy, noisy location near the station increases sensory overload for a child who is sensitive to sound.

Question 920AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (A Pap test, an HPV test, or both together at the recommended intervals): for adults aged 30 to 65, cervical screening uses cytology every 3 years, high-risk HPV testing every 5 years, or both together every 5 years. Colonoscopy screens for colorectal cancer and does not evaluate the cervix. Low-dose chest scans screen for lung cancer in older adults with a heavy smoking history. Bone density testing screens for osteoporosis, mainly in older women, and does not detect cervical cancer.

Question 921BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B ("It may take several weeks to feel better, and I should not stop it suddenly."): selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors usually take 2 to 6 weeks for full effect, and stopping abruptly can cause discontinuation symptoms. Stopping once mood improves raises the risk of relapse, so the medication is continued as prescribed. Antidepressant effects build gradually, so expecting improvement overnight leads to disappointment and early discontinuation. Doubling a missed dose increases adverse effects, so the client takes the next dose as scheduled instead.

Question 922DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Cushing's triad, a late sign of severely increased intracranial pressure): a widening pulse pressure, bradycardia, and irregular respirations form Cushing's triad, which signals possible brain herniation and needs emergency treatment. Hypoglycemia typically causes tachycardia, sweating, and confusion rather than bradycardia with a widened pulse pressure. These vital sign changes reflect worsening brainstem compression, not improvement. Hypovolemia causes low blood pressure and tachycardia, which is the opposite of this pattern.

Question 923DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (The baby has about 6 or more wet diapers each day after the first week): six or more wet diapers a day after the first several days, along with steady weight gain, shows the newborn is getting enough milk. Newborns need to feed during the night, and sleeping through it at this age may mean the baby is not feeding enough. Breastfed newborns usually feed 8 to 12 times in 24 hours, so feeding every 6 hours is too infrequent. Prolonged crying after feedings can suggest the baby is still hungry rather than satisfied.

Question 924BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Install a sturdy handrail on the stairs and add bright lighting at both ends): a handrail provides support and good lighting improves visibility, which together reduce the risk of falls on stairs. Throw rugs slide and catch the feet, making them a common tripping hazard at the top of stairs. Dark stairs increase fall risk, so the stairway needs to be well lit when the client uses it at night. Carrying loads with both hands prevents the client from holding a rail or cane and blocks the view of the steps.

Question 925CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Stop the infusion and assess the site for infiltration or purple glove syndrome): phenytoin is highly alkaline and can cause severe tissue injury, so the infusion is stopped at once and the provider is notified. Slowing the rate still allows the irritating drug to enter damaged tissue and worsen the injury. Applying heat while the infusion continues allows ongoing tissue damage from the caustic medication. Elevation alone delays action and lets the drug keep infusing into the injured tissue.

Question 926AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Serum lactate level drawn with the initial blood cultures on admission): an elevated lactate reflects anaerobic metabolism from poor tissue perfusion and guides fluid resuscitation in sepsis. Hemoglobin A1C shows long-term glucose control and does not reflect current perfusion. A lipid panel assesses cardiovascular risk and has no role in the urgent evaluation of sepsis. Thyroid testing may be useful later but does not measure tissue perfusion during suspected septic shock.

Question 927AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Sudden dyspnea and oxygen saturation of 84% with normal blood pressure and no jugular distention): acute hypoxemia and respiratory distress within 6 hours of transfusion without signs of fluid overload point to this lung injury. Itching and a few hives suggest a mild allergic reaction rather than an acute lung injury. A mild decrease in heart rate within the normal range is not a sign of a pulmonary transfusion reaction. Mild warmth at the site without swelling needs monitoring but does not indicate a lung injury.

Matching stable, predictable clients to the LPN/VN keeps unstable and new clients with the RN, who has the broader scope of practice.

Question 931CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Helping Client 4 with eating breakfast and with morning bathing and toileting): assisting a medically stable client with meals and hygiene is routine care that falls within the UAP's role. Client teaching requires a licensed nurse because it depends on assessing the client's learning needs and understanding. Evaluating chest pain and deciding on medication involve assessment and clinical judgment that cannot be delegated to a UAP. Delegating routine personal care frees the RN to manage the clients who need professional nursing judgment.

Question 932BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Stop the meal now and keep the client upright until the RN assesses swallowing): coughing with eating and a wet voice suggest aspiration, so feeding stops and the client stays upright until the RN evaluates swallowing. Thin liquids are the hardest consistency to control and increase the risk of aspiration in a client with signs of dysphagia. Feeding faster gives the client less time to swallow safely and raises the chance of food entering the airway. The RN then assesses the airway and lung sounds and requests a swallowing evaluation before the next meal.

Question 933AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Chest pain is relieved, and vital signs remain stable while testing continues): relief of chest pain with stable vital signs shows that the client is responding to treatment while the cardiac workup continues. New pallor, diaphoresis, and nausea suggest worsening ischemia and need immediate RN action. Increasing chest pressure shows that ischemia is not controlled and requires prompt notification of the provider. Ongoing evaluation of pain and vital signs helps the RN recognize early whether the plan needs to change.

Question 934CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Verify the caller's identity and the client's permission before sharing any health information): privacy rules require the nurse to confirm the caller is authorized, often with a password or by asking the alert client for consent. Knowing the client's name and room number does not prove the caller is family or authorized to receive information. Even partial condition updates are protected health information and should not be shared until authorization is confirmed. The unit secretary also cannot release health information to an unverified caller, so transferring the call does not solve the problem.

Question 935DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D ("Are you having any thoughts of ending your life or hurting yourself?"): statements of hopelessness are a warning sign, so the nurse directly asks about suicidal thoughts to assess the client's immediate safety. Suggesting exercise offers advice before the nurse has assessed whether the client is at risk of self-harm. Telling the client to focus on the positive minimizes the client's feelings and can block further communication. Redirecting to a group activity changes the subject and misses the chance to assess suicide risk.

Question 936DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Assess the client to determine the cause of the new fatigue and activity change): a sudden drop in activity tolerance may signal a complication such as bleeding, infection, or a cardiac problem, which requires RN assessment. Having the UAP force the walk could harm the client if a physical problem is causing the fatigue. Documenting a refusal without assessment misses a possible change in the client's condition. Canceling all walks without knowing the cause raises the risk of postoperative complications from immobility.

Question 937DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Give 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate and recheck the glucose in 15 minutes): the rule of 15 quickly raises glucose in a client who can swallow, and treatment is repeated if the level stays below 70 mg/dL. Giving insulin while glucose is low would drive the level even lower and could cause seizures or unconsciousness. Fat and protein slow the absorption of glucose, so a peanut butter snack is not the first treatment for hypoglycemia. Delaying treatment allows hypoglycemia to worsen, while a snack or meal is given once the glucose has recovered.

Question 938BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Serotonin syndrome caused by combining two medications with serotonergic effects): tremor, hyperreflexia, sweating, agitation, and tachycardia after adding tramadol to an antidepressant suggest serotonin syndrome, so the drugs are held and the provider notified. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome follows antipsychotic use and causes severe muscle rigidity with slowed reflexes rather than hyperreflexia. The client is not taking lithium, so lithium toxicity does not explain these findings. Nothing in the history suggests heavy alcohol use, and the timing points to the new medication.

Question 939BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Stop the alteplase infusion and notify the provider of the change immediately): a sudden severe headache with rising blood pressure may signal intracranial hemorrhage, so the infusion is stopped and an emergency head scan is anticipated. Treating the headache while continuing the thrombolytic could allow bleeding in the brain to expand. Encouraging rest delays action and can hide a declining level of consciousness. Waiting to recheck the blood pressure delays stopping a drug that may be causing hemorrhage.

Question 940DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (An elevated count that suggests an infection or an inflammatory process): a white blood cell count above about 11,000/mm3 commonly reflects infection, inflammation, or stress, which fits the fever and abdominal pain. Bone marrow suppression lowers the white blood cell count rather than raising it. Iron deficiency affects red blood cells and hemoglobin rather than causing a high white blood cell count. Dehydration can mildly concentrate blood values but does not explain a count this high with fever and localized pain.

Question 941BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B ("Lie still with your arms relaxed at your sides and breathe normally."): muscle movement creates artifact, so lying still with relaxed arms and normal breathing gives a clear recording. Holding the breath for 2 minutes is unsafe and unnecessary, and the strain can distort the tracing. Moving the arms and legs causes muscle artifact that can mimic or hide dysrhythmias. Talking activates chest and facial muscles and can introduce artifact into the tracing.

Question 942AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

Tying shoelaces requires fine motor skills that most children develop around age 5 or 6. Reading chapter books is a school-age skill that develops well after the preschool years. Writing complete sentences requires literacy and fine motor control expected in early school years.

Question 943CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Question 955DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

Question 958APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Oxygen saturation of 88% on room air with crackles in both lung bases): low oxygen saturation with crackles shows that fluid in the alveoli is impairing gas exchange, which is the most immediate threat to life. The elevated blood pressure and heart rate need treatment, but they reflect compensation and are less urgent than hypoxemia. Leg edema confirms fluid retention, yet peripheral swelling does not threaten oxygenation the way pulmonary congestion does. The nurse therefore reports the oxygen saturation and lung findings first so oxygen and diuresis can begin promptly.

Question 959BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Fluid volume overload from worsening heart failure after missed diuretic doses): rapid weight gain, edema, crackles and orthopnea after stopping a diuretic and eating salty foods point to fluid overload from decompensated heart failure. An allergic reaction would cause wheezing, hives or facial swelling rather than gradual weight gain and dependent edema. Pneumonia usually brings fever and productive cough, while this afebrile client with weight gain and leg edema fits fluid overload. Recognizing fluid overload guides the nurse toward oxygenation support, diuresis and sodium restriction.

Question 960CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Impaired gas exchange related to fluid accumulation in the alveoli): fluid in the alveoli is reducing oxygenation, and airway and breathing problems take priority over all other needs. Constipation is only a potential problem and does not threaten the client's life right now. Teaching about sodium and medication adherence is essential to prevent readmission, but it is delivered once the client is breathing comfortably. Addressing gas exchange first follows the airway, breathing and circulation framework used to set priorities.

The correct answer is C (Intravenous furosemide to promote diuresis and reduce excess circulating fluid volume): a loop diuretic given intravenously removes excess fluid quickly, relieving pulmonary congestion and edema. A saline bolus would add volume and worsen pulmonary congestion in a client who is already overloaded. The serum potassium of 4.1 mEq/L is normal, so potassium replacement is not the first treatment, although levels will be monitored during diuresis. The nurse also anticipates monitoring intake, output and daily weights to evaluate the diuretic response.

Question 962APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Raise the client to a high Fowler's position and apply oxygen per protocol): sitting upright reduces venous return and lets the diaphragm expand, and supplemental oxygen corrects hypoxemia while other treatments begin. Walking increases oxygen demand and could worsen hypoxemia in a client who is already struggling to breathe. Lying flat increases venous return to the congested lungs and worsens orthopnea and shortness of breath. Positioning and oxygen are rapid, low-risk actions the nurse can take while awaiting further orders.

Question 963BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Oxygen saturation of 95% with fewer crackles and 1,200 mL of urine output): improved oxygenation, fewer crackles and a brisk urine output show that diuresis is removing excess fluid from the lungs. A rising respiratory rate with accessory muscle use signals worsening respiratory distress and needs immediate reporting. Further weight gain with unchanged edema shows that fluid is still accumulating and the treatment is not yet effective. The nurse continues to track weight, breath sounds and output to confirm sustained improvement.

Question 964DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D ("I need to share this with the treatment team so we can keep you safe."): confidentiality does not extend to information about risk of self-harm, so the nurse is honest that the team must be told to protect the client. Telling the client how to feel dismisses the distress and discourages further disclosure. Promising secrecy is unsafe and breaks trust later, because the nurse is obligated to share suicide risk with the team. Postponing the discussion delays a suicide risk assessment and leaves the client unprotected overnight.

Question 965BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B ("Showers are safer than baths, and someone should be nearby when you bathe."): a seizure in a tub can lead to drowning, so showering and having someone nearby reduce the risk of serious injury. Timing a medication dose before bathing does not prevent breakthrough seizures in a client whose seizures are only partially controlled. Even shallow water can cause drowning during a seizure, and a locked door delays rescue. Seizures can occur at any time of day, so changing the bath time does not make bathing alone safe.

Question 966DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D ("The bacteria spread by touching the wound, your mother or items in the room."): this organism spreads by direct and indirect contact, so contact precautions with gowns and gloves reduce transmission to others. Contact precautions primarily keep the organism from spreading to staff, visitors and other clients rather than protecting the infected client. This organism is not transmitted by the airborne route, which is why a mask and negative-pressure room are not required for a wound infection. Gowns and gloves are not required for every client, and calling it a general rule misinforms the daughter about the purpose of isolation.

Question 967CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Respiratory depression, monitored with sedation level, respiratory rate and oxygen saturation): respiratory depression is the most dangerous opioid adverse effect, and increasing sedation usually precedes it, so the nurse monitors both closely. Urinary retention can occur with opioids and is monitored, but it is not life-threatening the way respiratory depression is. Itching is a common opioid effect that is uncomfortable but does not threaten the airway or breathing. Constipation is expected with opioid use and needs a bowel regimen, yet it is a lower priority than monitoring breathing.

Question 968CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Place the call light within the client's reach and review how to use it): a call light within reach lets the client summon help before getting up alone, which reduces the risk of falls. Raising all four side rails can act as a restraint and increase injury if the client tries to climb over them. A client who has not needed help yet may need it at any moment, so the call light must be reachable. Calling out loudly is unreliable because staff may not hear the client, especially with the door closed.

Question 969DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (A turkey sandwich on white bread with canned peaches without the skin): refined white bread, tender meat and peeled canned fruit are low in fiber and leave little residue in the bowel. Raw vegetables and seeds are high in fiber and are restricted on a low-residue diet. Whole-grain bran cereal and berries with seeds add significant fiber and residue. Nuts, raw apples with skin and dried fruit are all high-fiber foods that the client should avoid for now.

Question 970DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (A possible early sign of increasing intracranial pressure that needs prompt reporting): a change in level of consciousness is the earliest and most sensitive sign of rising intracranial pressure, so the nurse reports it immediately. Attributing new confusion to the environment could delay recognition of a worsening brain injury. Restlessness and confusion reflect declining neurologic function, not improvement. Late signs include pupil changes and Cushing's triad of hypertension, bradycardia and irregular respirations, which this client does not show.

Question 971BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Having a second nurse independently verify the dose and pump settings per policy): heparin is a high-alert medication, and an independent double check of the calculation and pump programming catches errors before they reach the client. Starting the infusion promptly matters, but speed without verification increases the risk of a dangerous programming error. Verbal orders increase the chance of miscommunication and do not replace an independent check of a high-alert infusion. Preparing medications ahead for another shift creates labeling and identification risks and does not verify the current dose.

Question 972ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Inspect both feet daily, using a mirror, and wear well-fitting closed shoes): daily inspection finds injuries the client cannot feel because of neuropathy, and proper footwear protects the feet from trauma. Hot soaks can burn insensate feet and dry the skin, leading to cracks and infection. Walking barefoot exposes the feet to cuts and burns that the client may not notice. Cutting corns at home can cause wounds and infection, so a podiatrist should treat them.

Question 973DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Instructions for the evening insulin glargine and morning metformin before surgery): fasting changes insulin needs, so clear dosing instructions are needed to prevent hypoglycemia or severe hyperglycemia around surgery. Visitor arrangements follow unit policy and do not affect the client's physiologic safety. Preoperative bathing instructions are usually covered by standard protocols and pose far less risk than medication dosing errors. Postoperative pain orders can be confirmed later and are less urgent than preventing glucose emergencies during the fast.

Question 974DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Ask about her preferences and assign a female caregiver for personal care when possible): asking about preferences and providing a same-gender caregiver when possible respects the client's cultural values and dignity. Dismissing the client's concern as unimportant fails to honor her cultural values and damages trust. Waiting for family delays hygiene care and assumes the client wants relatives involved without asking her. Adding a female observer still exposes the client to a male caregiver and does not address what she prefers.

Question 975BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Serum lipase elevated to more than three times the upper limit of normal): pancreatic injury releases lipase into the blood, and lipase is more specific than amylase for acute pancreatitis. An elevated platelet count is not a diagnostic marker for pancreatitis. Serum amylase typically rises, not falls, early in acute pancreatitis. Albumin is more likely to decrease with inflammation and fluid shifts, so a high albumin does not support the diagnosis.

Question 976BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Apply firm direct pressure to the puncture site for at least five minutes): firm pressure for at least five minutes, and longer for clients on anticoagulants, prevents arterial bleeding and hematoma formation. Repeated hand movement increases arterial flow at the site and can promote bleeding. Massaging the site disrupts clot formation and can cause a hematoma. A bandage alone does not provide enough pressure to seal an arterial puncture.

Question 977APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Within about 15 minutes before the meal, or with the meal as ordered): rapid-acting insulin begins working within about 15 minutes, so giving it just before or with the meal matches its action to food absorption. Giving the dose an hour after eating allows postprandial hyperglycemia and mismatches the insulin peak. Mealtime insulin covers carbohydrates at each meal and is not limited to a single bedtime dose. Giving rapid-acting insulin 30 to 60 minutes early, as with regular insulin, risks hypoglycemia if the tray is late.

Question 978DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Checking for return of the gag and swallow reflexes): topical anesthetic suppresses the gag reflex, so fluids are withheld until it returns to prevent aspiration. Bowel sounds are not affected in a way that determines readiness to swallow after this procedure. A sore throat is common and should be assessed, but pain does not tell the nurse whether the client can swallow safely. Urine output does not reflect airway protection and is not the priority before oral intake.

Question 979DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("Using condoms correctly every time lowers the risk of many sexually transmitted infections."): consistent and correct condom use reduces the transmission of many sexually transmitted infections. Hormonal contraception prevents pregnancy but provides no protection against infection. Many sexually transmitted infections cause no visible symptoms, so a partner can be infected without signs. Adolescents and young adults account for a large share of new infections, and screening is recommended for sexually active teens.

Question 980BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Give the scheduled warfarin dose as ordered and continue routine bleeding precautions): an INR of 2.5 falls within the therapeutic range of 2 to 3, so the dose is given as prescribed. Vitamin K reverses warfarin and is used for an excessively high INR or bleeding, neither of which is present. Doubling the dose would push the INR above the target range and raise the risk of bleeding. Holding a dose when the INR is therapeutic would leave the client with atrial fibrillation at increased risk for stroke.

Question 981AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Explore the client's reasons for wanting to leave and notify the provider): understanding the reasons may reveal concerns that can be resolved, and the provider must be informed while the client's right to decide is respected. Discharging the client immediately bypasses the provider and loses the chance to address the concerns. Holding a competent adult against their will could constitute false imprisonment. Signing the form may be needed later, but the nurse first explores reasons and involves the provider so risks can be explained.

Question 982DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D ("It encourages slow, deep breaths that expand your lungs and help prevent atelectasis."): sustained deep inhalation inflates the alveoli, which helps prevent atelectasis and pneumonia after surgery. Medication mist is delivered by a nebulizer, not an incentive spirometer. Oxygen levels are measured with pulse oximetry or blood gases, not with the spirometer. The device targets lung expansion and is not designed to strengthen the arms or speed mobility.

Question 983CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Hourly incentive spirometer use while awake, along with early and frequent ambulation): deep breathing with the spirometer and early walking expand the lungs and mobilize secretions, which prevents alveolar collapse. Lying flat limits lung expansion and increases the risk of atelectasis. Coughing clears secretions, and the nurse should provide pain relief and incision splinting rather than discourage it. Supplemental oxygen treats low saturation but does not reopen collapsed alveoli or prevent atelectasis.

Question 984ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Labeling each specimen at the bedside in the client's presence after confirming two identifiers): labeling at the bedside immediately after collection, with two identifiers confirmed, links the specimen to the correct client. Labeling a group of specimens later away from the clients makes mix-ups much more likely. Pre-labeled containers can be used for the wrong client if the nurse picks up the wrong one. The unit clerk did not collect the specimens and cannot verify which client each one came from.

Question 985APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (After being criticized by his boss, a client goes home and yells at his wife): displacement shifts feelings from the true source to a safer target, such as directing anger at a spouse instead of the boss. Insisting there is no drinking problem despite clear evidence illustrates denial. Attributing one's own resentment to the coworker is an example of projection. Being unable to remember a traumatic event reflects repression, which blocks painful memories from awareness.

Question 986BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Lisinopril, an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor taken once daily for heart failure): combining sacubitril-valsartan with an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor greatly increases the risk of angioedema, so a 36-hour washout is required. Low-dose aspirin can be continued and does not require a washout period. Loop diuretics are commonly continued with sacubitril-valsartan, although the dose may be adjusted. Statins have no washout requirement and can be taken with sacubitril-valsartan.

Question 987DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Completing schoolwork and playing board games with other children in the playroom): school-age children are in the stage of industry versus inferiority, and schoolwork and games with peers build a sense of competence. Strict bed rest with no activity is not needed for this mobile child and promotes boredom and regression. Infant toys do not match the developmental level of a 6-year-old. Watching television alone isolates the child from peers and does not build skills or competence.

Question 988CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Nonblanchable redness over the sacrum on skin that remains intact): nonblanchable redness on intact skin is a stage 1 pressure injury, showing that tissue damage has already started. Heels that blanch normally indicate adequate blood flow and are an expected finding. Poor intake is an important risk factor to address, but it does not show existing tissue damage the way the sacral finding does. Recognizing the stage 1 injury early allows the team to relieve pressure before the skin opens.

Question 989BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (High risk for pressure injury that requires a comprehensive prevention plan): lower Braden scores indicate greater risk, and a score of 10 to 12 places the client at high risk. Mild risk corresponds to a score of 15 to 18, so routine inspection alone would underestimate this client's needs. Moderate risk corresponds to a score of 13 to 14, and turning every four hours is too infrequent for this client. A high-risk score calls for frequent repositioning, a pressure-redistribution surface, moisture management and nutrition support.

Question 990ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Urinary incontinence that leaves the skin over the sacrum repeatedly wet): repeated exposure to urine softens and weakens the skin, making it far more vulnerable to breakdown from pressure and friction. Hip surgery reduces mobility but is not itself a source of moisture on the skin. Drowsiness lowers sensory perception and activity, which increases pressure risk rather than moisture-related damage. Prompt incontinence care and barrier products directly address this moisture risk.

Question 991ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Turning on a set schedule, a pressure-redistribution mattress and prompt incontinence care): scheduled repositioning, a support surface and keeping the skin clean and dry relieve pressure and reduce moisture damage. Massaging reddened areas can damage fragile tissue and is not recommended for pressure injury prevention. Raising the head of the bed to 60 degrees increases shear and pressure on the sacrum. The head of the bed is generally kept at 30 degrees or lower when the client's condition allows.

Question 992CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (A registered dietitian to assess poor intake and recommend protein and calorie support): adequate protein, calories and fluids are essential for tissue repair, and a dietitian can tailor supplements and food choices. An audiology evaluation is not indicated because the client's hearing is normal for age and does not affect wound healing. Voice therapy does not address nutrition or skin integrity, and the client's speech is normal. Improving nutrition supports both healing of the stage 1 injury and prevention of new injuries.

Question 993BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (The sacral redness has faded, and the skin over the sacrum remains intact): fading redness with intact skin shows that pressure and moisture relief allowed the tissue to recover. A shallow open area with a pink wound bed indicates progression to a stage 2 pressure injury. Declining intake and further weight loss show that nutrition needs are still unmet. These worsening findings would require the team to revise the plan rather than continue it unchanged.

Question 994DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (The client will demonstrate correct blood glucose self-monitoring technique by discharge): a correct goal is client-centered, measurable and time-limited, and demonstrating a skill by discharge can be directly observed. The word understand describes an internal state that cannot be measured directly, and the goal lacks a time frame. Feeling more positive is not measurable as written and has no target date. A goal describes the client's behavior, so a statement about what the nurse will do is an intervention.

Question 995CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Ibuprofen 400 mg taken by mouth three times daily for joint pain): nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs irritate the stomach lining, and taking them with food or milk reduces gastrointestinal upset. Levothyroxine is taken on an empty stomach, usually 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast, for consistent absorption. Omeprazole works best when taken 30 to 60 minutes before a meal. Alendronate is taken on an empty stomach with a full glass of plain water, and the client remains upright for at least 30 minutes.

Question 996APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Offer food in sealed packages and acknowledge the fear without agreeing with the belief): sealed packages meet nutritional needs while respecting the client's fear, and acknowledging feelings builds trust without reinforcing the delusion. Detailed explanations amount to arguing with a fixed false belief, which is ineffective and can increase suspicion. Trying to prove the food is safe challenges the delusion and may be incorporated into it rather than resolving it. Insisting on eating the full tray creates a power struggle and may further reduce intake.

Question 997CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C ("Do you have documented immunity to varicella through vaccination or prior disease?"): only staff with evidence of immunity to varicella should care for a client with active chickenpox, which protects both the nurse and other clients. Influenza vaccination is important for all health care workers but does not protect against varicella. Tetanus status matters for wound exposure but is unrelated to caring for a child with chickenpox. Latex allergy affects glove selection for any assignment and is not specific to the risk posed by varicella.

Question 998CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C ("I keep cleaning products in their original containers inside a locked cabinet."): original labeled containers stored in a locked cabinet keep toxic products out of a curious toddler's reach and easy to identify. Toddlers often explore purses, so medicines kept there are easily reached. The poison control number should be posted at home and saved in the parent's phone, not just kept in the car. Putting medicine in a beverage bottle invites a child to drink it and removes important label information.

Question 999BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Follow the infusion protocol, which may call for adjusting the rate and adding dextrose): protocols guide rate adjustments and dextrose addition so insulin can continue clearing ketones without causing hypoglycemia. An insulin bolus would drive the glucose lower and cause hypoglycemia. Increasing the rate without adding dextrose would cause dangerous hypoglycemia as glucose is already falling quickly. Stopping the infusion while the anion gap is still elevated allows ketoacidosis to worsen, and basal insulin is started at least 2 hours before the infusion ends once DKA resolves.

Question 1000CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (A set of large, soft plastic stacking rings too big to fit in the mouth): large, soft toys that cannot fit in the mouth are safe for infants who explore objects orally. Marbles are small enough to be swallowed or inhaled and are a serious choking hazard. Latex balloons, especially when broken or uninflated, are a leading cause of choking deaths in young children. Small removable pieces can be put in the mouth and cause airway obstruction.
