Question 501APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Stay with the client and coach slow, deep breathing using a calm, quiet voice): remaining with the client provides safety and reassurance, and coaching slow breathing lowers anxiety and corrects hyperventilation. Exploring the cause of the panic requires problem-solving that the client cannot do until the anxiety level decreases. Leaving a client alone during severe anxiety increases fear and removes needed support and supervision. Rebreathing into a paper bag is no longer recommended because it can cause hypoxia and may worsen distress.

Question 502AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (A social worker or case manager to explore medication assistance programs): a social worker or case manager can connect the client with medication assistance programs and community resources, which addresses the cost barrier and supports adherence. A home health aide helps with personal care but cannot resolve the client's inability to pay for medications. Dietary teaching about sodium is useful in heart failure but does not address the financial concern the client raised. A physical therapy evaluation supports mobility and safety but does nothing to make the new medications affordable.

Question 503CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Check the client's blood glucose and assess for signs of hypoglycemia): client safety comes first, and insulin given to a client without diabetes can cause dangerous hypoglycemia, so the nurse assesses the client before any other step. An incident report is required but is completed after the client is stabilized and the provider has been notified. The nurse manager is informed of the error, but reporting does not take priority over assessing a client who may be hypoglycemic. Factual documentation of the dose and the client's response is necessary but follows assessment and treatment of the client.

Question 504APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A ("Take the capsule 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast each morning."): proton pump inhibitors work best when taken before the first meal of the day, when the stomach's acid pumps are activated by food. Taking the capsule with a bedtime snack reduces its effectiveness because the drug is meant to be taken before a meal. Taking an antacid at the same time is not needed for the drug to work and does not improve its acid suppression. The delayed-release capsule should be swallowed whole, because chewing it destroys the coating that protects the drug from stomach acid.

Question 505BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (It lets the whole team confirm the correct client, procedure, and site): the time-out is the final verification step of the Universal Protocol, in which every team member stops and agrees on identity, procedure, and site to prevent wrong-site surgery. Fasting status is checked during preoperative verification, and the time-out is a shared team activity rather than a private check by one provider. Reviewing imaging may happen before the case, but it is not the purpose of the time-out. Instrument counts are done at set points to prevent retained items, which is a separate safety process from the time-out.

Question 506BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (During the midafternoon, several hours after the morning dose is given): NPH insulin peaks about 4 to 12 hours after injection, so a 0730 dose has its greatest effect in the afternoon, and the nurse makes sure meals and snacks are available then. By midnight, more than 16 hours have passed and the effect of the morning NPH dose is waning. The next morning is near the end of the dose's duration of action, so peak hypoglycemia risk has passed. NPH has an onset of about 1 to 2 hours, so the first hour after injection is too early for its peak effect.

Question 507CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Assess the site regularly and keep the dressing clean, dry, and intact): regular assessment allows early detection of complications, and a clean, dry, intact dressing protects the insertion site from contamination. Current guidelines do not recommend routine replacement of peripheral catheters every 24 hours, because extra insertions add risk without benefit. Topical antibiotic ointment is not recommended for peripheral sites because it can promote resistant organisms and fungal infection. Changing an intact transparent dressing every shift increases manipulation of the site, and the site can be inspected through the clear film.

Question 508DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Intimacy versus isolation, focused on forming deep, lasting bonds with others): young adults work on forming intimate, committed relationships, and fear of rejection reflects the risk of isolation in this stage. Generativity is the task of middle adulthood and centers on productivity and guiding younger people. Developing a sense of self is the main task of adolescence rather than young adulthood. Mastering skills and gaining competence is the task of the school-age child.

Question 509DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Ibuprofen 400 mg orally three times daily for joint pain): nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs inhibit protective prostaglandins in the stomach lining and can cause ulcers and bleeding, especially in older adults with long-term use. Loratadine is a nonsedating antihistamine that does not damage the gastric mucosa. Docusate is a stool softener that does not increase the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding. Acetaminophen at this dose is gentle on the stomach, although the nurse still monitors the total daily amount for liver safety.

Question 510BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Relieve distressing symptoms and improve quality of life for the client and family): palliative care focuses on symptom relief, comfort, and quality of life for the client and family at any stage of serious illness. Palliative care can be given along with disease-directed treatments, so stopping current therapy is not required. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease cannot be cured, and pursuing cure at any cost does not match the goals of palliative care. Family involvement is a core part of palliative care, and restricting visits would reduce support for the client.

Question 511APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A ("You seem overwhelmed by this news; what worries you most right now?"): acknowledging the client's feelings and inviting the client to share concerns is therapeutic and opens communication. Information about treatment is important, but teaching before addressing emotions is premature and the client is not yet ready to learn. Pointing out that many others have HIV minimizes the client's personal distress and closes the conversation. Telling the client not to think that way offers false reassurance and dismisses the client's feelings.

Question 512BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Get up slowly from lying or sitting, because the medicine can cause dizziness."): tamsulosin is an alpha blocker that can cause orthostatic hypotension, especially with early doses, nighttime trips to the bathroom, and other blood pressure medications. Symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia return if the medication is stopped, so it is continued as prescribed. Alpha blockers lower blood pressure rather than raise it, which is why dizziness is a concern. Tamsulosin is taken about 30 minutes after the same meal each day rather than on an empty stomach.

Question 513CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Elevated blood pressure and thrombotic events such as a clotted dialysis access): epoetin increases red blood cell production, which raises blood viscosity and can cause hypertension and clots, especially when hemoglobin rises above target. Epoetin does not affect blood glucose, so hypoglycemia is not an expected adverse effect. Ototoxicity is linked to drugs such as aminoglycosides and loop diuretics rather than epoetin. Hair loss is associated with chemotherapy and is not a typical effect of epoetin therapy.

Question 514CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Offer a back rub, dim the lights, and provide calm reassurance): comfort measures and a quiet, calm environment reduce anxiety and promote sleep without medication risks. Television provides light and stimulation that can delay sleep rather than promote it. Coffee and many teas contain caffeine, a stimulant that interferes with sleep. Sedative-hypnotics increase confusion and fall risk in older adults, so nonpharmacologic measures are tried first.

Question 515BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Droplet precautions, including a surgical mask when entering the infant's room): pertussis spreads through large respiratory droplets, so droplet precautions with a surgical mask are used along with standard precautions. Contact precautions alone do not protect against droplets produced during coughing. Airborne precautions are reserved for diseases such as tuberculosis, measles, and varicella that spread by small airborne particles. Protective isolation guards immunocompromised clients from infection and does not prevent the infant from spreading pertussis.

Question 516BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (A client with stable COPD who needs scheduled oral medications and routine care): a stable client with predictable needs and routine medications falls within the practical nurse's scope where policy allows. A new admission with new-onset breathing difficulty needs initial assessment and clinical judgment by the RN. Initial teaching about new equipment and treatment is the responsibility of the RN. A client in the immediate postoperative period is unstable and needs frequent RN assessment.

Question 517ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Verify the client's identity and the blood unit with another qualified nurse at the bedside): two qualified licensed staff members check the client's identification band and the blood product together at the bedside to prevent the wrong blood from reaching the client. Comparing paperwork away from the client does not confirm that the unit matches the person receiving it. Assistive personnel are not qualified to perform the verification of blood products. Room signs and charts are not acceptable identifiers, because the client's identification band must be checked directly.

Question 518DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Follow the employee health policy, which typically means staying home and reporting the illness): health care workers with fever and a respiratory infection can spread illness to vulnerable clients and coworkers, so employee health policy guides when it is safe to return. An antipyretic masks the fever without reducing contagiousness, and a mask does not make working with a fever safe. Newborns are highly vulnerable to respiratory infections, so caring for them while febrile places them at serious risk. Working on the unit still exposes coworkers and clients, and a febrile illness should be reported to employee health.

Question 519BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (The Moro reflex, a startle response with symmetric extension of the arms): the Moro, or startle, reflex is a primitive reflex that fades by about 4 to 6 months as the nervous system matures. The gag reflex is a protective reflex that persists throughout life. The cough reflex protects the airway and remains present for life. The blink reflex protects the eyes and continues into adulthood.

Question 520DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (40 drops per minute, counted carefully over one full minute): 1,200 mL divided by 600 minutes equals 2 mL per minute, and 2 mL per minute multiplied by 20 drops per mL equals 40 drops per minute. The hourly rate is 120 mL per hour, but setting 120 drops per minute confuses milliliters per hour with drops per minute. Using the drop factor alone ignores the volume and time, which are needed to calculate the rate. A rate of 33 drops per minute results from a calculation error and would underinfuse the prescribed volume.

Question 521BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (This morning the scale read 3 pounds more than it did yesterday): a 3-pound gain in one day exceeds the provider's threshold and may indicate fluid retention that requires a change in treatment. A 1-pound loss does not suggest fluid retention and does not meet the reporting threshold. A stable weight over several days indicates that fluid balance is being maintained. A 1-pound gain over a week is below the threshold for a single day and does not require a call.

Question 522DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Moist ground turkey with gravy and well-cooked, soft diced carrots): a mechanical soft diet includes moist, ground, or finely chopped foods that need little chewing. Raw vegetables and crunchy croutons are hard to chew and are not part of a mechanical soft diet. Steak is tough to chew and whole nuts are hard, so both are excluded from this diet. A toasted bagel, crisp bacon, and a whole raw apple all require significant chewing.

Question 523DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (An 85-year-old with reduced kidney function who takes eight daily medications): advanced age, reduced kidney clearance, and polypharmacy together greatly increase the risk of drug accumulation, adverse effects, and interactions. A healthy young adult taking a single antihistamine has a low risk of interactions. A middle-aged adult with normal kidney function taking only a multivitamin has minimal risk. Occasional ibuprofen use deserves teaching, but a young athlete on one drug is at much lower risk than an older adult on many.

Question 524CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Subarachnoid hemorrhage from a ruptured cerebral aneurysm needing emergency evaluation): a sudden, severe headache described as the worst ever, with vomiting, neck stiffness, and photophobia, suggests subarachnoid hemorrhage and requires emergency imaging. Tension headaches develop gradually with a band-like ache and do not cause sudden severe pain with neck stiffness. Sinus infections cause facial pressure and congestion rather than an abrupt, explosive headache. Dehydration may cause a mild headache, but it does not explain sudden severe pain with meningeal signs.

Question 525DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Epinephrine 0.5 mg intramuscularly into the anterolateral thigh): intramuscular epinephrine is first-line treatment for anaphylaxis because it reverses bronchospasm, vasodilation, and airway swelling. Antihistamines are adjuncts that relieve hives but do not treat hypotension or airway swelling. Corticosteroids act too slowly to treat acute anaphylaxis and are given only as adjunct therapy. Albuterol may relieve wheezing but does not correct hypotension or laryngeal swelling.

Question 526ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Rub all surfaces of the hands and fingers until they feel dry): the product must cover all surfaces of the hands and fingers and is rubbed until dry, which usually takes about 20 seconds. Two minutes is longer than needed and is not the standard for alcohol-based hand rub. Rubbing for only a few seconds and wiping off the product prevents the alcohol from acting long enough. Covering only the palms misses the fingertips, thumbs, and backs of the hands where organisms remain.

Question 527CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Identity versus role confusion, developing a stable sense of who one is): adolescents try out roles, styles, and peer groups as they develop a sense of identity, which is expected at this age. Trust versus mistrust is the task of infancy. Industry versus inferiority belongs to the school-age years. Generativity versus stagnation is the task of middle adulthood.

Question 528AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The client is alert, understands the risks, and has clearly stated his decision): a competent adult who understands the risks has the right to make decisions about treatment, including refusing surgery. The client's age does not change his legal and ethical right to accept or refuse treatment. The daughter's concern deserves support, but her disagreement does not override the decision of a competent adult. Recognizing that the client has decision-making capacity is the key cue that shapes the rest of the team's response.

Question 529BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Autonomy, the right of a capable adult to make his own choices): autonomy is the right of a competent person to make informed decisions about his own care, including refusal of treatment. Justice concerns fair allocation of care and is not the principle at issue in an individual refusal. Beneficence underlies the surgeon's recommendation, but it does not permit the team to override a competent client's choice. Honoring an informed refusal therefore rests most directly on respect for the client's self-determination.

Question 530CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Ensure the client understands risks and alternatives, then support his informed choice): the nurse acts as an advocate by confirming that the decision is informed and by respecting the choice the client makes. Joining the daughter in persuading the client adds to the pressure he described and undermines his autonomy. Urging consent based on the team's preference is coercive and does not produce truly informed consent. Advocacy means making sure the client's voice is heard while he receives accurate information about his options.

Question 531BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Ask the surgeon to review risks and alternatives again and document the client's refusal): the client should be fully informed by the surgeon, and his informed refusal is documented while other appropriate treatment continues. Refusing one treatment does not mean refusing all care, so stopping antibiotics and wound care would abandon the client. Keeping the surgery scheduled after a clear refusal disregards his decision and can feel like pressure to comply. This plan respects the client's choice, keeps him informed, and protects the team with accurate documentation.

Question 532AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A ("I see how worried you are; your father has the right to make this decision."): this response validates the daughter's feelings while clearly upholding the competent client's right to decide. Telling the daughter the decision does not involve her is dismissive and can escalate the conflict. Promising that the surgeon will convince the client violates his autonomy and gives the daughter false expectations. Therapeutic communication with family members supports them emotionally without undermining the client's choice.

Question 533CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (His informed refusal is documented, and antibiotics and wound care are continuing): ethical care respects the informed refusal while continuing other appropriate treatment for the infection. Consent obtained through family pressure is not freely given and does not reflect respect for autonomy. Stopping all care after a refusal of surgery abandons the client and is not ethical practice. Respecting a refusal and continuing other needed care together show that the client's rights and well-being were both protected.

Question 534CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (It lasts from full cervical dilation until the birth of the baby): the second stage of labor begins at full dilation of 10 cm and ends with birth of the baby. The first 1 to 2 hours after delivery of the placenta make up the fourth stage, or recovery period. The time from regular contractions to full dilation is the first stage of labor. The period from birth of the baby to delivery of the placenta is the third stage.

Question 535CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Confirm the lift and sling weight limits and use the staff number policy requires): following weight limits and staffing requirements prevents equipment failure and injury to both the client and staff. A frayed sling can tear under load, so damaged equipment is removed from use. Most policies require at least two staff members to operate a full-body lift safely. Moving the lift quickly increases swinging and fall risk, and calm explanation is a better way to ease anxiety.

Question 536BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (The renal system, because rising values suggest declining kidney function): rising BUN and creatinine with falling urine output indicate declining kidney function, possibly from dehydration or vancomycin nephrotoxicity. Liver function is evaluated with enzymes, bilirubin, and albumin rather than BUN and creatinine. Thyroid function is measured with thyroid-stimulating hormone and thyroxine levels, which these results do not reflect. Bone health is assessed with calcium, phosphorus, and bone density studies rather than these kidney markers.

Question 537DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (A client with a decreased level of consciousness receiving continuous tube feedings): a decreased level of consciousness impairs protective airway reflexes, and tube feedings add the risk of regurgitating formula into the lungs. An alert client who eats independently has intact protective reflexes and low aspiration risk. A wrist fracture affects the arm but does not impair swallowing or airway protection. An ambulatory, alert client who has finished eating is at low risk for aspiration.

Question 538DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Do regular strength and balance exercises and remove tripping hazards at home): strength and balance exercise and a hazard-free home are proven strategies that reduce falls in older adults. Darkness increases fall risk during nighttime bathroom trips, so night-lights are recommended instead. Socks on smooth floors are slippery, and sturdy, nonskid footwear is safer. Reducing activity leads to weakness and deconditioning, which raises the risk of falls.

Question 539DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A client with chronic back pain who is resting comfortably after oral analgesia): a client with a chronic, controlled problem who is resting comfortably is stable and can safely be seen after clients with urgent needs. New-onset confusion may signal hypoxia or sepsis and requires prompt assessment. A blood glucose of 48 mg/dL is hypoglycemia that needs immediate treatment. A chest tube that stops draining with new breathing difficulty may indicate an obstruction or tension pneumothorax and is an emergency.

Question 540CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Assess the location, quality, and characteristics of the client's pain): a complete pain assessment guides the choice of treatment and helps identify complications such as bleeding that need a different response. Documenting the score and delaying care leaves severe pain untreated and could miss a complication. Morphine is likely appropriate, but giving it before assessing could mask signs of a surgical complication. Repositioning and breathing techniques are useful adjuncts but are not enough alone for severe postoperative pain.

Question 541DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("It helps prevent asthma symptoms and is not meant for sudden attacks."): montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist used as a daily controller medication, and it does not relieve acute bronchospasm. The albuterol rescue inhaler is still needed to treat sudden symptoms. Montelukast has a slow onset and does not open the airways during an acute attack. The drug carries a boxed warning for neuropsychiatric effects, so mood changes, sleep problems, or suicidal thoughts must be reported.

Question 542AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Check that the gag reflex has returned before offering anything by mouth): the throat is numbed during bronchoscopy, so the client could aspirate until the gag and swallow reflexes return. A warm drink taken before the gag reflex returns could be aspirated and could burn the numbed throat. Ice chips melt into fluid that can be aspirated, so they are withheld until the gag reflex returns. Small sips of water and upright positioning do not protect an airway that lacks a gag reflex.

Question 543CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (A generalized tonic-clonic seizure lasting about 1 minute with rhythmic jerking): withdrawal seizures typically occur within 6 to 48 hours of the last drink and require immediate safety measures, airway protection, and treatment. A mild hand tremor is an expected early withdrawal sign that is monitored and scored on the withdrawal scale. Mild anxiety is common during withdrawal and is managed according to the scale score and protocol. Insomnia is a frequent, less urgent symptom of withdrawal that does not require immediate intervention.

Question 544ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Fried foods, such as french fries and fried chicken): greasy, high-fat foods stimulate bowel activity and are poorly tolerated, so they can worsen diarrhea. Bananas are bland and help replace potassium lost through diarrhea. Clear broth helps replace fluid and sodium and is easy to tolerate. Plain white rice is bland, low in fiber, and easy to digest during recovery.

Question 545CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (A stoma that appears dark purple to black and dry): a dark purple or black stoma indicates poor blood supply and possible necrosis, which requires prompt surgical evaluation. A pink to red, moist stoma shows healthy blood flow. Mild stomal edema is expected after surgery and gradually decreases over several weeks. The bowel normally secretes some mucus, so a small amount on the stoma is expected.

Question 546DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Notify the provider and avoid nose blowing or suctioning through the nose): clear nasal drainage after head trauma may be cerebrospinal fluid, and actions that raise pressure or introduce organisms are avoided to prevent meningitis. Blowing the nose increases intracranial pressure and can push bacteria through the dural tear. Packing the nose blocks the flow of fluid and raises the risk of infection. Nasal suctioning can pass through the fracture into the brain and introduce infection.

Question 547APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Adrenal crisis caused by abrupt withdrawal of long-term corticosteroid therapy): long-term corticosteroids suppress the adrenal glands, so stopping them suddenly can cause adrenal crisis with weakness, vomiting, and shock that requires emergency intravenous hydrocortisone and fluids. Thyroid storm presents with fever, tachycardia, and agitation rather than following steroid withdrawal. Mild gastroenteritis does not explain profound hypotension after abrupt steroid withdrawal. Low blood glucose may accompany adrenal crisis, but it does not account for the shock and the history of stopping prednisone.

Question 550BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

A mask on a coughing client contains respiratory secretions, so removing it during coughing increases spread. Question 551CManagement of Care

Rearranging furniture removes familiar landmarks and increases the risk of falls for a client who relies on memory of the layout. The client is competent and should be addressed directly, so routing questions through the daughter undermines the client's dignity and autonomy. Question 552BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Discuss the specific safety concerns with the charge nurse before accepting the assignment): concerns about an unsafe assignment are raised first through the chain of command, starting with the charge nurse, so the assignment can be adjusted before care begins. Accepting the assignment and reporting only after the shift leaves clients exposed to unsafe care for the entire shift. The state board of nursing is not the first step, because the issue should be addressed within the facility's chain of command. An informal arrangement with a coworker does not change who is accountable and bypasses the charge nurse who manages assignments.

Question 553BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (New confusion along with a flapping tremor of the outstretched hands): a high ammonia level points to hepatic encephalopathy, which causes confusion and asterixis, the flapping tremor of the hands. Cirrhosis tends to cause loss of body hair from hormonal changes, so increased hair growth is not an expected finding. Rising ammonia worsens thinking and alertness, so improving memory would not be expected. Cirrhosis more often causes low blood pressure from vasodilation, so hypertension with a slow pulse is not the finding to watch for.

Question 554BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Inspect the system for an air leak from the insertion site to the drainage unit): continuous bubbling in the water seal signals an air leak, so the nurse checks the insertion site and every connection and notifies the provider if the leak is not in the tubing. Emptying the water seal chamber breaks the seal and lets air enter the pleural space without addressing the leak. Vigorous stripping creates high negative pressure that can damage lung tissue and is not a routine intervention. Clamping for a prolonged time can trap air and cause a tension pneumothorax unless the provider specifically orders it.

Question 555DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("I will keep the cord clean and dry and fold the diaper below it."): keeping the stump clean and dry with the diaper folded below it promotes drying and lets the cord fall off naturally. Question 556APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Taking the whole daily amount at bedtime leaves the client weak at mealtimes and is not how the drug is scheduled. Taking each dose after meals means the drug peaks too late to help with chewing and swallowing. The medication must be taken on a regular schedule, because waiting for severe weakness risks a myasthenic crisis.

Question 557BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Flight of ideas, with rapid speech shifting between loosely connected topics): flight of ideas is rapid, continuous speech that jumps between topics with loose but traceable connections, a classic finding in mania. Word salad is a meaningless mix of words with no connection, whereas this client's ideas can still be followed. Echolalia is the repetition of another person's words, which this client is not doing. Clang associations link words by rhyme or sound, while this client's shifts are based on meaning.

Question 561APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Supplemental oxygen, inhaled short-acting bronchodilators and systemic corticosteroids): oxygen corrects hypoxemia, short-acting bronchodilators relieve bronchospasm and systemic corticosteroids reduce airway inflammation. Oral fluids and an oral antihistamine do not open the airways and would delay effective treatment. Lying flat pushes abdominal contents against the diaphragm and makes breathing harder. Trendelenburg position further restricts diaphragm movement and is not indicated for respiratory distress. Positioning is a quick nursing action that supports breathing while medications take effect.

Question 564ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Reorient the client, explain the purpose of the oxygen and check on the client often): less restrictive measures such as reorientation, simple explanation and frequent checks are tried before chemical or physical restraints. Sedation is a chemical restraint that can worsen confusion and depress breathing in a client with pneumonia. Stopping the oxygen while the client is awake leaves the client hypoxemic during the day. Restraints are a last resort after less restrictive interventions have failed and require a provider order.

Question 565DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (The infant does not respond to sounds or turn toward the parents' voices): lack of response to sounds at 6 months may signal hearing loss or another developmental concern and warrants further evaluation. Babbling with consonant sounds and laughing are expected language and social milestones at this age. Reaching for objects and transferring them between hands are expected fine motor skills around 6 months. Rolling in both directions is an expected gross motor milestone for a 6-month-old.

Question 566DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Hold the next dose and notify the provider so a lithium level can be checked): vomiting, diarrhea and a coarse tremor suggest lithium toxicity, so the dose is held and a serum level is obtained promptly. Lowering sodium intake raises lithium levels and would make toxicity worse. Taking the next dose with food adds more lithium when the client already shows signs of toxicity. A coarse tremor with gastrointestinal symptoms is a warning sign, not an expected side effect, and needs prompt evaluation.

Question 567CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Notify the provider about options to relieve the itching and continue to monitor): itching without rash or breathing changes is a common opioid side effect, and the provider can adjust the plan while the nurse watches for signs of a true allergic reaction. Question 569CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Allow the spouse to stay and provide a recliner or cot if available): policy permits one overnight visitor and the client wants the spouse present, and family presence supports comfort and recovery. Question 570DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Respect the client's request and include the family in discussions as the client wishes): a competent client may choose a family-centered decision style, and honoring that choice is culturally sensitive care while the client keeps the right to consent. Question 571CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Keep the head of the bed elevated 30 to 45 degrees unless contraindicated): elevating the head of the bed reduces aspiration of oral and gastric secretions, a main cause of ventilator-associated pneumonia. Frequent circuit changes do not lower pneumonia rates and may increase contamination, so tubing is changed only when soiled or malfunctioning. Question 572APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Use an infusion pump and follow the facility's maximum hourly infusion rate): intravenous potassium must be diluted and delivered by pump at a controlled rate to prevent dangerous cardiac effects. Intravenous push delivers a concentrated bolus that can cause fatal dysrhythmias or cardiac arrest. A rapid gravity infusion risks hyperkalemia and dysrhythmias, so the rate must be controlled by a pump. Adding concentrated potassium to a premixed bag risks an incorrect and dangerously high concentration.

Question 573AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (It helps prevent eye infection from bacteria the newborn may acquire during birth): erythromycin ointment is given to prevent neonatal conjunctivitis from organisms such as gonorrhea acquired in the birth canal. Jaundice results from bilirubin levels in the blood and is not prevented by eye ointment. The ointment has no effect on visual development and may briefly blur the newborn's vision. The ointment is an antibiotic prophylaxis and is not used to treat dry eyes.

Question 574DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Insertion of a nasogastric tube for decompression and intravenous fluids as ordered): nasogastric decompression relieves distension and vomiting, and intravenous fluids replace fluid and electrolyte losses while the bowel rests. Laxatives increase pressure behind a mechanical blockage and can lead to perforation. The client is kept on nothing by mouth, so a high-fiber diet and oral fluids would worsen distension and vomiting. Question 575DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (Stay with the client, speak calmly and help orient the client to the present): staying with the client and using calm grounding statements help the client feel safe and reconnect with the present. Medication may be considered later if ordered, but it is not the first step for a frightened client who needs reassurance. Bright lights and noise can overstimulate a client who is already hyperaroused. Dismissing the experience as only a dream minimizes the client's fear and leaves the client unsupported.

Question 576APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A ("Keep your salt and fluid intake about the same from day to day."): changes in sodium and fluid intake alter lithium levels, so steady intake keeps the level stable and prevents toxicity. Alcohol can worsen mood symptoms and dehydration, so it is discouraged rather than recommended.

Question 577BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Question 578BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Report the observations to the charge nurse or nurse manager immediately): suspected impairment puts clients at risk, so it must be reported through the chain of command before the colleague assumes care. Public confrontation is unprofessional, may escalate the situation and does not remove the colleague from client care. Waiting for an error exposes clients to harm that reporting now would prevent. Question 579APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Post-traumatic stress disorder, given flashbacks and avoidance lasting over a month): flashbacks, avoidance and hyperarousal lasting more than a month after a traumatic event are consistent with post-traumatic stress disorder. Bipolar disorder involves episodes of mania and depression that are not tied to a specific trauma. Flashbacks are re-experiencing of a real past event, unlike the hallucinations and delusions of schizophrenia. Generalized anxiety disorder involves broad, ongoing worry rather than trauma-linked flashbacks and avoidance.

Question 580AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A ("What makes you ask that, and what have you been feeling lately?"): an open-ended response invites the client to share fears and concerns and supports honest, therapeutic communication. Telling the client not to think about it shuts down the conversation the client is trying to start. False reassurance is dishonest and discourages the client from expressing feelings. Question 581BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (It helps more of the medication reach the lungs instead of the mouth): a spacer reduces the need for perfect timing and delivers more medication to the lungs, which also lowers the risk of oral thrush from inhaled corticosteroids. Spacers benefit clients of all ages, not only children who have trouble with coordination. The spacer attaches to the inhaler and does not replace it, because the inhaler still releases the medication. A spacer does not change the strength of the medication in each puff.

Question 582APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (To prevent Wernicke encephalopathy caused by thiamine deficiency from heavy drinking): thiamine deficiency is common with heavy alcohol use and can cause Wernicke encephalopathy, which giving thiamine helps prevent. Causing unpleasant reactions with alcohol describes disulfiram, not thiamine. Thiamine does not lower blood pressure, which is managed with other treatments during withdrawal. Reducing cravings is the role of drugs such as naltrexone or acamprosate, not thiamine.

Question 583AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Eat a diet rich in fruits, vegetables and whole grains and stay active): a heart-healthy eating pattern and regular physical activity are core recommendations for reducing cardiovascular risk. Processed meats and saturated fat raise cardiovascular risk and are not part of a heart-healthy diet. Avoiding activity worsens risk, and most adults can begin moderate exercise such as walking without a stress test. Aspirin is not routinely recommended for primary prevention, and supplements do not replace diet and activity changes.
