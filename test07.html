Question 601BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (A newly hired surgical technician whose blood test shows no hepatitis B immunity): health care workers who may be exposed to blood and body fluids should be vaccinated when testing shows they are not immune. An employee with documented antibody after a full series is already protected and does not need more doses. A young child who completed the infant series on schedule is considered protected and needs no further doses. An employee who already has chronic infection gains no benefit from the vaccine and instead needs ongoing medical follow-up.

Question 602DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D ("Take the hearing aid out before showering and keep it dry."): moisture damages the electronic parts of a hearing aid, so it is removed for bathing and kept dry. Rinsing the device under running water exposes the electronics to moisture, so it is wiped with a soft dry cloth instead. A car in hot weather can reach temperatures that damage the battery and casing. Wearing the device in the shower soaks it with water and can ruin it.

Question 603BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Change positions slowly, and report dizziness or a very slow pulse."): carvedilol blocks beta and alpha receptors, which can lower blood pressure and heart rate and cause dizziness or bradycardia. Beta blockers slow the heart rate rather than speeding it up. The drug is taken every day on a schedule to provide long-term benefit in heart failure, not as a rescue medication. Fatigue is common early and often improves, and stopping a beta blocker suddenly can worsen heart failure or cause rebound tachycardia.

Question 604CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("Take it on an empty stomach at the same time each morning."): levothyroxine is absorbed best on an empty stomach, usually 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast, and a consistent time keeps hormone levels steady. Calcium binds levothyroxine and reduces absorption, so the two are separated by about 4 hours. Food and coffee also decrease absorption, so taking the dose with breakfast makes the effect unreliable. Hypothyroidism usually requires lifelong replacement, so the medication is continued even after symptoms improve.

Question 605CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C ("I need to avoid aged cheese, cured meats and tap beer."): phenelzine is a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, and tyramine-rich foods such as aged cheese, cured meats and tap beer can trigger a hypertensive crisis. Limiting potassium-rich produce is not part of the restrictions for this drug and does not prevent a tyramine reaction. Grapefruit juice affects some other medications, and focusing on it alone leaves the client unprotected from tyramine. Keeping leafy green intake consistent is teaching for warfarin, not for a monoamine oxidase inhibitor.

Question 606BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Provide balanced information about each option so the client can make an informed choice): autonomy is the client's right to make decisions about care, and the nurse supports it by giving clear information and respecting the choice. Steering the client toward surgery to satisfy the son places the family's wishes above the client's own. Recommending the option the nurse prefers is paternalistic and replaces the client's judgment with the nurse's. A client with decision-making capacity keeps the right to decide, so handing the decision to the son violates autonomy.

Question 607APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Gradual exposure to elevator situations in steps while practicing relaxation techniques): systematic desensitization pairs relaxation with a step-by-step hierarchy of exposures, starting with the least frightening situation. Intense, prolonged exposure all at once describes flooding, which is a different behavioral technique. Medication may be used for some anxiety disorders, but it is not the basis of systematic desensitization. Searching past memories for the cause of the fear reflects psychodynamic therapy rather than this behavioral approach.

Question 608BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("I will take every dose as prescribed unless my provider tells me otherwise."): antibiotics work best and resistance is reduced when the client follows the prescribed course and checks with the provider before changing it. Stopping when symptoms fade can leave infection behind and encourages resistant bacteria. Saving leftover pills for a later illness leads to incomplete treatment and self-treatment without evaluation. Sharing antibiotics is unsafe because the other person may have a different illness, allergies or drug interactions.

Question 609DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Enteral-specific connectors, such as ENFit, that cannot attach to IV ports): enteral-specific connectors are designed so they physically cannot connect to intravenous ports, which prevents feeding or oral medication from entering the bloodstream. Luer-lock syringes that fit both systems are exactly what allows a dangerous misconnection to happen. Labels can help, but they rely on attention and do not physically block a wrong connection, so the nurse still traces each line from the client to the source. Taping junctions secures connections but does nothing to stop the wrong lines from being joined.

Question 610CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C ("I walk most days, meet friends weekly and keep my screening appointments."): regular physical activity, social connection and preventive care all support function and well-being in older adults. Staying home and limiting activity promotes deconditioning, isolation and a higher risk of falls. Stopping blood pressure medication without the provider's guidance raises the risk of stroke and heart disease. Eating a single large meal can lead to poor nutrition and muscle loss in older adults.

Question 611CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Within the first 15 minutes after the transfusion of the unit begins): acute hemolytic reactions usually appear early, often after only a small volume, so the nurse stays with the client and infuses slowly during this time. Severe hemolytic reactions rarely begin near the end of the unit, so the early period needs the closest watch. Waiting until after the transfusion to expect a severe reaction would miss the period of greatest risk. Reactions one to two weeks later are delayed hemolytic reactions, which are usually milder than acute ones.

Question 612DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Offer the prescribed analgesic and comfort measures shortly before the client's bedtime): treating pain before sleep and adding comfort measures such as positioning and ice help the client fall and stay asleep. Evening therapy and dressing changes increase pain and stimulation right before sleep. Tea and cola contain caffeine, which interferes with falling asleep. Bright light at night disrupts the sleep cycle, and a call light within reach is a better safety measure.

Question 613BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Intravenous isotonic fluids given before and after the contrast procedure): intravenous hydration with isotonic fluid is the main measure used to reduce the risk of contrast-associated kidney injury in high-risk clients. Restricting fluids concentrates the contrast in the kidneys and raises the risk of injury. Clients with kidney disease are prone to high potassium, and extra potassium does nothing to protect the kidneys. An extra diuretic dose can cause volume depletion, which increases the risk of kidney injury.

Question 614BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Ask the colleague to remove the post and report it according to facility policy): a rare diagnosis combined with a bed number can identify a client, so the post is a privacy breach that must be removed and reported. Identifiable details other than a name can still reveal who the client is, so leaving the post up continues the breach. Commenting on the post draws more attention to it and does not remove the protected information. Delaying action until the family complains allows the breach to continue and places the client's privacy at risk.

Question 615DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (The ventrogluteal site, located using the greater trochanter and iliac crest): the ventrogluteal site has thick gluteal muscle, holds larger volumes and is free of major nerves and vessels. The deltoid is a small muscle that is best for volumes of about 1 mL, such as vaccines. The dorsogluteal site lies near the sciatic nerve and major vessels, so it is no longer recommended. The abdomen is used for subcutaneous injections, not for intramuscular medications.

Question 616DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Let the child hold the stethoscope and listen to a stuffed animal's heart): preschoolers learn through play and concrete experience, so handling the equipment reduces fear and supports their sense of initiative. Having the parent leave removes the child's main source of security and increases fear. Detailed medical terms are beyond a preschooler's understanding, so simple words and demonstration work better. Rushing through the exam without preparation increases fear and reduces the child's cooperation.

Question 617BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Notify the surgeon that the client may not be able to give valid consent now): opioids can impair the capacity to understand and agree to a procedure, and the surgeon is responsible for obtaining consent and deciding how to proceed. A signature obtained while the client is sedated may not be valid informed consent. A spouse cannot sign for a client who is expected to regain capacity before an elective procedure. Reading the form aloud does not restore the client's capacity, so witnessing that signature would not make the consent valid.

Question 618DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D ("Avoid raising your left arm above your shoulder for as long as your provider advises."): limiting arm movement on the insertion side for the first weeks helps prevent lead displacement while the leads become secure. Cell phones are kept at least 6 inches from the device, so the pocket over the pacemaker is the wrong place. Household microwave ovens in good working order are generally safe for clients with pacemakers. Regular device checks, including remote monitoring, are needed to check battery life and function even without symptoms.

Question 619CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Air movement has become severely limited, signaling impending respiratory failure): a silent chest with exhaustion and drowsiness means too little air is moving to create wheezes, so the nurse calls for emergency help and prepares for airway support. Improvement would bring easier breathing and better alertness, not drowsiness and single-word speech. Drowsiness here reflects rising carbon dioxide and fatigue, not comfortable rest. Clearing mucus would not explain exhaustion and a decreasing level of consciousness.

Question 620DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Fatigue and shortness of breath when climbing stairs or walking quickly): low hemoglobin reduces oxygen delivery to tissues, causing fatigue, weakness and shortness of breath with exertion. Thirst and frequent urination suggest high blood glucose rather than anemia. Anemia lowers energy, so increased energy with less need for sleep does not fit this finding. Weight gain with ankle swelling points toward fluid retention, such as heart or kidney problems, not anemia alone.

Question 621ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Dress the client in light clothing and encourage frequent oral fluid intake): light clothing allows heat loss, and fluids replace losses from sweating and rapid breathing. Limiting fluids worsens the dehydration that fever already causes. A warmer room reduces heat loss and keeps the temperature elevated. Heavy blankets trap heat and can raise the body temperature further.

Question 622DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Maintain a safe distance near the exit, stay calm and call for help): the nurse's first priority is personal safety, so keeping distance, keeping an exit clear and getting help come before any other action. Threatening consequences can escalate anger and does not protect the nurse. Physical contact by one person alone puts both the nurse and the client at risk of injury. Closing the door traps the nurse with an aggressive client and blocks the exit.

Question 623BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Follow facility policy for arranging the client's access to the medical record): clients have a legal right to access their health information, and facilities have procedures for reviewing it safely. Altering or removing information from the record is illegal and denies the client full access. The facility owns the physical record, but the client has a right to the information in it. The original chart must stay in the facility, so the client receives access or copies through the proper process.

Question 624CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Rifampin, a rifamycin antibiotic used to treat tuberculosis infection): rifampin commonly turns body fluids reddish-orange and can permanently stain soft contact lenses, so clients are warned in advance. Isoniazid does not discolor body fluids, and its main concerns are liver toxicity and peripheral neuropathy. Amoxicillin may cause diarrhea or rash but does not change the color of urine or tears. Furosemide increases urine output but does not turn body fluids reddish-orange.

Question 625DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Droplet precautions, with staff wearing a surgical mask on entering the room): Neisseria meningitidis spreads through respiratory droplets, and droplet precautions continue until the client has received 24 hours of effective antibiotics. Airborne precautions are needed for organisms such as tuberculosis, measles and varicella, not meningococcus. Gown and gloves alone do not protect staff from droplets produced by coughing or suctioning. Meningococcal disease does spread from person to person, so standard precautions alone are not enough.

Question 626APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (200 mL/h, because 100 mL divided by 0.5 hour equals 200): the rate equals volume divided by time, and 100 mL divided by 0.5 hour equals 200 mL/h. Setting the rate equal to the bag volume would deliver the dose over 1 hour instead of 30 minutes. Multiplying the volume by the time gives 50 mL/h, which would take 2 hours to infuse. A rate of 300 mL/h would infuse the dose in 20 minutes, faster than ordered.

Question 627AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Dehydration and electrolyte imbalance from large losses of fluid and sodium): ileostomy output bypasses the colon, where water and sodium are normally absorbed, so high output quickly causes fluid and electrolyte loss. Replacement fluids are given to match losses, and with this much output the greater danger is deficit, not overload. Ileostomy output is liquid, so hard stool and constipation are not expected. High output leads to weight loss from fluid depletion rather than weight gain.

A long list from several prescribers does not show that conditions are well controlled and calls for review rather than reassurance. Stopping medications prescribed elsewhere without review could harm the client, so reconciliation with the providers is the safe step. The client's vague description of the routine also suggests a need for medication teaching.

Question 630CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (The client will return home safely with support services in place): this goal respects the wishes of an oriented client while reducing the identified risks through support at home. Placing a client with decision-making capacity in a nursing home against their wishes violates autonomy. Speeding up discharge before support is arranged increases the risk of falls and readmission. The nurse can share the daughter's concerns with the team while keeping the client's goal at the center of the plan.

Question 631CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Home health nursing, a pharmacist medication review and a home safety evaluation): these services directly address medication management, fall hazards such as throw rugs and dim stairs, and ongoing monitoring at home. Urology, nutrition and hearing services may have value later but do not target the main risks of falls and medication errors. Hospice is for clients with a terminal illness, which does not describe this client. Matching referrals to the identified risks gives the client the best chance of staying home safely.

Question 632BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Review each medication and warning sign, then have the client explain them back): teach-back confirms that the client understands each medication and warning sign, and identifying pills by color is unreliable when brands change. Printed instructions without discussion do not confirm understanding, especially for nine medications. The daughter is leaving after the weekend, so the client must be able to manage the medications. Including the daughter in the session is helpful, but the client remains the main learner.

Question 633AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The client explains how to take each medication and has home health visits scheduled): accurate teach-back and services in place show that the client can manage safely at home. Recognizing pills by color in a shoebox shows that the medication safety risk has not been addressed. Staying in bed leads to weakness and deconditioning, which increases fall risk over time. Successful planning keeps the client active and independent while reducing risk.

Question 634AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (A client asking for information about a local diabetes support group): information about a support group is important for long-term coping but is not time-sensitive. New confusion with fever may signal sepsis or another serious infection and needs prompt assessment. Severe chest pain may indicate acute coronary syndrome and requires immediate evaluation. Symptomatic hypoglycemia needs treatment right away to prevent loss of consciousness or seizures.

Question 635APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Dry mouth and constipation, which can be eased with fluids and fiber): oxybutynin is anticholinergic, so it commonly causes dry mouth, constipation and blurred vision, and older adults are at risk for confusion. Anticholinergic drugs reduce saliva, so excess saliva is the opposite of the expected effect. These drugs slow bowel motility, which causes constipation rather than loose stools. The drug reduces bladder spasms and urgency, and it can cause urinary retention rather than increased output.

Question 636CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C ("My child will ride in a belt-positioning booster until the seat belt fits properly."): a booster seat is used until the adult belt fits correctly, usually at about 4 feet 9 inches tall and between 8 and 12 years of age. Age alone does not determine readiness, since the belt must fit across the upper thighs and chest. Children younger than 13 years ride in the back seat, even when using a booster. A lap belt alone does not restrain the upper body and can cause serious abdominal and spinal injuries.

Question 637BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (The actual time given, the client's assessment and any provider notification required by policy): the medical record contains objective facts about care, including when the drug was actually given and how the client responded. Comments about staffing are subjective and belong in internal reports, not in the client's record. Incident reports are internal quality documents and are not mentioned in the medical record. Charting the scheduled time instead of the actual time falsifies the record.

Question 638BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Yellow discoloration of the skin and sclera noted at 18 hours of age): jaundice within the first 24 hours of life is not physiologic and needs evaluation for hemolysis or other causes. Small white papules on the nose and chin are milia, which are normal and resolve without treatment. Acrocyanosis of the hands and feet is common in the first day of life. A resting heart rate of 140 falls within the normal newborn range of about 110 to 160.

Question 639BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Repetitive lip smacking and involuntary tongue thrusting movements): tardive dyskinesia causes involuntary movements of the mouth, face and tongue after long-term antipsychotic use, and it may be permanent. Fever, rigidity and unstable blood pressure suggest neuroleptic malignant syndrome, a medical emergency. Inner restlessness and an urge to move describe akathisia. A resting tremor with shuffling gait reflects drug-induced parkinsonism.

Question 640DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D ("Increase fluids, fiber and walking, and we will ask about a bowel regimen."): opioids slow bowel motility, and fluids, fiber, activity and a prescribed stool softener or laxative help prevent and treat constipation. Tolerance does not develop to opioid constipation, so it usually persists without treatment. Stopping pain medication leaves pain uncontrolled and limits the walking that helps bowel function. Limiting water makes stool harder and worsens constipation.

Question 641APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (8 mL, because 400 mg divided by 250 mg, times 5 mL, equals 8): the dose desired divided by the dose on hand, times the volume, gives 400 divided by 250 times 5, which equals 8 mL. Inverting the ratio of supply to order underdoses the client with only about 3.1 mL. A teaspoon of this suspension contains only 250 mg, which is less than the ordered dose. Using a concentration of 125 mg per 5 mL instead of the labeled 250 mg doubles the dose to 16 mL.

Question 642BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B ("Take it first thing with plain water, then stay upright for 30 minutes."): taking alendronate on an empty stomach with a full glass of plain water and staying upright for at least 30 minutes improves absorption and prevents esophageal irritation. Food sharply reduces absorption, so the dose is taken before breakfast. Milk and calcium bind the drug and prevent it from being absorbed. Lying down allows the tablet to irritate the esophagus and can cause ulceration.

Question 643DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("I will season my food freely with a potassium salt substitute."): angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors can raise potassium, and many salt substitutes contain potassium, so this statement needs correction. Rising slowly is appropriate because the drug can cause dizziness from low blood pressure, especially early in therapy. A persistent dry cough is a common side effect that should be reported. Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat may be angioedema, which is an emergency.

Question 644CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Provide privacy and support and explain evidence collection, proceeding only with consent): trauma-informed care offers privacy, emotional support and control over each step, and explaining evidence collection lets the client make an informed choice about showering. Showering and changing clothes before the examination can destroy evidence. Asking why the client was at the location implies blame and can retraumatize the client. Leaving the client alone removes support at a time of acute distress.

Question 645AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Follow a clear liquid diet and take the bowel preparation the day before): clear liquids and the prescribed bowel preparation empty the colon so the provider can see the lining clearly. A large high-fiber meal leaves residue in the colon and interferes with the view. Dairy products are not clear liquids and leave residue that hides the lining. The colon must be emptied before the test, because the procedure itself does not clean it.

Question 646DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (A client on bed rest who takes oxycodone and drinks little fluid): immobility, opioids and low fluid intake all slow bowel motility and harden stool. A client already on a stool softener and stimulant laxative has measures in place to prevent constipation. Gastroenteritis causes diarrhea rather than constipation. Daily walking and a high-fiber diet promote regular bowel movements.

Question 647DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Measuring and recording a client's oral intake and urine output each shift): measuring intake and output is a routine, standardized task with predictable results for a stable client. Assessing a client after a fall requires nursing judgment and stays with the RN. Teaching a new skill requires the knowledge and judgment of a licensed nurse. Evaluating the response to a medication is part of the nursing process and cannot be delegated.

Question 648CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C ("I must avoid alcohol in all forms, including cough syrups and mouthwash."): disulfiram causes a severe reaction with any alcohol, including hidden sources such as mouthwash, some liquid medicines and topical products. Disulfiram works by causing an unpleasant reaction to alcohol, not by reducing cravings. Even a small amount of alcohol can cause flushing, vomiting, chest pain and low blood pressure. The client must be free of alcohol for at least 12 hours before the first dose.

Question 649AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The state nurse practice act and the clinic's written policies and procedures): the nurse practice act defines the legal scope of practice, and facility policy describes what is permitted in that setting. A coworker's experience elsewhere is not an authoritative source for legal scope. Internet searches may be inaccurate or apply to other states and do not define legal scope. The provider's request does not expand the nurse's legal scope, which is set by the state.

Question 650CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Human papillomavirus vaccine, given as a two-dose series at this age): human papillomavirus vaccination is routinely recommended at ages 11 to 12, along with the Tdap and meningococcal vaccines. Recombinant zoster vaccine is recommended for adults aged 50 and older and for some immunocompromised adults. Rotavirus vaccine is given only in infancy and is not used after 8 months of age. A child who completed the infant series on schedule is already protected and does not need the series restarted.

Question 651ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (A cup of smooth vanilla pudding served chilled from the dessert cart): a full liquid diet includes foods that are liquid or melt to liquid at room temperature, such as milk, smooth pudding, ice cream and strained cream soups. Rice is a solid food that requires chewing, so it belongs on a soft or regular diet rather than a full liquid diet. Toast is a solid bread product, even when cut into small pieces, and is not permitted until the diet is advanced further. Scrambled eggs are soft but still solid, so they fit a mechanical soft diet rather than a full liquid diet.

Question 652CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Notify the provider because insulin is usually held until potassium reaches at least 3.5): insulin shifts potassium into the cells, and current consensus guidance is to replace potassium before starting insulin when the level is below 3.5 mEq/L. Intravenous fluids remain essential for correcting dehydration in ketoacidosis and should continue as ordered while potassium is replaced. Starting the insulin infusion with a potassium of 3.2 mEq/L could cause life-threatening hypokalemia and dysrhythmias. An insulin bolus drives potassium into the cells even faster, so it carries the same danger as starting the infusion.

Question 653BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Clamp the tubing briefly, then aspirate urine from the sampling port using aseptic technique): the sampling port provides fresh urine without opening the closed drainage system, and cleaning the port and using sterile equipment keeps the specimen uncontaminated. Disconnecting the catheter from the tubing breaks the closed system and increases the risk of introducing bacteria. Urine that has collected in the bag is not fresh, and bacteria multiply there, so culture results would be inaccurate. Removing the catheter requires a provider order and is not needed to obtain a valid culture specimen.

Question 654DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Call for help, begin chest compressions and defibrillate as soon as the device arrives): a pulseless client in ventricular fibrillation is in cardiac arrest and needs high-quality compressions and defibrillation as quickly as possible. Aspirin is given for acute coronary syndrome in a conscious client and has no role in a pulseless arrest. A 12-lead electrocardiogram delays lifesaving treatment and is not needed once pulselessness is confirmed. The nurse has already confirmed the client is unresponsive and pulseless, so checking leads would only delay compressions and defibrillation.

Question 655CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (A client whose chest tube has separated from the drainage system tubing): a disconnected chest tube can allow air into the pleural space, so the nurse follows protocol, such as placing the tube end in sterile water, and notifies the provider. Gentle tidal fluctuation in the water-seal chamber is an expected finding that shows the system is patent. A small amount of serous drainage is expected after thoracic surgery and does not require urgent action. Questions about the purpose of the tube are an important teaching need but can be addressed after the unsafe situation is managed.

Question 656AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A ("Know how your breasts normally look and feel, and report any changes promptly."): current guidance emphasizes breast self-awareness, which means knowing what is normal and promptly reporting changes such as lumps, skin changes or nipple discharge. A normal mammogram does not rule out cancers that develop between screenings, so the client should still notice and report changes. Many breast cancers are painless, so a change should be reported whether or not it hurts. Daily self-examination is not recommended and adds no benefit over general awareness of what is normal.

Question 657DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Ask the client to empty the bladder just before the procedure begins): an empty bladder lowers the risk of accidental bladder puncture when the needle is inserted into the abdomen. Drinking extra water would fill the bladder and increase the risk of puncture during the procedure. The client is positioned upright or in a semi-Fowler position so fluid pools in the lower abdomen, not lying prone. There is no routine need to hold all medications, and the provider decides about specific drugs such as anticoagulants.

Question 658APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Sudden right-sided weakness with facial droop and slurred, hesitant speech): a sudden focal neurological deficit, such as one-sided weakness, facial droop and speech difficulty, is the hallmark presentation of stroke. A glucose of 118 mg/dL is checked to rule out hypoglycemia as a stroke mimic, but this normal value does not point to stroke itself. Taking metformin and lisinopril reflects risk factors for stroke but is not a finding of an acute stroke. The neurologist will use these focal deficits to guide urgent imaging and treatment decisions.

Question 659CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (It determines whether the client is eligible for time-sensitive clot-dissolving or clot-removal treatment): eligibility for thrombolytic therapy and mechanical thrombectomy depends on the time since the client was last known well. Rehabilitation needs are based on the deficits that remain after treatment, not on the exact time of onset. The timing of the blood pressure elevation is not the reason the onset time is documented so carefully. Accurate timing allows the team to act quickly within the treatment window and avoid giving therapy when risks outweigh benefits.

Question 660BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Obtaining an emergent noncontrast head CT scan to rule out intracranial bleeding): brain imaging must distinguish ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke because thrombolytics are contraindicated when bleeding is present. A physical therapy evaluation is useful later in recovery but does not affect the urgent treatment decision. Before thrombolysis, blood pressure is lowered only to below 185/110 mm Hg, and rapid lowering to normal could reduce blood flow to the injured brain. Imaging is completed as quickly as possible so that eligible clients can receive treatment within the time window.

Question 661APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Keep the client NPO until a bedside swallow screen has been completed): clients with stroke often have dysphagia, so nothing is given by mouth until a swallow screen shows it is safe. Even small sips of thin liquid can be aspirated by a client who coughs while swallowing saliva. Crushed medications in applesauce still require a safe swallow and could be aspirated before screening. The nurse can offer mouth care for thirst and ask the provider about other routes for essential medications.

Question 662BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Frequent neurological checks and blood pressure readings to detect signs of bleeding): bleeding, especially intracranial hemorrhage, is the main risk of alteplase, and blood pressure must stay within protocol targets. Glucose is monitored in stroke care, but hourly checks are not the priority for detecting complications of thrombolysis. Daily weights and intake and output are routine measures that will not detect the most dangerous complication of this therapy. Neurological checks and blood pressure readings are repeated often for the first 24 hours so any deterioration is found early.

Question 663CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (A sudden severe headache with new drowsiness and difficulty staying awake): a sudden severe headache with a decreasing level of consciousness may signal intracranial hemorrhage, so the infusion is stopped and the provider notified. A blood pressure of 164/88 mm Hg is within the target range after thrombolysis and is trending in the right direction. Clearer speech suggests improving blood flow to the brain and is a hoped-for response to treatment. After stopping the infusion, the nurse anticipates emergent head imaging and laboratory studies to evaluate bleeding.

Question 664APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Ask the client directly about suicidal thoughts, plans and access to means): a sudden lift in mood combined with giving away possessions may mean the client has decided on a suicide plan, so risk must be assessed immediately. Starting discharge planning would remove protective supervision at a time when suicide risk may be highest. Documenting improvement misreads warning signs and delays the safety assessment the client needs. Praising the giving away of belongings reinforces a possible warning behavior instead of exploring its meaning.

Question 665DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (Push-pull toys and large stacking blocks the child can move and build): toddlers learn through active, hands-on play that builds gross and fine motor skills, such as pushing toys and stacking blocks. Screen-based games with timed levels are not recommended for this age and do not build motor skills. A puzzle with hundreds of small pieces is far beyond a toddler's fine motor and cognitive abilities and poses a choking hazard. Games with rules and scoring suit school-age children, who can follow rules and take turns.

Question 666APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A ("If my chest pain is not better five minutes after one tablet, I'll call 911."): current guidance is to call emergency services if chest pain is unimproved or worse five minutes after the first dose. The tablet is placed under the tongue to dissolve, because swallowing it greatly reduces its effect. Nitroglycerin is stored in its original dark glass container, since light, heat and moisture degrade the tablets. Combining nitroglycerin with erectile dysfunction drugs such as sildenafil can cause severe, life-threatening hypotension.

Question 667CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (27 mL per hour, which delivers 1,350 units of heparin each hour): the dose is 18 units multiplied by 75 kg, or 1,350 units per hour, and the concentration is 25,000 units divided by 500 mL, or 50 units per mL, so 1,350 divided by 50 equals 27 mL per hour. Setting the pump to 18 mL per hour confuses the weight-based dose with the infusion rate and delivers only 900 units per hour. A rate of 54 mL per hour uses half the true concentration and would deliver double the ordered dose. A rate of 13.5 mL per hour delivers only half the ordered dose and would leave the client undertreated.

Question 668CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Keeping the leg straight lowers the risk of bleeding or hematoma at the puncture site): flexing the hip can disrupt the clot forming at the femoral artery puncture, so the leg is kept straight for the ordered time. Contrast dye does not cause calf cramps, and preventing cramps is not the purpose of this restriction. Contrast is cleared by the kidneys through hydration, and leg position does not change how quickly it is excreted. Cardiac monitor leads are placed on the chest and are not affected by the position of the leg.

Question 669AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (An irregular pulse with frequent premature ventricular beats on the cardiac monitor): severe hypokalemia can cause dangerous ventricular dysrhythmias, which are the most life-threatening effect of a low potassium level. Constipation from slowed bowel motility is common with hypokalemia but is not immediately dangerous. Fatigue is an expected symptom of low potassium and heart failure but does not threaten life as dysrhythmias do. Mild leg weakness reflects muscle effects of hypokalemia and needs attention, but cardiac rhythm changes take priority.

Question 670ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Limiting time near the client and following the facility's radiation safety instructions): time, distance and shielding limit radiation exposure, so the nurse organizes care to minimize time at the bedside and follows the radiation safety plan. Pregnant visitors are usually restricted because the fetus is especially sensitive to radiation exposure. Saliva and other secretions may be radioactive, so disposable utensils are used and items are handled as directed by radiation safety. Body fluids may contain radioactive iodine and must be handled according to the facility's radiation precautions.

Question 671APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Stop the transfusion, keep the line open with saline and notify the provider): any suspected transfusion reaction requires stopping the transfusion first, and the provider then decides whether an antihistamine and restarting are appropriate. Slowing the rate continues exposure to the blood product before the reaction has been evaluated. Acetaminophen treats fever rather than hives and continuing the transfusion could allow the reaction to worsen. A cool compress may ease itching, but finishing the unit before evaluation risks progression to a more severe reaction.

Question 672CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Ask the client to state full name and date of birth, then compare with the wristband): two identifiers stated by the client and compared with the wristband and record are the safest way to confirm identity. A family member is not a reliable substitute when the client is alert and able to respond. A room or bed number is not an acceptable identifier because clients are moved between rooms. A yes-or-no question can be answered incorrectly by a client who is distracted, hard of hearing or confused.

Question 673ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Inspecting the skin under the tubing, collar and splint at regular intervals each shift): devices create pressure on the skin, and regular inspection with padding and repositioning allows early detection and prevention of injury. Waiting until discharge to check the skin allows injuries to develop and progress unnoticed. Tight tape without padding increases pressure and friction on the skin beneath the tubing. Leaving devices in one position for days concentrates pressure on the same area of skin.

Question 674BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (A fit-tested N95 respirator or a higher-level respirator for every room entry): airborne precautions require a fit-tested N95 or higher-level respirator because tuberculosis spreads through tiny airborne particles. A surgical mask does not seal to the face and does not filter airborne particles adequately. A cloth face covering with a face shield does not filter the small particles that carry tuberculosis. Screening and routine vaccines do not protect the nurse from inhaling tuberculosis, so a respirator is still required.

Question 675DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D (A serum potassium level of 2.8 mEq/L on the admission laboratory panel): repeated purging causes potassium loss that can lead to life-threatening dysrhythmias, making this the most dangerous finding. Dry skin and brittle nails reflect poor nutrition but are not immediately life-threatening. Calluses on the knuckles are a common sign of self-induced vomiting and help confirm the diagnosis. Swollen parotid glands are a typical finding with frequent vomiting and do not require emergency treatment.

Question 676AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (A client with diabetes whose glucose is 64 mg/dL and who is diaphoretic and confused): hypoglycemia with diaphoresis and a change in mental status is an acute, potentially life-threatening problem that needs immediate treatment. Moderate pain after hip replacement needs attention soon but is not immediately dangerous. Anxiety about home care is an important psychosocial need that can be addressed after the unstable client. An oxygen saturation that matches this client's documented baseline is an expected finding for chronic lung disease.

Question 677BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B ("It may take a few weeks of daily doses to feel the full effect."): buspirone has a delayed onset and is taken daily, often requiring several weeks to reach its full effect. Alcohol should be avoided because it can increase dizziness and drowsiness and worsens anxiety over time. Buspirone is not a fast-acting sedative and is not effective when taken only as needed. Any change in this medication should be discussed with the provider rather than stopped once symptoms ease.

Question 678CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Assess withdrawal severity with the facility's scale and implement seizure precautions): these findings suggest alcohol withdrawal, which can progress to seizures and delirium tremens, and scoring severity guides medication treatment. Teaching about the long-term effects of alcohol is appropriate later, once the client is medically stable. A quiet environment reduces stimulation, but leaving the client unmonitored during active withdrawal is unsafe. Support group referrals are an important part of recovery planning but do not address the immediate physiological danger.

Question 679BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Stop the transfusion and keep the vein open with normal saline using new tubing): a temperature rise during a transfusion may signal a hemolytic or febrile reaction, so the nurse stops the transfusion, maintains access and notifies the provider and blood bank. Slowing the rate continues the infusion of blood that may be causing the reaction. Speeding up the transfusion increases the amount of blood infused and could worsen a serious reaction. Acetaminophen may mask a fever, but continuing the transfusion before evaluation is unsafe.

Question 680BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Help the client lie down and assess for dizziness and other symptoms of hypoperfusion): low blood pressure after a new medication can cause falls and poor perfusion, so the nurse keeps the client safe, assesses symptoms and then notifies the provider. Walking while hypotensive and lightheaded greatly increases the risk of fainting and falls. Giving more antihypertensive medication would lower the blood pressure even further. Waiting four hours to recheck leaves a symptomatic, hypotensive client without needed assessment and follow-up.

Question 681CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (The first dose of hepatitis B vaccine given intramuscularly in the thigh): the American Academy of Pediatrics and long-standing CDC guidance recommend the first hepatitis B vaccine dose for all medically stable newborns within 24 hours of birth. Varicella vaccine is a live vaccine given at 12 months of age or later. Human papillomavirus vaccine is given in late childhood or adolescence, usually starting at 11 to 12 years. The measles, mumps and rubella vaccine is a live vaccine first given at 12 to 15 months of age.

Question 682DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Notify the provider about the ginkgo use and the possible increased bleeding risk): ginkgo may increase bleeding risk when combined with anticoagulants, and the elevated INR makes it essential for the provider to review the regimen. Recording the supplement without reporting it leaves the bleeding risk and high INR unaddressed. Herbal products can interact with prescription drugs, so describing them as safe gives false reassurance. Separating the times of the doses does not prevent the interaction or reduce the bleeding risk.

Question 683CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Respiratory function, including vital capacity, breathing effort and ability to cough): ascending weakness can reach the respiratory muscles and cause respiratory failure, so breathing is monitored closely. Hearing is not typically affected in this condition and is not a priority assessment. Hydration status matters for overall care but is not as urgent as a threat to the airway and breathing. Nutritional intake is important but can be addressed after respiratory function is assessed.

Question 684DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Closing the curtain and keeping the client draped except for the area being washed): closing the curtain and draping protect the client's dignity and limit exposure during personal care. An open door exposes the client to people passing in the hallway during intimate care. Personal phones are not secure, and photographing a wound on one violates privacy and facility policy. Sharing the client's diagnosis with visitors without permission violates confidentiality.

Question 685DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("Avoid alcohol during treatment and for at least three days after the last dose."): drinking alcohol with metronidazole can cause a disulfiram-like reaction with nausea, vomiting, flushing and abdominal cramps. Metronidazole has important interactions, including with alcohol and warfarin, so saying it has none is inaccurate. Even a small amount of alcohol can trigger the reaction, and taking it with food does not prevent this. The risk continues throughout treatment and for several days afterward, not just during the first doses.

Question 686CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Reactive hyperemia, an early warning sign that pressure on the heel must be relieved): redness that blanches shows that circulation is intact, but it warns that pressure must be relieved, such as by floating the heels. A deep tissue injury appears as persistent, nonblanchable maroon or purple discoloration. A stage 2 injury involves an open, shallow wound or blister, which is not present here. Blanchable redness from pressure is not a sign of infection and does not call for a culture.

Question 687APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (A saturation of about 88% to 92%, titrated according to the provider's order): for clients with carbon dioxide retention, oxygen is commonly titrated to about 88% to 92% to correct hypoxemia without worsening hypercapnia. Aiming for 100% with high-flow oxygen can worsen carbon dioxide retention and lead to respiratory acidosis. A target of 96% to 100% is appropriate for many adults but is too high for a client who retains carbon dioxide. Leaving saturation below 85% allows dangerous hypoxemia, and oxygen should not be withheld from a hypoxic client.

The correct answer is C (Six weeks of cough with night sweats, weight loss and blood-streaked sputum): a prolonged cough with night sweats, unintended weight loss and blood in the sputum, along with an upper lobe cavity, is the classic picture of active tuberculosis. Question 689BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Crowded shared living settings such as shelters increase the risk of tuberculosis exposure): congregate settings such as shelters increase transmission because many people share air in close quarters, often with ill residents. Housing does matter for discharge planning, but here it also supports the suspected diagnosis. Tuberculosis can develop months after exposure, so a recent move does not lower the likelihood of disease. The history also helps the health department identify other people who may have been exposed.

Question 690ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Preventing airborne transmission of the infection to other clients, visitors and staff): until tuberculosis is ruled out or effectively treated, preventing spread to others is the priority, so the client is masked and moved to isolation. Nutritional support is important for recovery but is not as urgent as stopping transmission. Treating a low-grade fever improves comfort but does not address the risk to others in the triage area. Question 691CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Airborne precautions in a negative-pressure airborne infection isolation room with the door closed): tuberculosis spreads through airborne particles, so the client needs a negative-pressure room with the door closed and staff wearing respirators. Contact precautions protect against organisms spread by touch and do not prevent inhalation of airborne particles. Droplet precautions do not protect against small airborne particles, and a shared room would expose the roommate. These precautions continue until the client is no longer considered infectious based on treatment response and sputum results.

Question 692ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Put on a fit-tested N95 respirator and perform a seal check before entering): staff caring for a client with infectious tuberculosis wear a fit-tested N95 or higher-level respirator and check the seal each time. Gloves are used for contact with body fluids but offer no protection against inhaled particles. A surgical mask does not filter airborne particles, although the client wears one when leaving the room. Proper respirator use protects staff while the client remains infectious during early treatment.

Question 693BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B ("I'll take all my tuberculosis medicines for the full months, even after I feel better."): treatment lasts several months, and completing every dose prevents relapse and the development of drug-resistant tuberculosis. Missing doses lowers drug levels and allows the bacteria to survive and become resistant. Symptoms improve long before the infection is cured, so stopping early leads to relapse. Directly observed therapy helps the client take each dose and complete the full course.

Question 694DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Apply firm, direct pressure just above the insertion site and call for help): direct pressure above the puncture site controls arterial bleeding while help and the provider are summoned. Sitting upright flexes the hip and can increase bleeding from the femoral site. Documentation is needed but must come after the bleeding has been controlled. Changing the dressing delays control of active arterial bleeding and does not stop the hemorrhage.

Question 695BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Take a warm shower or bath before starting the morning's activities): warmth relaxes muscles and reduces joint stiffness, making gentle movement and daily activities easier. Cold is more useful for acute flares with swelling and tends to increase stiffness in the morning. Keeping joints still prolongs stiffness, while gentle range of motion helps maintain function. Staying in bed for long periods promotes stiffness and deconditioning rather than relief.

Question 696DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A client with newly diagnosed sepsis whose blood pressure is trending downward): a client with sepsis who is becoming hypotensive is unstable and needs the complex assessment and rapid decisions of the most experienced nurse. Discharge teaching for a stable client can be done by the newly licensed RN. A stable client receiving routine oral medications can be assigned to the LPN. Applying a topical cream for a mild rash is routine care suitable for the LPN.

Question 697DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Colchicine, taken by mouth to reduce the inflammation of the acute attack): colchicine, along with nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs or corticosteroids, is used to treat the inflammation of an acute gout attack. Warfarin is an anticoagulant and has no role in treating the crystal-induced inflammation of gout. Levothyroxine treats hypothyroidism and does not relieve a gout attack. Low-dose aspirin reduces uric acid excretion and can worsen gout rather than relieve it.

Question 698BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Obtaining a provider order, following facility policy and monitoring the client at set intervals): restraints require a provider order, regular monitoring and release as soon as possible, following facility policy and federal regulations. Restraints are removed as soon as the client no longer meets criteria, not kept on for an arbitrary period. Even when restraints are applied in an emergency, an order must be obtained promptly rather than at a later round. Ties are secured to the bed frame, because attaching them to side rails can cause injury when the rails are moved.

Question 699DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (The safety data sheet for the specific chemical product that was spilled): safety data sheets describe each chemical's hazards, required protective equipment, spill cleanup and first-aid measures. An infection prevention manual covers cleaning procedures but not the detailed hazard and first-aid information for a specific product. A drug reference guide covers medications, not industrial chemicals such as disinfectant concentrates. An incident report is completed after the exposure but does not provide hazard or first-aid instructions.

Question 700DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (A beta blocker such as propranolol to block the cardiac effects of thyroid hormone): beta blockers control the tachycardia and other cardiovascular effects of excess thyroid hormone, alongside antithyroid drugs, cooling and supportive care. Thyroid hormone replacement would add more hormone and worsen thyroid storm. Loop diuretics do not control heart rate, and fluid loss could worsen dehydration from fever. Anticoagulants may be considered for atrial fibrillation but do not slow the heart rate.
