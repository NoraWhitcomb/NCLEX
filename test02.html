Question 101CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("Keep the genital area clean and dry, and report itching, discharge, or burning."): empagliflozin causes glucose to be excreted in the urine, which raises the risk of genital yeast infections and urinary tract infections. Because these drugs cause osmotic diuresis, clients need adequate hydration, and severe fluid restriction could lead to dehydration and kidney injury. Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors usually cause modest weight loss from calorie loss in the urine, not weight gain. The medication is taken daily as prescribed for glucose control and heart failure benefit, not only when glucose readings are high.

Question 102BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Theophylline toxicity, which requires holding the dose and checking a serum drug level): theophylline has a narrow therapeutic range, ciprofloxacin slows its metabolism, and nausea, restlessness and tachycardia are early signs of toxicity. These symptoms are signs of a rising drug level rather than a harmless adjustment, and untreated toxicity can progress to dysrhythmias and seizures. Hypoglycemia can cause restlessness and a fast pulse, but repeated vomiting after starting an interacting drug points to theophylline toxicity. An allergic reaction typically causes hives, itching, swelling or wheezing, which are not part of this client's presentation.

Question 103DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Acknowledge the daughter's concern and explain that the client has the right to discuss her care): a competent adult has the right to information and to make decisions about her own treatment, and the nurse advocates for her while responding to the family with empathy. Deferring to the provider about whether a competent client may receive information takes the decision away from the client herself. Waiting for the daughter's approval gives a family member control over a competent client's right to discuss her own care. Repeating the daughter's request to the client creates conflict and still does not answer the client's question about her treatment.

Question 104BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Verify with the provider whether and when the anticoagulant should be held before the procedure): decisions about holding an anticoagulant belong to the provider, who weighs the client's bleeding risk against the risk of stroke and clots. Choosing a standard hold time on the nurse's own judgment could leave too much drug on board or expose the client to unnecessary clot risk. Continuing the usual dose without guidance could cause serious bleeding if polyps are removed during the colonoscopy. Vitamin K does not reverse apixaban and would require a prescription, so suggesting it is outside the nurse's scope and clinically incorrect.

Question 105CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Sit the client upright and check for a full bladder or a kinked urinary catheter): these findings signal autonomic dysreflexia, and sitting upright lowers blood pressure while the nurse finds and removes the trigger, most often bladder distention. Lying flat with the legs raised increases venous return and can raise the blood pressure further, worsening the risk of stroke. Treating the headache and waiting an hour delays relief of a hypertensive emergency that needs immediate action. A cool compress may ease discomfort but does nothing to remove the stimulus or lower the dangerously high blood pressure.

Question 106CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("It gives a steady background level of insulin, usually injected once a day."): glargine is a long-acting basal insulin with no pronounced peak, given once daily at about the same time to cover baseline needs. Basal insulin is needed every day, including days with normal readings, because a client with type 1 diabetes makes no insulin of their own. Glargine is not mixed with other insulins in the same syringe because mixing changes its absorption and action. Insulin given right before meals is the role of the rapid-acting lispro, not the basal glargine.

Question 107APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Perforation of the ulcer with peritonitis requiring emergency surgical evaluation): sudden severe pain with a rigid, board-like abdomen and tachycardia indicates perforation and peritonitis, a surgical emergency. Typical ulcer pain is burning and related to meals and does not cause a board-like abdomen or a sudden change in vital signs. Gas pain causes bloating and cramping, but the abdomen remains soft rather than rigid. Constipation develops gradually with distention and discomfort, not sudden severe pain with signs of peritoneal irritation.

Question 108ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (A stethoscope and blood pressure cuff kept in the room for this client only): noncritical reusable items that touch the client's skin, such as a stethoscope and cuff, stay in the room to prevent spread of resistant organisms. The crash cart serves the whole unit in emergencies, so keeping it in one isolation room would contaminate it and delay care for others. The medication cart stays outside the room, and only the client's own doses are brought in to avoid contaminating shared supplies. A personal phone is not client care equipment and can carry organisms out of the room, so it should not be used during care.

Question 109DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Regular insulin, so the clear vial is not contaminated by the cloudy insulin): drawing clear regular insulin before cloudy NPH keeps NPH out of the regular vial, which would otherwise alter its rapid action. Drawing NPH first risks pushing intermediate-acting insulin into the regular vial and changing every future dose from that vial. The sequence matters because contaminating the regular vial changes its onset and peak, even when the dose in the syringe is correct. Regular and NPH insulin are compatible and may be mixed in one syringe, which reduces the number of injections.

Question 110CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Finish scheduled medication administration before handoff or clearly report any doses still pending): completing doses or clearly reporting pending ones prevents both omitted and duplicate doses during the transition of care. Giving medications early outside the allowed window is a timing error and can cause duplicate dosing by the next nurse. A nurse should give only medications the nurse personally prepared, and unlabeled cups left for someone else invite wrong-drug errors. Delaying documentation until the end of the shift leaves the record inaccurate and can lead another nurse to repeat a dose.

Question 111APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A ("You sound very alone right now; are you having any thoughts of hurting yourself?"): the nurse reflects the client's feelings and asks directly about suicidal thoughts, which does not increase risk and allows for safety planning. Generalizing the feeling to everyone minimizes the client's distress and discourages further sharing. Changing the subject to something cheerful blocks communication and misses a possible warning sign of suicide. Disagreeing and offering reassurance dismisses the client's feelings and leaves the risk of self-harm unassessed.

Question 112BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (A positive urine pregnancy test detecting human chorionic gonadotropin at the clinic visit): a pregnancy test is a probable sign because conditions such as certain tumors or medications can also produce a positive result. Fetal heart tones detected by the provider come only from the fetus, so they are a positive sign of pregnancy. Ultrasound visualization of an embryo with cardiac activity confirms pregnancy and is a positive sign. Fetal movement felt by an examiner is a positive sign, unlike movement reported by the client, which is presumptive.

Question 113DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Damage to the heart muscle, consistent with an acute myocardial infarction): troponin is a highly specific marker of myocardial injury, and a rising level together with ischemic symptoms such as chest pressure is consistent with acute myocardial infarction, which the provider confirms using serial values and the full clinical picture. Liver cell injury is reflected by elevated liver enzymes such as alanine aminotransferase, not by troponin. Dehydration may raise blood urea nitrogen and hematocrit, but it does not explain a rising troponin with chest pressure. Kidney injury is shown by rising creatinine and blood urea nitrogen, and a rising troponin with symptoms still points to the heart.

Question 114ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Notify the receiving department so staff can use droplet precautions on arrival): communicating isolation needs to the receiving department lets staff prepare and protect themselves and other clients. Transport staff need to know, but radiology staff also have close contact during positioning and the scan. Sharing isolation status with staff involved in care is a permitted disclosure and is essential for infection control. The family does not provide care in the radiology department, so informing them does not protect staff or other clients.

Question 115CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Assess vital signs, oxygen saturation, and recently given medications for a cause): sudden confusion suggests delirium, which often has a reversible cause such as hypoxia, infection or opioid effects that the nurse must find quickly. Restraints are a last resort that can worsen agitation and do nothing to identify or treat the cause of the confusion. Dementia develops gradually, so labeling a sudden change as dementia misses a likely reversible delirium. Learning the client's baseline from the family can help later, but it should follow assessment of physical causes.

Question 116BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Serum calcium, because the parathyroid glands may have been injured during surgery): perioral and fingertip tingling are early signs of hypocalcemia from parathyroid injury or removal during thyroidectomy. Sodium imbalance from antidiuretic hormone excess is not a typical complication of thyroid surgery and does not cause tingling. Neck bleeding threatens the airway with swelling and pressure rather than causing tingling of the mouth and fingers. Glucose changes may cause shakiness or sweating, but perioral numbness after thyroidectomy points to low calcium.

Question 117AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A ("The recombinant zoster vaccine is recommended in two doses for adults 50 and older."): current guidance recommends two doses of recombinant zoster vaccine for adults 50 and older and for adults 19 and older with weakened immunity. Children receive the varicella vaccine to prevent chickenpox, while the zoster vaccine is for adults. Shingles comes from reactivation of the chickenpox virus, so adults with a history of chickenpox are the main group the vaccine protects. Waiting until age 70 leaves the client unprotected for years when vaccination is already recommended.

Question 118DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D (A baseline of 172 beats per minute by Doppler at 30 weeks of gestation): the normal fetal heart rate baseline is 110 to 160 beats per minute, so 172 indicates tachycardia that needs further assessment. A rate of 128 at term is within the normal range, and brief rises with movement are reassuring. A steady rate of 144 at 24 weeks is within normal limits and needs no further action. A rate near 156 at 20 weeks is within the normal range, since earlier pregnancies tend to have rates toward the upper end.

Question 119BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (A client who will need daily intravenous antibiotics at home through a PICC line): home intravenous therapy requires skilled nursing for line care, infusion monitoring and client teaching. Uncomplicated cataract surgery usually needs only eye drops and routine office follow-up. A client who is independent with one oral medication has no need for skilled nursing at home. A client with negative chest pain testing can usually be managed with routine outpatient follow-up.

Question 120CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Encourage the client to walk in the hallway several times throughout the day): walking stimulates peristalsis, helps restore bowel function and promotes passing of gas after surgery. Carbonated beverages add gas to the gastrointestinal tract and can increase bloating. Drinking through a straw causes the client to swallow extra air, which worsens gas pains. Bed rest slows the return of bowel motility and increases the risk of other complications such as clots.

Question 121CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Report the temperature reading per policy and follow instructions about the stored medications): vaccines and insulin can lose effectiveness outside the safe range, and policy and the pharmacy determine whether they can still be used. Turning the unit off and on does not address medications already exposed and may hide an equipment problem. A normal appearance does not show whether a medication has lost potency from improper storage. Giving medications early or quickly is a timing error and could deliver doses that are no longer effective.

Question 122AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Every client, because discharge planning and teaching begin at the time of admission): starting discharge planning at admission for all clients allows time for teaching, referrals and a safe transition home. Clients with acute illnesses also need teaching about medications, warning signs and follow-up care. Many clients do not know what to ask, so waiting for questions leaves important teaching undone. Short stays leave even less time to teach, so delaying until a longer stay is expected misses most clients.

Question 123DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (0.9% sodium chloride, primed through the blood administration set with a filter): isotonic saline is the standard solution given with red blood cells because it does not damage them or cause clotting in the tubing. Dextrose in water is hypotonic once the sugar is metabolized and can cause red cells to clump and break down. Lactated Ringer's solution contains calcium, which can trigger clotting of the anticoagulated blood in the tubing. Any solution containing dextrose can cause red cells to clump or lyse, so it is not used with blood products.

Question 124BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Hold the dose and contact the provider to clarify the route and rate): intravenous push potassium can cause fatal dysrhythmias and cardiac arrest, so it must be diluted and infused at a controlled rate after the order is clarified. Changing the route to intramuscular is outside the nurse's authority and the injection is painful and damaging to tissue. Diluting the drug in a small volume and pushing it still delivers a dangerous bolus of potassium to the heart. Giving the drug over two minutes is still an intravenous push, and cardiac monitoring does not prevent the resulting dysrhythmia.

Question 125DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Notify the provider immediately and prepare for urgent neurologic evaluation and imaging): a drop of five points signals neurologic decline from possible bleeding or rising intracranial pressure that needs immediate evaluation. Waiting four hours for the next check could allow brain herniation to develop before treatment begins. Sleep makes neurologic changes harder to detect and does not address the cause of the declining score. A sedative can mask further neurologic deterioration and depress breathing in a client with a worsening head injury.

Question 126DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (An occupational therapist, to assess the home and recommend adaptive safety equipment): occupational therapists evaluate the home environment and recommend changes such as grab bars, lighting and adaptive equipment. A pharmacist can help reduce fall risk from medications but does not evaluate the physical home setting. A chaplain supports spiritual and emotional needs rather than assessing hazards in the home. A dietitian supports nutrition and bone health but does not assess the home for safety hazards.

Question 127DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Truncal weight gain, a rounded face, and elevated blood glucose levels): excess cortisol causes central obesity, a moon face, hyperglycemia and thin, fragile skin. Heat intolerance, weight loss and a bounding pulse are signs of hyperthyroidism rather than cortisol excess. Cortisol raises blood glucose, so low glucose with dizziness suggests cortisol deficiency instead. Bronze skin, salt craving and high potassium are classic findings of Addison's disease, the opposite of Cushing's syndrome.

Question 128AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The living will the client completed while competent, stating her treatment wishes): a living will records the client's own decisions about life-sustaining treatment for times when she cannot speak for herself. A proxy speaks for the client, but his personal request does not replace the wishes she documented while competent. A general consent for treatment covers routine care and does not address resuscitation or mechanical ventilation. Recognizing the living will as the guiding document is the first step in advocating for this client.

Question 129BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (To make decisions based on the client's known wishes when she cannot decide): a proxy is expected to make the choices the client would make, using her known wishes and documented directives as the guide. A proxy who substitutes personal preferences for the client's written wishes is not fulfilling the role. A health care proxy makes medical decisions, while finances are handled through a separate financial power of attorney. Explaining the role gently helps the son see that honoring his mother's wishes is how he speaks for her.

Question 130CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Advocate for the client's documented wishes while providing comfort for her breathing): the nurse upholds the do-not-resuscitate status that reflects the client's wishes and addresses her distress with comfort measures. Starting resuscitation against a valid living will and code status order would violate the client's autonomy. Removing the son is not warranted for verbal distress, and the family needs support and information rather than exclusion. Combining advocacy with comfort care meets both the client's physical needs and her ethical right to self-determination.

Delaying relief of severe dyspnea until a meeting occurs leaves the client suffering unnecessarily. Question 133AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The client rests comfortably, her documented wishes are honored, and her family feels supported): ethical care honors the client's documented wishes, relieves her symptoms and supports the family through the end of life. Intubating the client, even briefly, goes against her living will and does not reflect her wishes. Limiting the son's visits excludes family from the end of life and fails to support them. Comfort, respect for autonomy and family support together show that the care plan met its ethical goals.

Question 134ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (A pair of sneakers with laces and a leather belt in the closet): shoelaces and belts can be used for hanging, so they are removed from the room of a client at risk for suicide according to policy. Paper cups are lightweight and tear easily, so they pose little risk and are commonly used on psychiatric units. A soft blanket is standard bedding that is generally allowed for clients under close observation. A paperback book poses no realistic means of self-harm and can provide helpful distraction.

Question 135ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A (Going to bed and waking up at the same times every day, including weekends): a consistent sleep and wake schedule reinforces the body's circadian rhythm and makes falling asleep easier. Long afternoon naps reduce sleep drive and make it harder to fall asleep at night. Caffeine is a stimulant that can disrupt sleep for many hours after it is consumed. Light from screens suppresses melatonin, and using the bed for activities other than sleep weakens the link between bed and sleep.

Question 136CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C (Agitation, elevated blood pressure, a rapid heart rate, and dilated pupils): methamphetamine is a stimulant that causes agitation, hypertension, tachycardia, dilated pupils and sometimes psychosis or hyperthermia. Slow breathing, pinpoint pupils and drowsiness are signs of opioid intoxication. Low blood pressure, slurred speech and an unsteady gait suggest a depressant such as alcohol or a benzodiazepine. Stimulants raise heart rate and body temperature, so a slow pulse and low temperature do not fit this client.

Question 137BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Position the client to protect the airway, start intravenous access, and notify the provider): coffee-ground emesis with tachycardia and hypotension signals a significant upper gastrointestinal bleed, so airway protection, access and urgent notification come first. An oral antacid does not control active bleeding and adds to the risk of aspiration. The client should take nothing by mouth because of aspiration risk and the likely need for urgent endoscopy. This client is unstable and needs urgent inpatient endoscopy, not an outpatient appointment.

Question 138BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B ("Reporting errors and near misses helps the team find and fix system problems."): a culture of safety encourages reporting every error and near miss so the team can correct system weaknesses such as look-alike names. Limiting reports to harmful errors hides patterns that could lead to serious harm later. A verbal mention alone does not create a record that the organization can analyze and use to prevent repeat errors. A just culture focuses on fixing systems rather than blaming individuals, and fear of discipline discourages reporting.

Question 139DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Helping a client manage postoperative pain so the client can rest comfortably): beneficence means taking action to do good for the client, such as relieving pain to promote comfort and recovery. Distributing care fairly according to need is an example of justice. Being truthful about a diagnosis is an example of veracity. Keeping a promise to a client is an example of fidelity.

Question 140BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B ("No amount of alcohol is known to be safe at any time during pregnancy."): no safe amount, type or time for alcohol during pregnancy is known, and alcohol can cause fetal alcohol spectrum disorders. All types of alcohol, including beer and wine, carry the same risk to the developing baby. Daily drinking at any point in pregnancy can harm fetal brain development, so no amount is considered safe after the first trimester. Alcohol can harm the fetus throughout pregnancy, including during organ formation in the first trimester.

Question 141BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Acute dystonia, an early extrapyramidal reaction to the antipsychotic medication): sudden neck spasm and upward eye deviation early in antipsychotic therapy are classic signs of acute dystonia, which the provider may treat with an anticholinergic. Tardive dyskinesia develops after months or years of therapy and causes repetitive facial and tongue movements. The client is taking haloperidol rather than lithium, and lithium toxicity causes tremor, vomiting and confusion. Serotonin syndrome causes agitation, fever, sweating and hyperreflexia and is linked to serotonergic drugs, not haloperidol alone.

Question 142BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Heart rate of 46 beats per minute with blood pressure of 84/50 mm Hg): intravenous amiodarone can cause bradycardia and hypotension, which need prompt evaluation and possible adjustment of the infusion. Conversion to sinus rhythm at a normal rate is the desired therapeutic effect of the medication. A stable blood pressure that matches the baseline shows the client is tolerating the infusion. Mild thirst relieved by fluids is a minor finding and is not a recognized serious adverse effect of the drug.

Question 143CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C ("Tell your provider right away if you have new or worsening thoughts of suicide."): antidepressants carry a boxed warning about increased suicidal thinking in clients younger than 25, so new or worsening thoughts must be reported promptly. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors usually take several weeks to reach full effect, and expecting rapid results can lead to discouragement. Alcohol worsens depression and can increase sedation, so clients are advised to avoid it. Stopping abruptly can cause discontinuation symptoms, so the client should contact the provider before making changes.

Question 144AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Giving scheduled subcutaneous insulin to a stable client with type 2 diabetes): practical nurses commonly give scheduled subcutaneous medications to stable clients with predictable outcomes. Developing a teaching plan requires RN-level assessment and planning, although the practical nurse may reinforce teaching. Evaluating the response to a new high-alert infusion requires RN judgment. The initial admission assessment is an RN responsibility and cannot be assigned to a practical nurse.

Question 145BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Neuroleptic malignant syndrome, a medical emergency requiring the antipsychotic to be stopped): high fever, muscle rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability are the hallmark signs of neuroleptic malignant syndrome. Acute dystonia causes sudden muscle spasms but not high fever or unstable vital signs. Tardive dyskinesia develops after long-term use and involves involuntary movements, not fever and rigidity. Sedation may occur with antipsychotics but does not explain high fever, rigidity and unstable vital signs.

Question 146DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (A client receiving brachytherapy with a sealed radioactive implant for cervical cancer): facility radiation-safety policies commonly avoid assigning pregnant staff to clients with radioactive implants to keep fetal exposure as low as possible, with the radiation safety officer consulted when needed. Diabetes teaching involves no radiation or infectious hazard to a pregnant nurse. Caring for a client receiving an antihypertensive infusion poses no special risk to the fetus. A client with a casted fracture presents no radiation or infection hazard during pregnancy.

Question 147BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Ambulatory clients, who can be directed to exit while staff help others): directing ambulatory clients out first clears the way quickly, then staff move clients who need assistance and finally those who are most dependent. Moving clients on life support first would tie up many staff and delay the evacuation of everyone else. Isolation status does not set evacuation order, since fire and smoke pose the immediate threat. Bedbound clients need the most staff and time, so they are usually moved after those who can walk or use wheelchairs.

Question 148DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Knowing who identified the allergy does not clarify what kind of reaction the client had. Asking about other pain medicines is useful later, but it does not clarify the reported codeine reaction. Other allergies must be documented, but the reported codeine reaction should be clarified first.

Question 149APsychosocial Integrity

Weight gain is associated with some antidepressant medications rather than with electroconvulsive therapy. A muscle relaxant is given during the procedure, but lasting weakness or paralysis is not an expected effect. Question 150CSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is C (Use long-handled forceps to place the implant in the lead container and call radiation safety): long-handled forceps maintain distance from the source, the lead container provides shielding and the radiation safety officer guides next steps. Question 151CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Air embolism from air entering the venous circulation through the open catheter): sudden dyspnea, chest pain, hypotension and a churning heart murmur while the line is open are classic signs that air has entered the circulation. The nurse clamps the catheter, places the client on the left side with the head down if protocol allows, gives oxygen and calls for help. A bloodstream infection develops over hours to days with fever and chills, and anaphylaxis would more typically bring hives, wheezing or swelling rather than a churning murmur. Pneumothorax is a risk during catheter insertion, not during a routine tubing change on a catheter already in place.

Question 154CPsychosocial Integrity

Question 155DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Osmotic demyelination from a sodium rise that is faster than the prescribed limit): hypertonic saline can raise serum sodium quickly, and correction that is too fast can cause osmotic demyelination, so sodium levels are checked frequently. Hypertonic saline does not drive glucose into cells, so hypoglycemia is not an expected complication. Hemolysis is a concern with hypotonic solutions such as sterile water, not with hypertonic saline. Rising sodium pulls water out of brain cells rather than into them, so cerebral edema is a risk of correcting hypernatremia too fast.

Question 156ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (Compare the medication with the order and confirm two client identifiers): checking the medication against the order and confirming the client's name and date of birth at the bedside ensures the right drug reaches the right client. A room number or bed sign is not an acceptable identifier because clients move and signs can be wrong. Preparing medications for more than one client at a time increases the chance of mixing them up, especially with similar names. Relying on memory for doses bypasses verification and is a common cause of medication errors.

Question 157DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Place the call light, walker and personal items within the client's reach): keeping the call light and needed items close reduces unassisted reaching and getting up, which lowers the risk of another fall. Four raised side rails can lead a client to climb over them, causing falls from a greater height. A dark room makes nighttime trips to the bathroom more dangerous, so a night-light is preferred.

Addressing the anxiety also comes after restoring a closed system and supporting oxygenation.

Question 161APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Submerge the open chest tube end in sterile water, then connect a new system): placing the tube end 2 to 3 cm under sterile water creates a temporary water seal that lets air escape but not re-enter, until a new sterile system is connected. Clamping for a prolonged time traps air in the pleural space and can cause a tension pneumothorax. Removing a chest tube is a provider decision, and pulling it out would allow the pneumothorax to reaccumulate. Once the seal is restored, the nurse can connect a new drainage system and reassess the client's breathing.

Question 162BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Notify the provider, apply oxygen as ordered and monitor respiratory status closely): the provider needs to evaluate the client and may order a chest radiograph, while supplemental oxygen and close monitoring support the client in the meantime. Walking increases oxygen demand in a client who is still hypoxic and could worsen the dyspnea. Lying flat reduces chest expansion, so an upright position is better for a client who is short of breath. Ongoing monitoring of respiratory rate, oxygen saturation and the drainage system helps the nurse detect a tension pneumothorax early.

Question 163CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Oxygen saturation of 95% and respiratory rate of 20 with gentle tidal fluctuation): a return toward baseline oxygen saturation and respiratory rate, with tidaling that shows a patent system, indicates the lung is re-expanding. A rising respiratory rate with worsening dyspnea shows the client is deteriorating, not improving. A tracheal shift away from the affected side with hypotension suggests a tension pneumothorax, which is an emergency. The nurse continues to monitor the client and the drainage system closely after this improvement.

Question 164AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Stimulate the client, open the airway, coach deep breaths and apply oxygen): oversedation threatens the airway and breathing, so the nurse supports ventilation immediately and calls for help if the client does not respond, anticipating a reversal agent. Delaying intervention for 15 minutes allows hypoxemia and respiratory depression to worsen. Updating the family is appropriate later but does nothing to correct the client's breathing problem. Raising the legs and increasing fluids address low blood pressure, not the respiratory depression caused by the sedatives.

Question 165AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A ("Screening is recommended for adults aged 45 to 75, so let's discuss test options."): the USPSTF recommends colorectal cancer screening for average-risk adults aged 45 to 75, and several test options can be discussed with the provider. Waiting until age 60 would miss years of recommended screening for this client. A family history raises risk and may change when screening starts, but average-risk adults also need screening. Screening is meant for people without symptoms, while people with symptoms need a diagnostic evaluation instead.

Question 166BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Titrate to maintain a mean arterial pressure of at least 65 mm Hg): sepsis guidelines recommend an initial target of at least 65 mm Hg for clients receiving vasopressors to maintain organ perfusion. A systolic target above 140 mm Hg would require excessive vasopressor doses and increase the risk of ischemia and dysrhythmias. A pressure of 50 to 55 mm Hg is too low to perfuse the kidneys, brain and other vital organs. Pushing the pressure above 110 mm Hg adds vasopressor side effects without proven benefit.

Question 167DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Contact precautions with gown and gloves until 24 hours after effective treatment begins): scabies spreads through direct skin contact and contaminated linens or clothing, so gowns and gloves are used until treatment has taken effect. Droplet precautions protect against organisms spread by large respiratory droplets, which does not apply to mites. Airborne precautions are for organisms such as tuberculosis and measles that remain suspended in the air. Standard precautions alone do not adequately prevent spread of mites to staff and other clients through skin and linen contact.

Question 168DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (A client with anorexia nervosa who has eaten very little for three weeks): prolonged starvation depletes body stores, and feeding can cause dangerous drops in phosphate, potassium and magnesium as cells take them up. A short fast in a well-nourished client does not deplete electrolyte stores enough to cause refeeding syndrome. A client with normal weight and nutrition who has an orthopedic injury has no starvation history placing them at risk. A client who eats regular meals is not malnourished, so feeding does not cause the electrolyte shifts seen in refeeding.

Question 169APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Assess the airway closely and prepare for early endotracheal intubation): facial burns, singed nasal hair, hoarseness and stridor signal inhalation injury, and airway swelling can progress quickly to complete obstruction. Pain control is important for burn care but does not take priority over a threatened airway. Weight is needed for fluid resuscitation, but circulation comes after securing the airway. Wound care is performed after the airway, breathing and circulation have been stabilized.

Question 170DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Notify the provider and anticipate a stool test for Clostridioides difficile infection): antibiotic therapy raises the risk of Clostridioides difficile infection, and three or more unformed stools in 24 hours call for testing and contact precautions. Antidiarrheal drugs require an order and are usually avoided until this infection is ruled out because they can worsen it. Restricting fluids increases the risk of dehydration in a client already losing fluid through diarrhea. Monitoring for two more days delays diagnosis and treatment, and yogurt does not treat this infection.

Question 171BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (The feeding rate, formula concentration and any sorbitol-containing liquid medications): a recent rate increase, hyperosmolar formula and liquid medications containing sorbitol are common, correctable causes of diarrhea in tube-fed clients. Bowel obstruction causes distention and absent stools rather than frequent liquid stools. Albumin and weight reflect long-term nutritional status but do not identify the cause of acute diarrhea. Nasal skin checks are part of routine tube care but are unrelated to the client's diarrhea.

Question 172APsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is A (Keep a consistent routine with bright daytime light and a quiet, calm evening): structure, exposure to daylight and reduced evening stimulation support the sleep-wake cycle and lessen sundowning. Loud, busy evening activities add stimulation at the time the client is most easily overwhelmed. A dark room and daytime napping disrupt the sleep-wake cycle and can worsen evening confusion. Changing rooms each evening creates an unfamiliar environment that increases disorientation.

Question 173BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Inform the charge nurse and request training or direct assistance before proceeding): nurses practice within their competence, so the nurse seeks training, supervision or reassignment before performing an unfamiliar procedure. A video does not establish competence or meet facility requirements for validated skills. Assistive personnel cannot be delegated a nursing procedure outside their scope, and delegation does not solve the nurse's lack of training. The client is not a qualified source of instruction, and relying on the client's description puts safety at risk.

Question 174CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (By about 9 months, most infants can sit steadily without any support): CDC milestone checklists list sitting without support at about 9 months, so this 6-month-old is developing within expected limits. At 4 months most infants hold the head steady and may begin rolling, but they still need support to sit. At 2 months infants are just beginning to lift the head during tummy time and cannot sit. Waiting until 15 months would be well past the expected milestone and would warrant further evaluation.

Question 175CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (A client with a repaired second-degree perineal laceration after vaginal birth): a sitz bath soaks the perineal and rectal area in warm water, which eases pain and swelling and promotes healing after a perineal repair. A sitz bath does not reach the arm, and a cast must be kept dry. A sternal incision is far from the area a sitz bath treats and would not benefit from it. A scalp wound and head injury require neurological monitoring, not a perineal soak.

Question 176CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Preoperational stage, marked by egocentrism, magical thinking and lack of conservation): children from about 2 to 7 years think egocentrically, give life to objects and cannot yet understand that quantity stays the same when shape changes. The concrete operational stage begins around age 7, when children understand conservation and think logically about real situations. Formal operational thinking with abstract reasoning develops in adolescence. The sensorimotor stage spans birth to about 2 years, when infants learn through the senses and develop object permanence.

Question 177BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Canned soups, deli meats and other processed or packaged convenience foods): canned and processed foods are major sources of hidden sodium, and a single serving can supply a large share of a 2-gram daily limit. Fresh fruits are naturally very low in sodium and fit well in a sodium-restricted diet. Fresh or plain frozen vegetables cooked without salt are low in sodium and are encouraged. Plain grains prepared without salt contain little sodium and do not need to be limited.

Question 178DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Administer 0.4 mL, which delivers the ordered 4 mg of morphine): dividing the desired dose of 4 mg by the concentration of 10 mg per mL gives 0.4 mL. Giving 4 mL would deliver 40 mg of morphine, ten times the ordered dose. A volume of 0.04 mL would deliver only 0.4 mg, which is one tenth of the ordered dose. Dividing the vial strength by the dose reverses the formula and would deliver 25 mg.

Question 179CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (High Fowler's, or sitting upright leaning forward with arms on the overbed table): sitting upright lets the diaphragm descend fully, and leaning forward with the arms supported helps the accessory muscles work efficiently. Lying flat pushes the abdominal organs against the diaphragm and makes breathing harder. Prone positioning is used for selected ventilated clients with severe hypoxemia, not for a dyspneic client with this condition. Lowering the head shifts abdominal contents toward the chest and further restricts lung expansion.

Question 180APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Contact the provider to clarify the order because of the hypertensive crisis risk): phenelzine is a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, and sympathomimetic decongestants taken with it can cause a dangerous rise in blood pressure. Taking the drug with food does not reduce the risk of a hypertensive crisis. Giving the first dose exposes the client to the interaction before the order is clarified. Changing the dose is outside the nurse's scope, and a lower dose can still trigger a hypertensive crisis.

Question 181DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("Osteoporosis screening with a bone density test is recommended for women 65 and older."): the USPSTF recommends osteoporosis screening for women aged 65 and older and for younger postmenopausal women at increased risk. Screening aims to find osteoporosis before a fracture occurs, so waiting for a fracture defeats its purpose. Long-term steroid use increases risk, but women 65 and older are screened even without that history. Screening starts at 65 for women at average risk, not at 75, because bone loss accelerates after menopause.

Question 182DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Per facility policy, commonly at least every 2 hours, with more frequent monitoring): facility policy sets the schedule, which commonly requires release at least every 2 hours with frequent checks of circulation, skin, hydration and toileting. Every 8 hours leaves the client at risk for skin breakdown, impaired circulation and unmet basic needs. A restrained client may not be able to request care, so the nurse assesses proactively. A 4-hour interval is too long, and the nurse remains responsible for assessing the restrained client.

Question 183BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Have the child keep a symptom diary and practice inhaler technique with a spacer): school-age children are in the stage of industry versus inferiority and gain confidence from mastering concrete, hands-on tasks. School-age children can learn to use inhalers and should be included in their own care. Long lectures without visual aids exceed a school-age child's attention span and do not build skills. Avoiding the topic leaves the child unprepared and can increase anxiety rather than reduce it.

Question 184ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A ("For safety, personal heaters aren't allowed here, but I'll bring you warm blankets."): personal space heaters are fire hazards, especially with oxygen in use, and warm blankets meet the client's comfort need safely. Placing a heater under the bed puts it near linens that could ignite. Using a heater near an oxygen source greatly increases the risk of fire. Question 185AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (A client whose oxygen saturation dropped from 95% to 86% with new confusion): an acute drop in oxygen saturation with a new change in mental status meets common rapid response criteria and signals possible deterioration. A blood pressure of 128/80 with a normal heart rate reflects a stable client. A glucose of 142 at bedtime is acceptable, and a snack request is a routine need. Moderate incisional pain after knee surgery is expected and can be managed with ordered analgesics.

Question 186DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D ("Drink plenty of fluids each day and report any skin rash right away."): good hydration helps prevent uric acid kidney stones, and a rash can be the first sign of a severe hypersensitivity reaction. Allopurinol lowers uric acid to prevent attacks and is taken daily, not as a treatment for acute flares. Limiting fluids raises the risk of kidney stones and dehydration, especially in a client working in the heat. Flares are common when therapy begins, and the provider may add a preventive medication rather than stop allopurinol.

Question 187DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A 19-year-old client with type 1 diabetes whose condition is stable today): a stable young adult with a condition familiar to pediatric nurses is the safest match for a nurse working outside the usual specialty. A ventilated client needs critical care skills that a pediatric float nurse may not have for adults. A new titrated antidysrhythmic infusion requires experience with adult cardiac monitoring and dosing. An unstable bleeding client needs an experienced adult nurse who can recognize and respond to rapid deterioration.

Question 188BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (The warm, wet sensation and new firm swelling at the femoral puncture site): a warm, wet groin with blood on the linen and a new firm swelling suggests bleeding from the arterial puncture site. Hunger after fasting for the procedure is expected and does not threaten the client's safety. Question 189CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (A hematoma forming as blood collects in tissue around the arterial puncture): a firm, tender, discolored swelling at the puncture site shows blood leaking from the artery into the surrounding tissue. A contrast reaction would cause hives, itching, wheezing or hypotension rather than a localized swelling at the groin. Normal healing does not produce a firm, enlarging swelling with bleeding at the site. A hematoma requires prompt intervention because it can enlarge and compromise circulation to the leg.

Question 190AReduction of Risk Potential

Back discomfort is common during bed rest and can be eased once the bleeding is controlled. Hunger is a comfort need that can be met after the bleeding has been addressed. Question 191CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (Apply firm manual pressure just above the puncture site over the femoral artery): direct pressure over the artery just above the skin puncture compresses the vessel at the point of entry and controls the bleeding while help is summoned. Bending the leg can dislodge a forming clot and increase bleeding from the femoral site. Observing the site delays treatment and allows more arterial blood loss. The nurse keeps the leg straight and maintains pressure until the bleeding stops.

Question 192BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Call for help, notify the provider and check vital signs and distal pulses): the provider must be informed of the bleeding, and the nurse monitors circulation and hemodynamic stability while keeping pressure in place. Releasing pressure to leave the room allows bleeding to resume and leaves an unstable client alone. Standing violates the bed rest order and can increase bleeding and cause a fall. The falling blood pressure and rising heart rate show why close monitoring is needed.

Question 193AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (No new bleeding, stable vital signs and strong right pedal pulses with a warm foot): the absence of bleeding, stable vital signs and a warm foot with strong pulses show both hemostasis and good distal circulation. A cool, pale foot with a weak pulse suggests impaired arterial flow that needs urgent evaluation. An enlarging swelling indicates continued bleeding under the skin even if the dressing looks dry. The nurse continues frequent checks of the site and pulses for the rest of the bed rest period.

Question 194DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Transfusion of packed red blood cells as prescribed with close monitoring): a hemoglobin below 7 g/dL with active bleeding and signs of instability calls for red blood cell transfusion to restore oxygen-carrying capacity. Discharge is unsafe for a client with active bleeding, tachycardia and a critically low hemoglobin. Oral iron takes weeks to raise hemoglobin and cannot replace acute blood loss. Fresh frozen plasma replaces clotting factors but does not raise hemoglobin.

Question 195DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Use a footboard or high-top boots as ordered and perform ankle range of motion): supporting the feet in a neutral position and moving the ankle joints prevent contracture of the calf muscles that leads to foot drop. Pillows under the knees promote knee flexion contractures and impair venous return. Tight sheets push the feet into plantar flexion and worsen the risk of foot drop. Crossing the legs compresses nerves and vessels and does not keep the feet in a neutral position.

Question 196CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Keep the head of the bed at 30 degrees or lower and use a lift sheet): a lower head-of-bed angle reduces sliding, and a lift sheet moves the client without dragging the skin across the linen. A high head-of-bed angle makes the client slide down, increasing shear on the sacrum. Pulling the client up by the arms drags the skin along the sheets and can injure the shoulders. Allowing the client to slide creates shear forces, and repositioning frequency should be individualized to the client's skin and support surface.

Question 197CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Set the infusion pump to deliver 10 mL per hour for the ordered dose): the concentration is 25,000 units divided by 250 mL, or 100 units per mL, and 1,000 units per hour divided by 100 units per mL equals 10 mL per hour. A rate of 25 mL per hour would deliver 2,500 units per hour, which is more than twice the ordered dose. Running 100 mL per hour would deliver 10,000 units per hour and create a serious bleeding risk. A rate of 1 mL per hour delivers only 100 units per hour, which is far below the ordered dose.

Question 198CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (A client with obesity on bed rest after a total hip replacement yesterday): major orthopedic surgery, immobility and obesity each increase the risk of venous thromboembolism, and together they place this client at greatest risk. Brief outpatient eye surgery with same-day discharge carries a low risk of clot formation. Frequent walking after a short laparoscopic procedure promotes venous return and lowers risk. A minor skin procedure in a healthy adult does not cause the immobility or vessel injury that leads to clots.

Question 199DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Write down the order, read it back to the provider and await confirmation): writing down and reading back a verbal order allows the provider to confirm it and reduces errors during emergencies. Waiting until the end of the shift to document risks inaccurate records and missed details. Relying on another person's memory is unreliable and does not verify the order with the provider. Verbal orders are acceptable in emergencies, and delaying treatment for electronic entry could harm the client.

Question 200DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D ("Move both crutches and your right leg forward, then step through with your left leg."): in a three-point gait the crutches and the affected leg move forward together, and then the unaffected leg steps forward while weight rests on the hands. Bearing weight on the armpits can compress the axillary nerves and cause crutch palsy. Moving the unaffected leg first leaves the client without a stable base of support from the crutches. Alternating one crutch and the opposite leg describes a four-point gait, which requires weight bearing on both legs.
