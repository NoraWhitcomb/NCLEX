Question 401APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Stop the transfusion and keep the vein open with normal saline through new tubing): chills, back pain, fever and tachycardia early in a transfusion suggest an acute hemolytic or febrile reaction, and stopping the blood limits further exposure while the saline line preserves venous access. Slowing the rate still delivers incompatible or reactive blood to the client and can allow a hemolytic reaction to progress. The blood bank is notified and the unit is returned for testing, but only after the transfusion has been stopped and the client assessed. Treating the fever with acetaminophen while the blood keeps running masks the reaction and continues the harmful exposure.

Question 402AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Hold the medication and contact the prescriber to clarify the ordered dose): the nurse is accountable for questioning any order that appears unsafe, and holding the dose until the prescriber clarifies it protects the client from a possible overdose. Giving half the dose changes the prescription without authority and is outside the scope of nursing practice. Following an order that appears unsafe does not transfer responsibility, because the nurse shares legal accountability for every medication given. The client's report of a home dose may be useful information, but the nurse cannot substitute a different dose without a new prescription.

Question 403CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is C (Intramuscular administration of Rh immune globulin as prescribed by the provider): an Rh-negative client who is not sensitized routinely receives Rh immune globulin at about 28 weeks to prevent formation of antibodies against fetal Rh-positive red blood cells. Amniocentesis is an invasive procedure that is not needed for routine prevention and can itself cause fetal-maternal bleeding. Repeated antibody screening every two weeks does not prevent sensitization and is not part of standard prenatal care for an unsensitized client. Waiting until birth leaves the client unprotected during the third trimester, so the postpartum dose is given in addition to, not instead of, the antenatal dose.

Question 404BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 right away, or 911 if symptoms appear): an unknown amount of a cardiac or blood pressure medication can be life-threatening to a toddler even when no symptoms are present, so immediate expert guidance is needed. Inducing vomiting with ipecac or a gag reflex is no longer recommended because it delays treatment and can cause aspiration. Milk does not neutralize most medications and may delay appropriate care or increase the risk of vomiting and aspiration. Many blood pressure medications cause delayed bradycardia, hypotension or hypoglycemia, so home observation alone can miss a dangerous ingestion.

Question 405CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C (Teach ankle pumps and leg exercises and apply sequential compression devices as prescribed): active leg exercises and sequential compression devices promote venous return and reduce the venous stasis that leads to clot formation. Massaging the calves can dislodge an existing clot and cause a pulmonary embolism, so it is avoided in clients at risk. Restricting fluids causes hemoconcentration and increases blood viscosity, which raises the risk of thrombosis. Pillows under the knees compress the popliteal vessels and slow venous return, which promotes clot formation.

Question 406DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (An EPA-registered sporicidal disinfectant, such as a bleach-based cleaning product): Clostridioides difficile forms hardy spores that survive many cleaners, and a registered sporicidal agent such as a bleach-based product is recommended to kill them on surfaces. Alcohol does not kill the spores, so frequent alcohol wipes leave the environment contaminated. Quaternary ammonium compounds are useful for routine cleaning but are not sporicidal and do not eliminate these spores. Soap and water can physically remove spores from hands, but on surfaces they do not disinfect and the spores can persist for months.

Question 407DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Place the client in an airborne infection isolation room and wear a fit-tested N95 respirator): tuberculosis spreads by airborne droplet nuclei, so the client needs a negative-pressure isolation room and staff need fit-tested respirators. Cohorting with another client is unsafe because tuberculosis is only suspected and the strains or drug resistance may differ, and a semiprivate room is not an airborne isolation room. Gowns, gloves and dedicated equipment describe contact precautions, which do not protect against airborne transmission. The client wears a surgical mask only during transport, and the door of an airborne isolation room must stay closed to maintain negative pressure.

Question 408BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Move the client away from the fire and out of the room to safety): following the RACE sequence, the nurse first rescues any client in immediate danger before taking other steps. Activating the alarm is the second step and follows removal of the client who is next to the flames. Extinguishing the fire comes after rescue, alarm and containment, and attempting it first leaves the drowsy client exposed. Closing the door contains the fire only after the client is out, and doing it first would trap the client in the room.

Question 409CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C ("It sounds like you miss him very much, and I would like to hear about him."): reflecting the feeling of loss and inviting the client to talk about her husband supports healthy grieving and shows empathy. Saying that he is no longer suffering is a cliché that minimizes the client's grief and shifts focus away from her feelings. Suggesting a pet offers premature advice and does not acknowledge the client's expressed emotion. Telling the client to move on after one month places a time limit on normal grief and can make her feel judged.

Question 410CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Atropine 1 mg intravenous push, repeated every 3 to 5 minutes as needed): atropine is the first-line drug for symptomatic bradycardia with hypotension, and it is given as 1 mg every 3 to 5 minutes up to a total of 3 mg. Metoprolol slows the heart rate and lowers blood pressure, so it would worsen this client's bradycardia and hypotension. Nitroglycerin causes vasodilation and is withheld when systolic pressure is below 90 mm Hg, especially with an inferior infarction. Digoxin slows conduction through the atrioventricular node and would further reduce the already slow heart rate.

Question 411CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (A registered dietitian who can plan low-sodium meals and teach food choices): the registered dietitian is the team member trained to design therapeutic diets and to teach the client how to choose low-sodium foods. A social worker can help arrange services such as meal delivery but does not design a therapeutic sodium-restricted diet. A physical therapist addresses strength and mobility rather than the nutritional content of the client's meals. A pharmacist reviews medications and interactions, but the main sodium source here is the client's food, which the dietitian addresses.

Question 412DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Prolongation of the QT interval, which can lead to dangerous dysrhythmias): ondansetron can prolong the QT interval, and the added risks of methadone and hypokalemia increase the chance of torsades de pointes. Ondansetron does not cause vasoconstriction, and hypertension is not a recognized adverse effect of this drug. Hair loss is an effect of many chemotherapy agents but is not caused by ondansetron. Hyperglycemia is associated with corticosteroids used as antiemetics, not with ondansetron.

Question 413DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D ("I clean my hands before and after client contact and after removing gloves."): hand hygiene is required before and after touching a client, before aseptic tasks, after body fluid exposure, after touching the client's surroundings and after removing gloves. Cleaning hands only at the start and end of a shift and at breaks misses the many contacts in between that spread pathogens. Gloves can have microscopic defects and hands become contaminated during removal, so hand hygiene is needed even when hands look clean. Body fluid exposure is one required moment, but limiting hand hygiene to those contacts misses routine contact with the client and environment.

Question 414BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Eat small, frequent meals and get fresh air in a well-ventilated room): small, frequent meals avoid gastric distension, and fresh air reduces exposure to odors that trigger nausea. Large, fatty meals delay gastric emptying and stretch the stomach, which tends to worsen nausea. Lying flat after eating promotes reflux and discomfort, so sitting upright after meals is recommended instead. Strong scents of any kind can trigger or worsen nausea, so the room should be kept free of odors.

Question 415BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (A client 1 day after thyroidectomy who reports new tingling around the mouth): perioral tingling after thyroidectomy suggests hypocalcemia from parathyroid injury, which can progress to tetany, laryngospasm and seizures. Mild nausea after eating is uncomfortable but is an expected and stable finding that can be addressed next. A headache rated 4 of 10 needs attention, but moderate pain is not an immediate threat to the airway or life. Help to the bathroom matters for safety, yet the risk is lower than a possible airway emergency and can be delegated or handled shortly.

Question 416APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Notify the provider or rapid response team because the client may have sepsis): new confusion, fever, tachycardia, rapid breathing and hypotension in a client with an infection suggest sepsis, which requires immediate escalation for fluids, cultures and antibiotics. Oral fluids and a 4-hour recheck delay treatment while the client may already be in septic shock. Acetaminophen may lower the fever but does nothing for the hypotension and altered mental status that signal organ hypoperfusion. Reorientation and a sitter address safety, but treating the new confusion as a behavior problem misses a sign of a life-threatening infection.

Question 417DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (Assess the client's current condition and notify the provider about the missed dose): client safety comes first, so the nurse assesses the client and informs the provider, who decides how to manage the missed antibiotic dose. Giving the dose and shifting the schedule changes the prescription without direction from the provider or pharmacist. Holding the dose and resuming at 1800 leaves a long gap in antibiotic coverage without the provider's knowledge. The event is reported according to policy, but documentation comes after the client has been assessed and the provider notified.

Question 418BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (The right foot is cool and pale, with a weak dorsalis pedis pulse): a cool, pale foot with a weak pulse distal to the puncture site suggests arterial occlusion from a clot or hematoma, and the provider must be notified at once. Mild soreness at the insertion site is expected after femoral access and is managed with routine comfort measures. Strong, equal pulses with brisk refill indicate adequate circulation to the affected leg. A small, flat bruise is a common finding after catheterization, unlike an expanding hematoma or active bleeding.

Question 419DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Airborne precautions in an airborne infection isolation room with N95 respirators for staff): measles is spread by airborne particles that remain suspended in the air, so it requires a negative-pressure room and fit-tested respirators for staff. Contact precautions block spread by touch but do not prevent inhalation of airborne measles virus. Droplet precautions and a surgical mask protect against large droplets only, and a semiprivate room exposes another client. Standard precautions alone in a shared room do not contain an airborne pathogen and place roommates at high risk.

Question 420AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (Assess for hypovolemia and bladder distension from a blocked catheter, then notify the provider): output below about 30 mL/h for several hours may reflect low fluid volume, an obstructed catheter or kidney injury, so the nurse assesses and then reports the findings. Restricting fluids would worsen the likely hypovolemia suggested by the rising heart rate after surgery. Continued monitoring and documentation alone delay the assessment and reporting that low output requires. A diuretic requires a prescription based on the cause and could deepen hypovolemia and harm the kidneys.

Question 421DManagement of Care

The correct answer is D (A referral to hospice or palliative care services for comfort-focused support): hospice and palliative care focus on symptom relief, comfort and family support when a client chooses to stop curative treatment. Physical therapy may help function, but it does not address the client's stated goal of comfort-focused care. Occupational therapy supports daily activities but is not the comprehensive service that matches the client's wishes. A dietitian can help with nutrition, yet aggressive weight-gain goals may not fit the comfort-centered plan the client describes.

Question 422DBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is D (Increase fluid intake, add high-fiber foods and take a daily walk): adequate fluids, dietary fiber and physical activity soften stools and stimulate peristalsis, addressing each of the client's risk factors. Bed rest reduces intestinal motility and would worsen constipation in an already sedentary client. Limiting fluids makes stools harder, and nightly stimulant laxatives can lead to dependence. Fruits and vegetables are key fiber sources, so avoiding them would worsen the client's constipation.

Question 423AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (The client's parent or legal guardian, such as her mother): an unemancipated minor generally cannot consent to an elective procedure, so a parent or legal guardian must give consent. Some states allow minors to consent to specific services, but an elective cosmetic procedure is not among them for an unemancipated minor. An adult sibling has no legal authority to consent for a minor unless appointed as guardian. The nurse may witness the signature and advocate for the client but cannot give consent on the client's behalf.

Question 424DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Confirm the client's identity using facility policy and replace the band before giving care): two approved identifiers must be confirmed through facility procedures, and the band is replaced before any medication or treatment is given. Giving medications before the identity is confirmed and the band replaced risks giving drugs to the wrong client. A visitor is not an approved source for identification, and the information must be confirmed through facility procedures. Room and bed numbers are not acceptable identifiers because clients can be moved or may be in the wrong bed.

Question 425BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Uncompensated metabolic alkalosis, likely related to loss of gastric acid): the pH is high, the bicarbonate is high and the PaCO₂ is normal, which identifies a metabolic alkalosis without compensation, consistent with gastric suction. Metabolic acidosis would show a low pH and a low bicarbonate, which is the opposite of these results. Respiratory alkalosis requires a low PaCO₂, but this client's carbon dioxide level is within the normal range. Respiratory acidosis requires a high PaCO₂, and a pH above 7.45 rules out an acidosis even with compensation.

Question 426APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Hold the dose and contact the prescriber to clarify the order): the safe range is 18 kg times 20 mg, or 360 mg/day, to 18 kg times 40 mg, or 720 mg/day, and the order totals 250 mg times 3, or 750 mg/day, which exceeds the maximum. The prescribed daily total of 750 mg is above the 720 mg maximum, so the dose does not fall within the safe range. Halving the dose would change the prescription without authority and could leave the infection undertreated. Changing the frequency to every 12 hours also alters the prescription without the prescriber's direction, even though it lowers the daily total.

Question 427CReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is C (A temperature of 101.2°F (38.4°C) and a new productive cough since yesterday): fever with new respiratory symptoms may signal an infection that increases surgical and anesthesia risk and could lead the surgeon to postpone an elective procedure. A signed and witnessed consent form is an expected part of preoperative preparation. Mild anxiety before surgery is common and is addressed with teaching and reassurance rather than reporting to the surgeon. Fasting since midnight meets the preoperative instructions and is an expected finding.

The timing of the error helps predict the drug's peak effect, but the client's present condition must be addressed first. Question 429CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Beta blockade from receiving twice the ordered dose of metoprolol): metoprolol 50 mg is double the ordered dose, and excess beta blockade slows the heart rate and lowers blood pressure, matching the client's findings. The client tolerated the usual 25 mg dose with a heart rate in the 70s, so the sharp drop is not an expected response. Dehydration typically causes a faster heart rate, and moist mucous membranes and fair intake make it unlikely here. Recognizing the excess dose as the cause directs the nurse to monitor for further bradycardia and hypotension during the drug's peak.

Question 430BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (The client's risk for falls and further drops in heart rate and blood pressure): a lightheaded client with bradycardia and hypotension who wants to walk is at immediate risk for a fall and further cardiovascular decline, so physical safety comes first. The safety event report is required, but it is completed after the client is safe and the provider is informed. Identifying who removed the tablet does not protect the client, and system review focuses on causes rather than on individual blame. Once safety and monitoring are established, the nurse notifies the provider, discloses the error and reports it.

Question 431BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Monitor the client closely, notify the provider and report the error according to policy): close monitoring protects the client, provider notification allows medical direction, and reporting supports disclosure and system improvement. Quietly giving fluids and reporting only if the client worsens conceals the error and delays medical evaluation. Holding the evening dose is a change in the prescription that only the provider can make, and delaying disclosure to the client is not transparent. A transparent plan also allows the provider to decide on holding parameters for later doses and how to disclose the error to the client.

Question 432AManagement of Care

The correct answer is A (Keep the client safely in bed, complete a focused assessment, then notify the provider): keeping the lightheaded client in bed and offering a bedpan or urinal prevents a fall, and a focused assessment gives the provider current data. Walking a lightheaded client with bradycardia and hypotension to the bathroom risks syncope and a serious fall. Documentation of the event is required, but it comes after the client is safe and the provider has been notified. Assessment and safety come before any paperwork whenever a client shows signs of physiologic instability.

Question 433CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (The event is reviewed to find system causes, and the two strengths are stored apart): a culture of safety treats errors as chances to learn and fixes system problems, such as separating look-alike medication strengths. A punitive warning without system review discourages reporting and leaves the cause of the error in place. Keeping the event private prevents other staff from learning about the hazard and recognizing similar risks. Open, nonpunitive reporting and system redesign reduce the chance that the same error will happen again.

Question 434DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("The MMR vaccine is recommended after you give birth, not during pregnancy."): MMR is a live vaccine that is contraindicated during pregnancy, so a nonimmune client receives it after delivery, before discharge if possible. The vaccine remains contraindicated in the third trimester because the restriction applies to the entire pregnancy. Nonimmune adults, especially those who may become pregnant again, are recommended to receive the vaccine. Giving a live vaccine today exposes the fetus to a theoretical risk, so vaccination is deferred until after birth.

Question 435CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C ("It stimulates your bone marrow to make more infection-fighting white blood cells."): filgrastim is a granulocyte colony-stimulating factor that increases neutrophil production and lowers the risk of infection after chemotherapy. Filgrastim does not raise platelets, and other agents are used to treat chemotherapy-related thrombocytopenia. Bone pain is actually a common adverse effect of filgrastim rather than a symptom it treats. Red blood cell production is stimulated by erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, not by filgrastim.

Question 436BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B ("These medicines often take several weeks to work, so let's talk with your provider first."): selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors usually take several weeks to improve mood, and any change should involve the provider. Doubling the dose without a prescription is unsafe and raises the risk of adverse effects such as serotonin syndrome. Supporting abrupt discontinuation ignores the delayed onset of action and risks discontinuation symptoms and worsening depression. A why question sounds judgmental and can make the client defensive rather than open to teaching.

Question 437AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Dress the baby in one more layer of clothing than an adult would wear): newborns lose heat easily and generally need one more layer than an adult would wear in the same environment. Soft bedding and pillows in the crib increase the risk of suffocation and sudden infant death, so a wearable blanket or sleep sack is used instead. Bulky snowsuits compress in a crash and leave the harness too loose, so blankets are placed over the buckled straps instead. An overly warm room causes overheating, which is a risk factor for sudden infant death.

Question 438CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C ("Keep your hip bent less than 90 degrees, and do not cross your legs."): posterior hip precautions limit flexion beyond 90 degrees, adduction across the midline and internal rotation to prevent dislocation. Bending forward to reach the floor flexes the hip past 90 degrees, so a reacher is used instead. Low, soft seats force deep hip flexion when sitting and rising, so firm chairs of adequate height and raised toilet seats are recommended. Turning the knees and toes inward is internal rotation, which places the new joint at risk of posterior dislocation.

Question 439BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (An emergency tracheostomy tray and working suction equipment at the bedside): neck swelling, hematoma, laryngeal nerve injury or hypocalcemic laryngospasm can obstruct the airway, so emergency airway equipment must be immediately available. Fever can occur with thyroid storm, but cooling measures are obtained if needed and do not address the life-threatening airway risk. A tourniquet cannot be applied to the neck, and bleeding is managed with pressure and urgent surgical evaluation. Hourly urine monitoring is not a routine need after thyroidectomy and does not prepare for an airway emergency.

Question 440BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (A client after an ischemic stroke whose right leg weakness limits walking): physical therapists help restore strength, balance and mobility, which this client needs to walk safely after a stroke. A urinary tract infection treated with antibiotics does not cause a mobility deficit that requires physical therapy. Seasonal allergy symptoms are managed with medication and do not affect strength or gait. Otitis media is treated medically, and any lasting hearing concern would be referred to audiology rather than physical therapy.

Question 441DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Contact the provider or pharmacist to request an alternative form of the medication): extended-release tablets are not crushed or split unless the label or pharmacist confirms it is safe, so the nurse seeks a form the client can swallow. Crushing an extended-release tablet releases the entire dose at once and can cause severe hypotension. Cutting this tablet can damage its release mechanism, so splitting is done only when the product labeling allows it. Holding the dose leaves the client's blood pressure untreated and does not solve the swallowing problem.

Question 442BReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is B (Warm the client's hands or move the probe to the earlobe, then recheck): cold hands and a poor waveform suggest low peripheral perfusion causing an inaccurate reading, so the nurse improves the signal and correlates it with the client's condition. Silencing the alarm without confirming the reading could miss true hypoxemia. High-flow oxygen is not warranted for a comfortable client when the reading itself is unreliable. Documenting a questionable value as accurate and delaying the report fails to verify the client's true oxygenation.

Question 443ABasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is A ("Hold the cane in your right hand, on your stronger side."): holding the cane on the strong side widens the base of support and lets the cane move forward together with the weak leg. Using the cane on the weak side causes the client to lean toward the affected side and disrupts a normal gait. The cane moves forward with or slightly before the weak leg so that it supports that leg during weight bearing. Placing the cane far from the body makes the client lean and reduces stability, so it is kept a few inches to the side.

Question 444CBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is C ("Drink plenty of fluids each day and urinate regularly when you feel the urge."): adequate fluid intake and regular voiding flush bacteria from the urinary tract before they can multiply. Holding urine allows bacteria to multiply in the bladder and increases the risk of infection. Wiping from back to front moves bowel bacteria toward the urethra, so females should wipe from front to back. Perfumed soaps and douches irritate tissue and disrupt normal flora, which raises the risk of infection.

Question 445CReduction of Risk Potential

Question 446DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (Offer scheduled toileting, keep the bed low and place the call light within reach): scheduled toileting addresses the reason the client gets up, and a low bed with a reachable call light reduces the chance of injury. Severe fluid restriction in an older adult taking a diuretic risks dehydration, hypotension and confusion. Question 447APsychosocial Integrity

The nurse does not introduce a new topic but focuses directly on the client's apparent emotion. The statement offers no promise that things will improve, so it is not reassurance. The nurse does not tell the client what to do, so the statement is not advice.

Question 448BPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is B (Connect the family with respite care services and a local caregiver support group): respite care gives the caregiver needed rest, and support groups reduce isolation and stress so the family can continue caring safely. Restraints can cause injury, agitation and loss of dignity and are not a safe solution for caregiver fatigue. Encouraging the daughter to keep managing alone increases the risk of caregiver burnout. Limiting social contact worsens the isolation and strain the daughter is already experiencing.

Question 449DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Oral lactulose titrated to produce two to three soft stools daily): confusion and asterixis suggest hepatic encephalopathy, and lactulose traps ammonia in the colon and promotes its excretion in the stool. Diuretics do not lower ammonia, and overdiuresis with electrolyte imbalance can actually worsen encephalopathy. Adequate protein is part of cirrhosis care, but a supplement does not lower ammonia or treat the acute encephalopathy. Benzodiazepines are metabolized by the liver and can deepen the confusion and sedation of hepatic encephalopathy.

Question 450CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Report the concern to the nurse manager through the chain of command): falsifying records is unethical, illegal and harmful to clients, so the nurse reports it through the appropriate chain of command. Discussing a coworker's suspected misconduct with clients is unprofessional and does not stop the practice. Altering another person's documentation is itself a falsification of the legal record. A private conversation may seem collegial, but repeated falsification that has already harmed clients requires formal reporting.

Question 451BHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is B (Takes a few steps alone across the room with a wide-based, unsteady gait): by 15 months most children take a few steps on their own, often with a wide stance and frequent falls, and most walk without holding on by 18 months. Riding a tricycle requires coordination that typically appears around 3 years of age. Speaking in sentences of three or more words is expected closer to 3 years, while a 15-month-old usually says a few single words. Copying a circle is a fine motor skill of the 3-year-old and is not expected in a young toddler.

Question 452BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Use plain language and pictures, then ask the client to explain the plan back): plain language and visual aids make the content easier to understand, and teach-back confirms that the client has understood the plan. A detailed handout full of medical terms relies on reading skills the client has said are limited. Presenting everything at once overloads the learner, so key points are better taught in small, prioritized pieces. Involving the daughter can help, but the client remains the primary learner and has the right to understand his or her own care.

Question 453CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (A client fell while walking to the bathroom but had no visible injury): a fall is an unexpected event that could cause harm, so it is reported through the incident system even when no injury is seen. A client's informed refusal of a medication is documented and reported to the provider but is not an incident. A delayed discharge while waiting for a ride is a routine scheduling issue rather than a safety event. A complaint about noise is addressed through customer service or the manager and does not represent an adverse event.

Question 454CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Flumazenil given intravenously, with close observation for possible seizure activity): flumazenil blocks benzodiazepine receptors and reverses sedation, but it can trigger seizures, so the client is watched closely. Naloxone reverses opioids and has little effect on a pure benzodiazepine overdose. Protamine sulfate neutralizes heparin and does not affect benzodiazepine sedation. Vitamin K reverses warfarin by restoring clotting factors and has no role in sedative overdose.

Question 455ASafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is A (A wall suction unit that produces no negative pressure when turned on): suction that does not work is a serious hazard for a client with a tracheostomy, so it is tagged, removed and reported for repair. A charged pulse oximeter that reads accurately is working properly and can stay in the room. Locked wheels keep the bed stable, and rails are positioned based on the client's needs after arrival. A freshly disinfected IV pole is clean and ready for use.

Question 456CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Myxedema coma from severe, untreated hypothyroidism with slowed body metabolism): myxedema coma is the most severe form of hypothyroidism and causes hypothermia, bradycardia, hypoventilation and decreased consciousness. Thyroid storm produces fever, tachycardia and agitation, which are the opposite of these findings. A hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state causes confusion and dehydration but is not linked to stopping thyroid replacement or to marked hypothermia. Adrenal crisis causes hypotension and shock after steroid withdrawal, and this client has no history of corticosteroid use.

Question 457DPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is D ("You are safe here, and you can tell me to stop at any time."): trauma-informed care emphasizes safety, choice and control, which helps prevent retraumatization. Demanding a full account right away removes the client's control over what to share and when. Claiming to know exactly how the client feels minimizes the client's unique experience and shifts focus to the nurse. Rushing through the exam does not offer choices or pacing, which can make the client feel powerless again.

The correct answer is B (Urine output under 20 mL/h, creatinine doubled from baseline and potassium 5.8 mEq/L): oliguria, a doubled creatinine and an elevated potassium signal acute kidney injury with a dangerous electrolyte imbalance. Question 459APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Acute kidney injury with fluid retention causing early pulmonary congestion): falling urine output and rising creatinine show reduced kidney function, while weight gain, crackles and edema show retained fluid. A doubled creatinine with oliguria is not an expected postoperative change and needs prompt treatment. Hypovolemia would cause weight loss and clear lungs, whereas this client has gained weight and has crackles. Recognizing fluid overload guides the team to avoid extra fluid while treating the kidney injury.

Question 460CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Cardiac dysrhythmias caused by the elevated serum potassium level): hyperkalemia disrupts cardiac conduction and can cause sudden, fatal dysrhythmias. A pressure injury is a real risk with limited mobility, but it develops over time and is not immediately life-threatening. Paralytic ileus causes distention and discomfort but does not threaten life as quickly as a dysrhythmia. The nurse therefore prioritizes cardiac monitoring and prompt treatment of the potassium level.

Question 461APhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is A (Continuous cardiac monitoring and medications to shift and remove excess potassium): peaked T waves with hyperkalemia require cardiac monitoring and urgent treatment that stabilizes the heart and lowers potassium. Potassium supplements would raise an already dangerous potassium level. A large fluid bolus would worsen the crackles and edema in a client who is already fluid overloaded. Question 462BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Scheduled ibuprofen 600 mg orally every 6 hours for incisional pain): nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs reduce blood flow to the kidneys and can worsen acute kidney injury, so the nurse clarifies the order. Acetaminophen at this dose is a reasonable pain option because it does not reduce kidney blood flow. Calcium gluconate stabilizes the heart muscle against the effects of hyperkalemia and is appropriate to give. Questioning the ibuprofen allows the provider to choose a safer pain plan for the client.

Question 463CPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is C (Urine output of 50 mL/h, falling creatinine and potassium of 4.6 mEq/L): recovering urine output, a falling creatinine and a normal potassium show that kidney function and electrolyte balance are improving. Spreading crackles with lower oxygen saturation show worsening fluid overload. A potassium of 6.4 mEq/L with electrocardiogram changes shows worsening hyperkalemia that needs emergency treatment. Only the improved output and laboratory values indicate that the treatment plan is working.

Question 464CPsychosocial Integrity

The correct answer is C ("Tell me more about what worries you about returning to work."): an open-ended invitation encourages the client to explore and express concerns. Giving personal advice takes over the client's decisions and closes the conversation. A question that starts with why can sound judgmental and make the client defensive. False reassurance dismisses the client's feelings and discourages further sharing.

Question 465CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (An 82-year-old on bed rest with an indwelling urinary catheter and IV line): older age, immobility and two invasive devices together raise the risk of pressure injuries, falls and infections. A young adult under brief observation after a minor injury has few risk factors for complications. A child receiving a single dose of fluids has a short exposure to an IV line and is otherwise healthy. An ambulatory adult having outpatient surgery is mobile and expected to have a brief stay.

Question 466BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (4 mL, because 1,000 mg divided by 250 mg per mL equals 4): 1 g equals 1,000 mg, and dividing the 1,000 mg dose by the 250 mg per mL concentration gives 4 mL. Dividing the concentration by the dose inverts the formula and gives a volume far too small. One gram equals 1,000 mg, not 10,000 mg, so this conversion error produces a tenfold overdose volume. Using 400 mg per mL misreads the label, and the actual concentration of 250 mg per mL requires a larger volume.

Question 467CHealth Promotion and Maintenance

Abstract and hypothetical reasoning develops in adolescence during the formal operational stage. Intense concern with peer group approval and identity is typical of adolescents. Logical thinking about concrete objects and conservation develops in school-age children.

Question 468BManagement of Care

The correct answer is B (Go to the bedside and assess the client's condition and surgical site): a sudden drop in blood pressure after surgery may signal bleeding or shock, and the RN must assess the client to judge the cause and urgency. Waiting an hour for a recheck could delay treatment of a serious problem such as hemorrhage. The surgeon will need to be notified, but the call is more useful once the RN has gathered assessment data. Recording the value and continuing routine care does not respond to a significant change in condition.

Question 469BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Blood glucose above 600 mg/dL with high serum osmolality and minimal ketones): this condition causes extreme hyperglycemia and severe dehydration with little or no ketosis, often in older adults with type 2 diabetes. A low pH with large ketones describes diabetic ketoacidosis rather than a hyperosmolar state. A glucose of 52 mg/dL with sweating and tachycardia indicates hypoglycemia. Very high glucose and water loss raise serum osmolality, so a low osmolality does not fit this condition.

Question 470AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (Massage the fundus while supporting the lower uterine segment with the other hand): a boggy fundus indicates uterine atony, and massage stimulates contraction to control the bleeding at its source. Increasing IV fluids supports circulating volume but does not stop the uterus from bleeding. Weighing pads gives an accurate blood loss estimate but should follow measures that stop the bleeding. The provider is notified if bleeding continues after massage, but the nurse first acts to firm the uterus.

Question 471DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D ("I will take leftover tablets to a drug take-back site at a pharmacy."): take-back programs remove unused opioids from the home safely and are the preferred disposal method recommended by the FDA. Saving tablets for family members encourages use without a prescription and leaves them available for misuse. An unlocked kitchen cabinet is not disposal and leaves the opioid within reach of the teenagers in the home. Question 472DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Average blood glucose has been above target over the past 2 to 3 months): hemoglobin A1C reflects average glucose over about 2 to 3 months, and 9.2% is well above the common target of less than 7%. The test measures glucose attached to hemoglobin over the life of red blood cells rather than a single fasting value. A value of 9.2% indicates poor control, so being under 10% does not mean the client is at goal. An elevated A1C points to high average glucose, not to frequent low blood sugar.

Question 473CManagement of Care

The correct answer is C (Inform the team of the living will and advocate for the client's documented wishes): a valid living will states the client's own wishes and guides care when the client cannot speak, and the nurse advocates by making sure the team honors it. A family member's preference does not override a valid advance directive, although the team supports the spouse through the decision. A new consent from the spouse cannot replace the treatment choices the client already documented. Waiting for the client to communicate leaves the documented wishes unaddressed when the directive already provides guidance.

Question 474AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A ("These symptoms need emergency care, so please go to the emergency department now."): a persistent severe headache with vision changes is an urgent maternal warning sign of postpartum preeclampsia, which can develop after discharge even with normal pregnancy blood pressures. Waiting several weeks for a routine visit could allow a seizure or stroke to occur. Attributing the symptoms to poor sleep overlooks a potentially life-threatening condition. Question 475DSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is D (An indwelling urinary catheter placed in the emergency department on admission): an indwelling catheter bypasses normal defenses and gives microorganisms a direct route into the bladder. Adequate protein and fluids support immune function and wound healing, which lowers infection risk. Walking regularly improves lung expansion and circulation and reduces the risk of pneumonia and other complications. A short stay limits exposure to hospital organisms and procedures, so it reduces rather than raises risk.

Question 476DReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is D (Circulation, sensation and movement of the right hand along with the puncture site): radial access can cause bleeding, hematoma or arterial occlusion, so the hand's circulation and the site are checked frequently. Question 477APsychosocial Integrity

Question 478AHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is A (The child does not say any words and uses gestures to communicate): most 2-year-olds use many single words and some two-word phrases, so having no words warrants a developmental and hearing evaluation. Two-word phrases are an expected language milestone at 2 years. Pointing to named pictures shows normal receptive language for this age. Walking well and beginning to run are typical gross motor skills of a 2-year-old.

Question 479BSafety and Infection Prevention and Control

The correct answer is B (Remove the belt and store it securely according to unit safety policy): a belt can be used for hanging or harm to others, so it is removed and stored to keep the environment safe. Voluntary status does not change the unit's duty to remove items that could be used for self-harm. Staff presence on the unit does not prevent a client from using the belt when unobserved. Question 480BBasic Care and Comfort

The correct answer is B (Scan the bladder to measure urine volume and help the client try to void): these findings suggest urinary retention, and measuring bladder volume while promoting voiding comes before catheterization. Catheterization requires an order or protocol and carries infection risk, and intermittent catheterization is preferred if needed. Question 481BPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is B (Intravenous dextrose, or glucagon by injection or nasal route if access is lost): a client who cannot swallow safely needs a nonoral glucose source, and intravenous dextrose or glucagon raises glucose quickly. Question 483APsychosocial Integrity

Weekly thyroid testing is not part of clozapine monitoring because the drug does not commonly affect the thyroid. Daily sodium levels are not required, although sodium may be checked if symptoms suggest an imbalance. Question 485DHealth Promotion and Maintenance

The correct answer is D ("A mild cold is usually not a reason to delay vaccines; I'll confirm with the provider."): mild illness, with or without a low-grade fever, is generally not a contraindication to vaccination, and the provider makes the final decision. Waiting for the cold to clear delays protection, since only moderate or severe illness is a reason to postpone. Question 486DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is D (Protamine sulfate given by slow intravenous injection as ordered by the provider): protamine binds heparin and neutralizes its anticoagulant effect, and it is given slowly to avoid hypotension. Vitamin K reverses warfarin by helping the liver make clotting factors and does not neutralize heparin. Naloxone reverses opioid effects and has no effect on anticoagulation. Flumazenil reverses benzodiazepines and does not affect bleeding from heparin.

Question 487DPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

Limiting fluids increases the concentration of the drug in urine and raises the risk of crystal formation. Question 488CPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is C (Yellowish visual halos with nausea and an apical pulse of 52): visual halos, nausea and bradycardia are classic signs of digoxin toxicity and need prompt follow-up. Less ankle edema is the intended effect of the new diuretic and shows a positive response. The blood pressure and respiratory rate are within acceptable limits for this client. The visual changes and slow pulse are the cues that point to a dangerous drug effect.

Question 489BPharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is B (Low potassium from furosemide makes the heart more sensitive to digoxin's effects): loop diuretics cause potassium loss, and hypokalemia increases digoxin binding in the heart, raising the risk of toxicity. Hypokalemia increases rather than decreases digoxin's effect, so raising the dose would be dangerous. Poor appetite may contribute slightly, but the potassium level is closely linked to digoxin toxicity. Understanding this link helps the family see why potassium will be monitored closely.

Question 490APharmacological and Parenteral Therapies

The correct answer is A (Digoxin toxicity made worse by low potassium related to the new diuretic): the timing after starting furosemide, the low potassium and the combination of visual, gastrointestinal and cardiac signs point to digoxin toxicity. Aging does not cause sudden yellow halos and a new slow pulse within a week. A viral illness could explain nausea and fatigue but not the visual halos or bradycardia. Recognizing toxicity as the leading hypothesis guides the nurse to hold the drug and seek orders.

The correct answer is D (Emergency assessment by a team with critical care and airway management skills): a total of 7 or more triggers an emergency response with continuous monitoring and staff skilled in critical care and airway management. Question 499DPhysiological Adaptation

The correct answer is D (Ruptured esophageal varices caused by increased pressure in the portal system): portal hypertension creates fragile dilated veins in the esophagus that can rupture and cause massive, life-threatening bleeding. Question 500AReduction of Risk Potential

The correct answer is A (A modified Allen test to confirm ulnar artery blood flow to the hand): the modified Allen test checks collateral circulation through the ulnar artery so the hand stays perfused if the radial artery is injured.
